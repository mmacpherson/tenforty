## Common treatment (all scenarios)

- **Federal AGI.** Wages go on Form 1040 line 1a/1z; the capital gain in
  scenario 8 on line 7 (2024) / 7a (2025); total income is line 9; with no
  adjustments (line 10 = 0), AGI is line 11 (2024) / 11a (2025) (US24, US25).
  So AGI = wages (+ gain in scenario 8). The 2025 federal H.R. 1 deductions for
  seniors, tips and overtime are taken after AGI and do not change line 11a.
- **M1 line 2 additions = 0.** None of the listed M1M/M1MB addition triggers
  (other-state municipal interest, bonus depreciation, pass-through state tax,
  lump-sum distributions, etc.; I24 pp.11-12, I25 pp.11-12) applies to wages or
  an ordinary long-term capital gain.
- **M1 line 6 = 0** (no state refund in federal income). **M1 line 7
  subtractions = 0**: none of the listed triggers applies (I24 pp.14-15,
  I25 pp.14-15). Scenario 7 is 65 or older but does not qualify for the
  Schedule M1R age-65 subtraction because line 1 (50,000) is not less than the
  33,700 limit for a single filer (same limit printed in both years).
- **Long-term capital gains** (scenario 8) get no Minnesota preferential rate
  or subtraction: line 1 starts from federal AGI and the M1 booklet lists no
  capital gain subtraction; the gain is taxed as part of line 9 at the ordinary
  table/schedule rates.
- **Line 4** is the Minnesota standard deduction from the Standard Deduction
  Table for Line 4 (Minnesota's own amounts, not the federal ones), reduced by
  Worksheet A only when line 1 exceeds 232,500 (2024) / 238,950 (2025).
- **Line 5** is from the Worksheet for Line 5: number of dependents x 5,050
  (2024) / 5,200 (2025), reduced only when line 1 exceeds the filing-status
  threshold. Minnesota has no exemption for the taxpayer or spouse.
- **Line 9** = line 3 - line 8 (line 8 = lines 4 + 5 + 6 + 7).
- **Line 10**: tax table if line 9 < 90,000 (2024) / < 86,800 (2025); rate
  schedule otherwise. Schedule results are rounded to the nearest dollar
  (.50 rounds up).
- **Line 11 AMT = 0 in every scenario.** Schedule M1MT was worked for each
  (line 20 = line 1, since there are no federal Form 6251 preferences and no
  M1SA deductions; exemption line 21 reduced by 25% of line 20 over line 22;
  line 27 = 6.75% of the remainder). Line 27 is below line 10 everywhere; the
  closest case is scenario 9 (shown there).
- **Excluded by instruction**: Minnesota Child Tax Credit and Working Family
  Credit (Schedule M1CWFC, refundable, claimed via Schedule M1REF) exist and
  would apply in principle to returns with qualifying children (scenarios 5, 6,
  9 subject to their income phase-outs); they are **not** computed here. Also not
  computed: the nonrefundable Marriage Credit (Schedule M1MA, via Schedule M1C,
  line 16), which is after line 12 and needs each spouse's earned income; other
  M1C credits; withholding. None of these changes lines 9-13.

## Uncertainties

1. **2025 scenario 9 line 4 / line 9 rounding ($1).** Worksheet A step 3 is
   1,831.50. The booklet gives only the general nearest-dollar rule and does not
   say whether worksheet steps or only form entries are rounded. Line 4 is
   28,069 (round the final entry; preferred reading) or 28,068 (round each
   step); line 9 is 261,531 or 261,532. Line 10 is 17,853 either way. Do not
   pin line 9 for this case to better than +/- $1.
2. **2025 conformity wording is inconsistent across DOR documents.** The 2025
   M1 booklet (PDF modified 2026-10-01) still says Minnesota follows the IRC as
   amended through May 1, 2023 and points to Schedule M1NC. The 2025 Schedule
   M1NC (Rev. 7/26) says Minnesota "has generally adopted the Internal Revenue
   Code as amended through May 1, 2026" and has only two live lines (529
   credentialing expenses; rural/agricultural loan interest). The 2024 Schedule
   M1NC (Rev. 5/26) lists only business/depreciation items. None of these lines
   applies to wage or ordinary capital-gain income, so the derivations do not
   depend on which statement is current, but I did not read the underlying 2026
   session law and cannot confirm that it left the 2025 standard deduction,
   exemption and bracket amounts untouched. The amounts used are those printed
   in the latest posted booklet (October 2026 posting).
3. **Forms were revised after first publication.** The 2025 booklet, 2025 M1MT
   and both M1NC schedules carry 2026 revision or posting dates. I used the
   versions live on 2026-11-11 and did not compare them with the originally
   released versions. The 2024 booklet PDF's internal date is 2024-12-11.
4. **AMT.** Line 11 = 0 rests on my own completion of Schedule M1MT with no
   federal Form 6251 adjustments (lines 2c-3) and, for 2025, nothing on M1MT
   line 3 (from Schedule M1NC line 23). That is the natural reading for
   wage/capital-gain-only standard-deduction returns but I did not read the
   federal Form 6251 instructions. The margin in the tightest case (scenario 9)
   is about 1,500.
5. **Capital gains (scenario 8).** The conclusion that Minnesota gives no
   long-term capital gain preference is from the absence of any such item in
   the M1 line 2 / line 7 lists and Schedule M1M, not from a positive statement.
   Minnesota's separate net investment income tax (Schedule NIIT, M1 line 14)
   is outside line 12 and was not analysed; I did not verify its threshold.
6. **Federal AGI** is taken as wages (+ gain) by reading the face of Form 1040
   only; the scenarios stipulate no adjustments to income.
7. **Credits not computed**: Child Tax Credit and Working Family Credit
   (M1CWFC), Marriage Credit (M1MA) and all other M1C / M1REF credits. A test
   comparing against "total tax after credits" would not match these figures
   for scenarios 4, 5, 6 and 9.
8. **Dependents** are assumed to be qualifying dependents under Schedule
   M1DQC; that schedule's qualification rules were not read.
9. **PDF text extraction.** Values were read from `pdftotext -layout` output of
   the official PDFs, not from the rendered pages. Each table row used was
   cross-checked against the rate schedule at the row midpoint and all agree,
   which makes a column mis-read unlikely, but the rendered PDF pages were not
   viewed.
