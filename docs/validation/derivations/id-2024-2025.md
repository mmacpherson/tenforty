Blind derivation, 2026-11-11. Sources: Idaho State Tax Commission and IRS
publications only, fetched from the web on 2026-11-11. No tenforty code, spec,
table, graph, test, fixture, tax software or third-party calculator was read or
run. The only repository file read was
`docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md` (evidence format).

**Evidence kind for every whole-return value below: Independent derivation.**
Idaho publishes no tax table for 2024 or 2025; line 20 is computed by formula
(the "Tax from worksheet"), so no tax amount here is a published table row.
The parameters (standard deductions, zero-rate thresholds, rates, $205 credit,
$10 PBF) are **published instruction amounts / rate-schedule rows**, cited in
"Sources". None of these is a published worked example.

### Answers to the stated questions

- **Rate and zero-rate threshold.** Single flat rate above a zero-rate amount.
  2024: 5.695% above $4,673 (Single/MFS) or $9,346 (MFJ, HOH, QSS).
  2025: 5.3% above $4,811 (Single/MFS) or $9,622 (MFJ, HOH, QSS).
  Head of household uses the *joint* threshold in both years.
- **Table or formula.** Formula is required; there is no Idaho tax table for
  either year. The form line itself is titled "Tax from worksheet".
- **Child tax credit.** $205 per qualifying child (age 16 or under at year
  end) in **both** 2024 and 2025. It is **nonrefundable**: the worksheet takes
  the lesser of $205 × children or the tax remaining after the other-state,
  39R and Form 44 credits ("This credit is limited to your tax liability…"),
  and it sits in the "Credits" block subtracted from line 20, not in
  "Payments and Other Credits". No carryforward is stated in the instructions
  read. No income phase-out is stated in the worksheet.
- **Permanent building fund tax.** $10 per return, owed whenever Idaho
  requires the return to be filed. It is listed under "Other Taxes", after the
  income-tax-after-credits line (2024 line 27 / 2025 line 26), and is added
  into "Total Tax" (2024 line 33 / 2025 line 32). It is **not** part of the
  income tax computed on line 20, is not reduced by the child tax credit, and
  is a flat amount rather than a function of income. All nine scenarios have
  gross income at or above the filing threshold in both years, none is blind
  or on Idaho public assistance, so $10 applies to every row — including
  scenario 1 / 2025, where income tax is zero.
- **Grocery credit.** Exists (2024 "Grocery Credit", $120 per person; 2025
  "Food Tax Credit", $155 per person), refundable, on line 43 under "Payments
  and Other Credits". Excluded from every figure here as instructed.
- **Capital gains deduction (scenario 9).** Does not apply. The 60% Idaho
  capital gains deduction (Form 39R Part B line 10) is limited to qualified
  Idaho property — real property, tangible personal property used in a
  revenue-producing enterprise, certain livestock, timber, certain partnership
  interests. Both years' instructions add: "Gains from the sale of stocks,
  goodwill, and other intangibles don't qualify." So the $10,000 gain stays in
  Idaho taxable income and is taxed at the ordinary flat rate; Idaho has no
  preferential capital gains rate in the line 20 worksheet.
- **Additions and subtractions.** Zero in all scenarios. Wages and a stock
  gain trigger none of the Form 39R Part A additions or Part B subtractions
  (assumes no state-specific items such as non-Idaho municipal interest,
  bonus depreciation, retirement-benefit deduction, etc.).
- **Rounding.** Whole dollars, half-up ("Round down if under 50 cents, round
  up if 50 cents or more"). Every input and intermediate here is already a
  whole dollar except worksheet line 4 (subtotal × rate), which is rounded
  once to give line 20. No scenario lands within a cent of a .50 tie, so the
  tie rule is not exercised.

## Uncertainties

- **U1 — 2025 line 18 and the federal Schedule 1-A deductions (material,
  scenario 8 / 2025 only; $318 of tax).** The 2025 instructions (I25 p.9, and
  the copy of the instructions bundled in F25) head line 18 "Qualified
  Business Income and Federal Schedule 1-A Deductions" and say to "Add lines
  13a and 13b from federal Form 1040". The printed 2025 Form 40 line 18 label
  still reads only "Qualified business income deduction". I followed the
  instructions, which are explicit and consistent with the stated conformity
  ("Idaho conforms to the IRC as of January 1, 2026"), and found no Form 39R
  addition that adds the senior deduction back (searched I25 for "1-A",
  "senior", "13b"). I did not read the Idaho statute or the "Idaho Worksheet
  1-A" (referenced only in the Form 43 part-year instructions), and I did not
  fetch the IRS Schedule 1-A instructions to confirm eligibility details
  beyond what the schedule itself prints. The alt row gives the value if
  line 18 were zero. A fixture for this case should also state the
  assumptions: valid SSN, born before January 2, 1961, no tips/overtime/car
  loan interest deductions.
- **U2 — the same line 18 rule reaches other 2025 scenarios only if extra
  facts are added.** Scenarios 1-7 and 9 have no Schedule 1-A deductions as
  stipulated (wage earners under 65, no stated tips, overtime or car-loan
  interest), so line 18 = 0. If a harness supplies overtime or tips inputs,
  2025 Idaho taxable income would fall accordingly.
- **U3 — packet revisions.** The 2024 packet used is rev. 10-23-2024 and the
  2025 packet rev. 03-02-2026, the versions the Tax Commission's index page
  linked on 2026-11-11. The 2024 Form 40 is rev. 09-04-2024. I did not compare
  against earlier or later revisions.
- **U4 — rounding inside the tax worksheet.** The instructions give one
  general rule (whole dollars, half-up) and no worksheet-specific rule. I
  rounded once, at worksheet line 4/5. No scenario is near a .50 boundary
  (closest fractional parts: .40, .15, .90, .81, .54, .85, .27, .53, .91), so
  any reasonable reading gives the same whole-dollar tax; the tie direction
  itself is untested by these cases.
- **U5 — child tax credit conditions not exercised.** The worksheet prints no
  income phase-out and no carryforward; both children/the child are assumed to
  be qualifying children age 16 or under at year end with the credit fully
  absorbed by tax. Refundability is inferred from the worksheet's "lesser of"
  limit and the note "limited to your tax liability", not from the statute.
- **U6 — PBF characterization.** "Not part of income tax" is read from the
  form layout (a separate $10 line under "Other Taxes", added after the
  income-tax-after-credits line) and the instruction text; the statute was
  not consulted. Whether a consumer of these fixtures wants "Idaho income
  tax" to mean line 27/26 (used here) or total tax line 33/32 (add 10) is a
  mapping choice; both are tabulated per scenario.
- **U7 — federal inputs.** Federal AGI is taken as wages (plus the capital
  gain in scenario 9) with no adjustments; for the Idaho computation only the
  AGI and, in 2025, Form 1040 lines 13a/13b matter. Federal tax itself was not
  derived.
- **U8 — rate-schedule web page.** The RS page was read through a
  fetch-and-summarize tool rather than verbatim; its thresholds and rates
  agree with the worksheet text extracted directly from the PDFs, which is
  the controlling source here.
