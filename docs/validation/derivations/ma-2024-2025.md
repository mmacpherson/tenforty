- 10 "TOTAL 5.0% INCOME. Add lines 3 through 9."
- 11a "Amount you paid to Social Security, Medicare, Railroad, U.S. or Massachusetts retirement. Not more than $2,000"; 11b "Amount spouse paid to … Not more than $2,000". 12, 13 reserved. 14 rental deduction. 15 Schedule Y line 19. 16 "TOTAL DEDUCTIONS. Add lines 11 through 15".
- 17 "5.0% INCOME AFTER DEDUCTIONS. Subtract line 16 from line 10. Not less than 0."
- 18 "Total exemption amount (from line 2g)".
- 19 "5.0% INCOME AFTER EXEMPTIONS. Subtract line 18 from line 17. Not less than 0. If line 17 is less than line 18, see instructions."
- 20 "INTEREST AND DIVIDEND INCOME (from Schedule B, line 38). Not less than 0."
- 21 "TOTAL TAXABLE 5.0% INCOME. Add lines 19 and 20."
- 22 "TAX ON 5.0% INCOME (from tax table). If line 21 is more than $24,000, multiply by .05."
- 23a "8.5% income … × .085"; 23b "12% income … × .12"; 23 is the total of the two.
- 24 "TAX ON LONG-TERM CAPITAL GAINS (from Schedule D, line 22). Not less than 0."
- 28a "Income tax. Add lines 22 through 26"; 28b "4% Surtax (from Schedule 4% Surtax, line 7)"; 28 "Total tax. Add lines 28a and 28b".

**Personal exemptions (Form 1 line 2a; same both years):** "If single or married filing separately, enter $4,400. If head of household, enter $6,800. If married filing jointly, enter $8,800." The 2025 form reads "Single/Married filing separately ($4,400), Head of household ($6,800), Married filing jointly ($8,800)". Other line 2 amounts: dependents $1,000 each, age 65+ $1,700, blind $2,200. Statute: c.62 §3.B(b)(1)(A) "shall not exceed $4,400"; (1A) "$6,800"; (2) "$8,800".

**Rates:**
- c.62 §4(b) sets Part B at 5.3% with automatic step-downs and adds "Part B taxable income shall be taxed at a rate of not less than 5 per cent." DOR applies 5.0% ("multiply by 0.05", instructions line 22). §4(a)(2): Part A interest and dividends are "taxed at the same rate as provided for in subsection (b)", so 5.0%. §4(c): Part C (long-term) gains are taxed at the Part B rate, 5.0%.
- §4(a)(1): "Part A taxable income consisting of capital gains shall be taxed at the rate of 12 per cent; provided, however, that any gain from the sale or exchange of capital assets held for 1 year or less shall be taxed at the rate of 8.5 per cent."
- Optional 5.85% rate election: not elected in any case.

**4% surtax:**
- c.62 §4(d): "Where the sum of Part A taxable income, Part B taxable income and Part C taxable income exceeds $1,000,000 … the portion … exceeding $1,000,000 shall be taxed at … plus an additional 4 per cent. … any negative amount or loss in any part of taxable income shall not be applied to reduce income in any other part … the $1,000,000 … threshold … shall be subject to the cost-of-living adjustment".
- 2024 instructions p.3: "For the 2024 tax year, the threshold is $1,053,750." 2025 instructions p.3: "For the 2025 tax year, the threshold is $1,083,150."
- Schedule 4% Surtax (same both years): line 1 = Form 1 line 19; line 2 = Schedule B line 37; line 3 = Schedule D line 21; each "Not less than zero". Line 4 is the sum. Line 5 is the threshold ($1,053,750 or $1,083,150). Line 6 = line 4 − line 5. Line 7 = line 6 × .04, entered on Form 1 line 28b.
- **Surtax base = Form 1 L19 + Sch B L37 + Sch D L21.** Sch B L37 already includes both the 5% interest and dividends (L38 → Form 1 L20) and the 8.5%/12% gains (L39).

**Social Security wage base (for FICA and SE arithmetic):** $168,600 for 2024 and $176,100 for 2025 (IRS Schedule SE line 7 of each year). The base never binds the MA deduction, which caps at $2,000. FICA rates are 6.2% + 1.45%. SE tax is 92.35% of net SE earnings × (12.4% + 2.9%), and the federal deduction is 50% of SE tax (Schedule SE lines 4a, 10, 11, 12, 13).

**Massachusetts bank interest:** there is **no exclusion amount**. MA-bank deposit interest is excluded from Part A by c.62 §2(b)(1)(A), so it falls into Part B (5%). It goes on Form 1 line 5 and is fully included in line 10 (instructions p.9, "Line 5. Interest from Massachusetts Banks": "Enter in line 5 the total amount of interest received…"). Other interest goes to Schedule B. No case here has MA-bank interest.

## Q2. The $2,000 contribution deduction (lines 11a/11b)

Instructions, 2024 PDF p.10 and 2025 PDF p.11, "Line 11. Amount Paid to Social Security (FICA), Medicare, Railroad, U.S. or Massachusetts Retirement Systems". The text is identical in both years apart from the year:

> "If you have paid into any of the retirement systems listed above during 2024, you may deduct those contributions, up to a maximum of $2,000. Enter in lines 11a and 11b the amount you, and your spouse if filing jointly, paid to Social Security (FICA), Medicare or Railroad Retirement and the U.S. or Massachusetts retirement systems during 2024 as shown on your Form W-2, but not more than $2,000 each. Payment amounts may not be combined or transferred from one spouse to the other. Be sure to add any amount of Medicare tax withheld as shown on Form W-2 and any amount of self-employment tax as reported on your U.S. Form 1040 to the amount of Social Security tax withheld, the total not to exceed $2,000 per person. Note: Medicare premiums deducted from your Social Security or retirement payments are not deductible. Payments to an IRA, Keogh, … SEP … or SIMPLE … are not deductible for Massachusetts income tax purposes."

Statute: c.62 §3.B(a)(3) covers "Taxes paid to the United States under the provisions of the Federal Insurance Contributions Act or the Federal Railroad Retirement Act." It goes on: "In no event shall the aggregate of the otherwise allowable deductions of this subparagraph and of all sums deducted from wages as contributions to an annuity, pension … fund of the United States government, the commonwealth … attributable to any one taxpayer exceed two thousand dollars." §3.B(a)(4) covers government retirement contributions under the same per-taxpayer cap. (The caller cited §3.B(a)(9); that subparagraph is actually the rent deduction.)

- **Counts:** Social Security (OASDI) tax withheld (W-2 box 4), Medicare tax withheld (W-2 box 6), Railroad Retirement, U.S. or MA government retirement contributions, and SE tax from U.S. Form 1040 (Schedule 2 line 4).
- **Additional Medicare Tax:** the instruction says "any amount of Medicare tax withheld as shown on Form W-2". W-2 box 6 includes Additional Medicare Tax withheld, so it counts on the text. It is moot in practice: Additional Medicare Tax starts at $200k of wages, and regular FICA reaches the $2,000 cap at about $26,144 of wages (2,000 / 0.0765).
- **Joint returns: per spouse.** "but not more than $2,000 each … may not be combined or transferred from one spouse to the other … not to exceed $2,000 per person". The statute says "attributable to any one taxpayer". One earner gets 11a = 2,000 and 11b = 0, so the return gets $2,000. Two earners each at or above about $26,144 get $4,000.
- **W-2-only arithmetic:** 11a = min(2,000, 0.062 × min(wages, SS base) + 0.0145 × wages), rounded to whole dollars. Wages 15,000: 930.00 + 217.50 = 1,147.50, rounded to **1,148**. Wages 4,000: 248 + 58 = **306**. Wages of 26,144 or more give **2,000**.

## Q3. Self-employment adjustments

- **The federal deduction for one-half of SE tax is NOT allowed in Massachusetts.** c.62 §2(d)(1) admits the federal §62 deductions in computing Part B AGI, "provided, however, that the following deductions shall not be allowed: … (K) The deduction allowed by section one hundred and sixty-four (f) of the Code." §164(f) is the one-half SE tax deduction. The forms agree. Schedule Y has no line for it, and the line 9a instruction (2024 PDF p.20) says "Do not include amounts reported on U.S. Form 1040, Schedule 1, lines 12 through 23". The half-SE-tax deduction is Schedule 1 line 15. Form 1 line 6a takes MA Schedule C line 37 net profit, which carries no SE-tax deduction.
- **SE tax paid IS counted toward the $2,000 deduction**, per the line 11 instruction quoted above: "any amount of self-employment tax as reported on your U.S. Form 1040 … the total not to exceed $2,000 per person." The SE-tax portion belongs to the self-employed spouse's line (11a or 11b).

## Q4. Schedule B (same both years)

- **What goes there:** Part 1 lines 1–9 hold taxable interest (U.S. 1040 lines 2a+2b) and ordinary dividends (U.S. Sch B Part II line 6 / 1040 line 3b), less MA-bank interest (line 5) and exempt items such as U.S. obligations (line 6a). Line 9 is the subtotal. Part 2 lines 10–28 hold short-term gains and losses (U.S. Sch D lines 1–5 col. h), long-term collectibles and pre-1996 installment gains (line 11, from Sch D line 12), short-term §1231 gains and losses, and short-term carryovers (line 18).
- **8.5% treatment:** line 39 "Total taxable 8.5% and 12% capital gains. Subtract line 38 from line 37 … If no entry on Part 2, line 11, enter result here and on Form 1, line 23a". Form 1 line 23a multiplies by .085. With collectibles (line 11), the line 39 worksheet splits the total into 12% (23b) and 8.5% (23a).
- **Netting order, short-term loss** (statute §2(c)(2)(a); Sch B lines 19c–23):
  1. Line 20 applies the net ST loss against interest and dividends first: "Enter the smaller of line 9 or line 19c (considered as a positive amount). Not more than $2,000."
  2. Line 22 applies the remaining loss against long-term gains (also entered on Sch D line 14): "enter the smaller of Schedule B, line 21 (considered as a positive amount) or Schedule D, line 13".
  3. Line 23 carries the rest forward (line 40).
- **Netting order, long-term loss** (statute §2(c)(2)(b); Sch B line 25 / Sch D lines 14–16):
  1. Line 25 applies the net LT loss against net ST gains first: "enter the smaller of Schedule B, line 24 or Schedule D, line 13 (considered as a positive amount)".
  2. The worksheet for Sch B line 32 / Sch D line 16 applies it next against interest and dividends.
  3. Sch D line 23 carries the rest forward.
- **Up-to-$2,000 offset against interest and dividends:** c.62 §2(c)(4): "not more than an aggregate amount of $2,000 in Part A capital loss and Part C capital loss shall be applied against any interest and dividends". The Sch B line 32 worksheet enforces the shared cap ("2. Enter the lesser of line 1 or $2,000 … 3. Enter the amount from Schedule B, line 30 [ST loss already applied] … 4. Subtract line 3 from line 2"). The offset is also limited to the interest and dividends actually present. Capital losses never offset wages or other 5% Part B income.
- **Excess exemptions** (Form 1 line 19 instructions, 2024 PDF p.11): "If your total exemptions in Form 1, line 18 are more than the amount of your 5.0% income after deductions in Form 1, line 17, the excess may be applied against any interest and dividend income and income taxed at 8.5% or 12%. Any remaining excess amount may then be applied against any long-term capital gain income." The worksheet is titled "(Only if Single, Head of Household, or Married Filing Jointly)". Worksheet line 5 = min(Sch B line 35, L18 − L17), entered on Sch B line 36. Line 8 = min(Sch D line 19, remaining excess), entered on Sch D line 20. Statute: §3.A(b), which allows no excess exemption to a married person filing separately.
- **Feeds to Form 1:**
  - Line 35 = adjusted interest and dividends (L33) + ST gains after netting (L34 = L28).
  - L37 = L35 − L36.
  - L38: "If line 37 is greater than or equal to line 9, enter the amount from line 9 here and on Form 1, line 20 … If line 37 is less than line 9, enter the amount from line 37". This goes to **Form 1 line 20** (taxed at 5% through line 21/22).
  - L39 = L37 − L38 goes to **Form 1 line 23a**.
  - In effect, excess exemptions and capital losses reduce the interest-and-dividend layer before the gains layer only through this min(); L38 takes interest and dividends first, up to line 9.

## Q5. Schedule D (same both years)

- Lines 1–8 take long-term gains and losses from U.S. Sch D lines 8a/8b–13 col. h, Form 4797 Part II, and MA carryover (line 8). Line 9 combines them, line 10 holds MA adjustments, and line 11 is the MA net.
- Line 12 removes collectibles and pre-1996 installment gains to Sch B line 11. Line 13 is the subtotal.
- Line 14 holds the cross-netting with Sch B (an ST loss applied against an LT gain, or an LT loss applied against an ST gain). Line 15 is the subtotal.
- Line 16 holds the LT loss applied against interest and dividends (shared $2,000 cap). Line 17 is the subtotal. Line 19 = L17 − C-2 deductions, not less than 0.
- Line 20 holds excess exemptions (Single, HoH or MFJ only). Line 21 "Taxable long-term capital gains". Line 22 = L21 × .05, entered on **Form 1 line 24**. Line 23 is the carryover loss.
- A net loss on line 17 sets Form 1 line 24 to 0 and carries forward ("enter 0 on Form 1, line 24").

## Rounding

- Instructions "Whole Dollar Method Required" (2024 PDF p.8, 2025 PDF p.9): "DOR requires that the whole dollar method be used for entries made on forms or schedules. For example, amounts between $1.00 and $1.49 should be entered as $1.00 and amounts between $1.50 and $2.00 should be entered as $2.00." Worksheets may round each item then add, or add to the penny then round, used consistently. That is half-up rounding to whole dollars. Here the FICA lines are the only non-integer entries, and both methods give the same result.
- **Line 22 uses a tax table when line 21 ≤ $24,000.** The table heading reads "Use this table to calculate tax for taxable 5.0% income (line 21) of not more than $24,000". Line instruction: "If line 21 is less than $24,000, find the proper tax by using the tax tables … If line 21 is greater than $24,000 multiply by 0.05". The table has $50 brackets ("More than / But not more than"); the first bracket is $9–$50 with tax $1, and "If your 5.0% income for the tax table is less than $10, your tax is 0."
- Each bracket's tax equals 5% of the bracket midpoint, rounded half-up. Examples: $4,000–4,050 → $201 (5% × 4,025 = 201.25); $50–100 → $4 (3.75). The 2024 table (PDF p.36, outlined text, read visually) and the 2025 table (PDF p.38, text) agree on the rows used here: **8,250–8,300 → $414; 9,450–9,500 → $474.**
- Above $24,000, line 22 = line 21 × 0.05, rounded to whole dollars. Lines 23a, 24 (Sch D line 22) and 28b are always rate × amount, rounded to whole dollars.

## Ambiguities

1. **SE tax and the statute.** §3.B(a)(3) names only taxes under "the Federal Insurance Contributions Act or the Federal Railroad Retirement Act". SE tax is imposed under SECA (IRC ch. 2), not FICA (IRC ch. 21). The DOR instructions nonetheless direct taxpayers to include SE tax. I follow the DOR instruction, so case L takes 11a = $2,000. If SECA tax were held outside the statute, 11a would be $0; see the case L alternative.
2. **Exactly $24,000 on line 21.** The line 22 text says "less than" for the table and "greater than" for the rate; the table heading says "not more than $24,000". The two methods give the same tax at $24,000 ($1,200). No case lands there.
3. **Additional Medicare Tax.** Counted per the text ("Medicare tax withheld as shown on Form W-2"), but it never matters because of the cap.
4. **Case O and the Limited Income Credit.** MA AGI = 13,000, between $8,000 and $14,000, so the LIC worksheet applies even though the brief says "no credits". It produces 0: (13,000 − 8,000) × 10% = 500 exceeds the tax of 414, and the worksheet says "If line 4 is smaller than line 5, you are not eligible … Enter 0". Line 28 is unaffected either way (the LIC is line 29). In case B, MA AGI 15,000 exceeds $14,000, so there is no LIC and no No Tax Status.
5. **Federal AGI in case L:** this assumes no SE health insurance, retirement or QBI items. SE tax is computed to the cent then rounded: 8,477.73 → 8,478, and the half 4,238.87 → 4,239.
6. **Schedule SE, two-earner FICA in N:** irrelevant (cap).
7. **Source path:** mass.gov blocked automated fetch, so the official PDFs came from Internet Archive raw captures of the official URLs (see Sources). The 2024 instructions capture is the Dec 2025 one (a revised booklet hash relative to the Jan–Aug 2025 captures). Its line 11, line 19–24, Schedule B and Schedule D text matches the 2025 booklet.

## Per-case arithmetic

Common steps:

- L17 = L10 − L16.
- L19 = max(0, L17 − L18).
- L21 = L19 + L20.
- L22 = table if L21 ≤ 24,000, else 0.05 × L21.
- Surtax base = L19 + Sch B L37 + Sch D L21.
- FICA = 6.2% × min(wages, base) + 1.45% × wages.

**A. Single, wages 100,000.** FICA = 6,200 + 1,450 = 7,650, so 11a = 2,000. L17 98,000; L19 = 98,000 − 4,400 = 93,600; L22 = 4,680. Tax **4,680**. Fed AGI 100,000.

**B. Single, wages 15,000.** FICA = 930.00 + 217.50 = 1,147.50, rounded to 11a = 1,148 (under the cap). L17 = 13,852; L19 = 9,452 = L21.
