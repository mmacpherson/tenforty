spec, tables, graphs, tests or fixtures were read (only
`docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`, for the evidence format); no tax
software or calculator was run. The only computation aid was arithmetic and a
text extraction (`pdftotext`) of the official PDFs.

"Tax" below is **D-40 Line 20** (tax before the Line 21-23 non-refundable
credits, before Line 25 health-care shared responsibility, before refundable

## Rules applied (both years unless noted)

1. **Federal AGI → D-40 Line 4.** Wages (1040 line 1z) and capital gain (line 7 / 7a) are in total income (line 9); no adjustments are assumed, so AGI = total income. The 2025 federal enhanced senior deduction and the other Schedule 1-A items are below-the-AGI-line federally and are disallowed by DC anyway, so they do not affect Line 4.
2. **DC additions (Lines 5-6) and subtractions (Lines 8-14): none** apply to wage or long-term-capital-gain income of a full-year resident with no unemployment, Social Security, state refund or franchise income. DC AGI (Line 16) = federal AGI. DC has no preferential capital-gain rate on the D-40; the gain is ordinary DC taxable income.
3. **No personal or dependent exemptions.** The D-40 has no exemption line between Line 16 and Line 19; dependents do not change Line 19 or Line 20. (They matter only for credits, which are out of scope: 2024 scenarios with children could have Line 21/Schedule U/ELC/EITC effects after Line 20.)
4. **Standard deduction (Line 18).** 2024: follows federal — $14,600 single, $21,900 HOH, $29,200 MFJ; +$1,950 per box (65+/blind) single or HOH, +$1,550 married. 2025: DC's own — $15,000 single, $22,500 HOH, $30,000 MFJ; +$2,000 per box single or HOH, +$1,600 married.
5. **Itemized deduction (Line 18), Calculation F.** a = federal Schedule A line 17; b = Schedule A line 7 (total state and local taxes, after the federal cap); c = a − b; d = Schedule A line 5b real estate tax (added back in full, uncapped); e = Schedule A line 6 other taxes; f = c + d + e. If DC AGI ≤ $200,000 ($100,000 MFS), f goes to Line 18. Above that, lines g-n reduce f (other than medical, investment interest and casualty/theft) by 5% of DC AGI over $200,000. The deduction type must match the federal return (Line 17).
6. **Line 19** = Line 16 − Line 18.
7. **Line 20.** Line 19 ≤ $100,000: the tax table is required ("use the tax tables"). Line 19 > $100,000: Calculation I:

   | Taxable income | Tax |
   | --- | --- |
   | Not over $10,000 | 4% |
   | Over $10,000 but not over $40,000 | $400 + 6% of excess over $10,000 |
   | Over $40,000 but not over $60,000 | $2,200 + 6.5% of excess over $40,000 |
   | Over $60,000 but not over $250,000 | $3,500 + 8.5% of excess over $60,000 |
   | Over $250,000 but not over $500,000 | $19,650 + 9.25% of excess over $250,000 |
   | Over $500,000 but not over $1,000,000 | $42,775 + 9.75% of excess over $500,000 |
   | Over $1,000,000 | $91,525 + 10.75% of excess over $1,000,000 |

8. **Rounding.** "Do not enter cents. Round to the nearest dollar" ($x.50 rounds up). Every figure in these scenarios is already a whole dollar except none — all Calculation I results below are exact whole dollars, so rounding is not exercised. Table rows are $50 wide ("lower – lower+49") and print a whole-dollar tax.

The 2024 and 2025 tax tables were compared row by row after text extraction
(2,000 rows of $50 plus the "100,000" row each): they are identical.

## Uncertainties

1. **Table versus schedule.** For Line 19 ≤ $100,000 the booklet says "use the
   tax tables", so the table row is the figure reported. It differs from the
   rate schedule evaluated at the exact income by $1-$2 in every table case
   above. Up to $60,000 the printed rows equal the schedule at the row midpoint
   (lower + 25), rounded. From $60,000 up, all 800 printed rows are about $1
   below the midpoint value (e.g. row 70,000-70,049 prints 4,351; schedule at
   70,025 is 4,352.125; schedule at 70,000 is 4,350), in both years. I did not
   find a published explanation; the transcribed rows are what the booklet
   prints. An implementation computing the schedule at the midpoint will be $1
   high for scenarios 2, 4 and 5; one computing the exact schedule will be
   $1-$2 low everywhere in the table range. Whether OTR's e-file/MyTax
   processing accepts schedule-computed tax below $100,000 is not stated in the
   booklet.
2. **2025 text extraction.** Parts of the 2025 PDF extract with dropped glyphs
   (e.g. the p. 13 single amount reads "$ ,000", HOH "$22, 00", and the MFJ
   bullet on p. 4 is blank). The three basic amounts are each confirmed by at
   least two clean passages (p. 4 bullets for $15,000 and $22,500; p. 13 for
   $30,000; the p. 7 filing-requirements chart and the Schedule HSR chart for
   all three). The table rows used extracted cleanly. I did not view rendered
   page images.
3. **2025 booklet revision.** The file name is dated 08/20/26 (v1) while the
   cover says "Revised 05/2025"; I used the copy currently posted. I did not
   compare it with earlier postings of the 2025 booklet.
4. **Scenario 10 assumptions.** "8,000 is state and local income tax" is read as
   Schedule A line 5a = line 7 = 8,000 with no real estate, personal property or
   other taxes. If any of the remaining 12,000 were real estate tax, Calc F line
   d would add it back and the answer would change. Calc F line b is the
   *capped* federal SALT figure; a taxpayer with SALT above the federal cap is
   not tested here.
5. **Scope stops at Line 20.** Credits are excluded by the task. For the
   scenarios with children, later lines (Line 21 child-care credit, Schedule U,
   Schedule ELC, DC EITC; the 2025 letter says the DC Child Tax Credit is
   repealed for 2025) could change tax after credits; none is derived here.
   Line 25 (health-care shared responsibility) is assumed zero.
6. **Federal AGI** is taken as wages (+ gain) with no adjustments; IRS forms
   were consulted only for line identities. No IRS worked example was used.
7. **Married filing separately on the same return** (Calculation J) was not
   considered for the joint scenarios; the task specifies married filing jointly.
