Blind derivation, 2026-11-11. Sources: Utah State Tax Commission and IRS PDFs
only. The only tenforty file read was `docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`.
No tax software, tenforty backend, or third-party calculator was run. A short
script did the multiplication and rounding on the transcribed parameters; it
contains no tax logic beyond the form lines shown below.

**Evidence kind for every expected value here: Independent derivation.** None is
a published worked example. Utah publishes no tax table; the rate, exemption
amount, base phase-out amounts and federal standard deduction amounts are
published form/instruction amounts (parameters), transcribed as cited.

"State tax" is TC-40 line 22, Utah income tax: line 10 less the Taxpayer Tax
Credit on line 20, before the apportionable and nonapportionable credits on
lines 24 and 26 and before withholding.

### Rules applied

- **Rounding** (U24I and U25I p. 3, identical wording): "Round off cents to the
  nearest whole dollar. Round down if under 50 cents; round up if 50 cents and
  above. Do not enter cents on the return." Every TC-40 money field is printed
  ".00". Each computed line (10, 16, 19) is therefore rounded half-up to whole
  dollars before it feeds the next line.
- **Line 4**: federal AGI from 1040 line 11 (2024) or line 11a (2025). Wages
  only, no adjustments, so AGI equals wages.
- **Line 9**: line 6 less lines 7 and 8. No additions, refund or subtractions,
  so Utah taxable income equals federal AGI. The federal standard deduction is
  not subtracted from Utah taxable income; it enters only through the credit.
- **Line 2 / line 11**: qualifying dependents are those "you were allowed to
  claim for a tax credit" in the federal Dependents section (IRC section 24).
  The filer and spouse are not counted. Each scenario's dependents are taken as
  claimed for the child tax credit or credit for other dependents, none born in
  the tax year (a birth-year dependent is counted twice, on 2a and 2c).
- **Line 12**: 2024, "federal standard or itemized deduction from line 12 of
  your federal return"; 2025, "from line 12e of your federal return (1040,
  1040-SR)". The age-65 additional amount is part of federal line 12 / 12e, so
  it is included. The 2025 enhanced deduction for seniors is reported on
  Schedule 1-A and reaches Form 1040 on line 13b, not 12e, so it is **not** in
  Utah line 12 (see Uncertainties).
- **Line 14**: blank when the federal standard deduction is claimed.
- **Lines 18, 20, 22**: each floored at zero.
- **Line 21**: exempt only if federal AGI is at or below the basic federal
  standard deduction (age/blindness extras excluded; the 2025 worksheet adds
  the Schedule 1-A line 37 senior deduction). No scenario qualifies.

## Uncertainties

1. **Scenario 8, 2025, enhanced deduction for seniors.** A single filer aged 67
   with $50,000 of wages would ordinarily also take the 2025 federal enhanced
   deduction for seniors ($6,000 per the 2025 Form 1040 instructions). I
   excluded it from Utah line 12 because U25I p. 8 says to enter the amount
   from federal line 12e, and F25F puts Schedule 1-A deductions on line 13b.
   The Utah instructions name the senior deduction only in the line 21
   exemption worksheet. That reading follows the form text and I found no
   contrary Utah statement, but I found no Utah statement that says outright
   "the senior deduction is not part of the Taxpayer Tax Credit" either. If it
   were included, line 12 would be 23,750, line 16 1,425, line 20 1,012 and
   line 22 1,238 instead of 1,598. I did not verify the senior deduction's
   eligibility conditions or phase-out threshold from Schedule 1-A itself; they
   do not affect the value under the line 12e reading.
2. **Whole-dollar rounding of intermediate lines.** The values round lines 10,
   16 and 19 individually, as the paper form requires. An implementation that
   carries cents and rounds only the result can differ by $1. The affected
   cases are those where the unrounded line 10, 16 or 19 has cents (shown in
   each table); scenarios 4 and 9 have a zero credit and whole-dollar line 10,
   so they are insensitive to this.
3. **Dependents.** The scenarios say "dependent child" or "dependents" without
   ages. The count on line 2d is the same whether a dependent goes on 2a (16 or
   under) or 2b (other), so age does not matter, provided each is claimed for a
   federal dependent credit and none was born in the tax year. I did not fetch
   Schedule 8812 to confirm credit eligibility at $250,000 MFJ; Utah's test is
   that the dependent was claimed for the credit in the federal Dependents
   section, which I took as given.
4. **Scenario 8 date of birth.** "Age 67" is taken as born before January 2,
   1960 (2024) and before January 2, 1961 (2025), not blind: one box checked.
5. **Scenario 1, 2025** is zero because the credit (922) exceeds the tax (900),
   not because of the line 21 exemption (AGI 20,000 exceeds 15,750).
6. **Document revisions.** The PDFs are the versions served on 2026-11-11. I
   did not check for later errata or a revised 2025 TC-40; the form and the
   instruction booklet agree with each other on every parameter in both years.
7. **Statute not consulted.** Citations are to forms and instructions only; I
   did not read Utah Code 59-10-104 or 59-10-1018 to confirm the amounts.
