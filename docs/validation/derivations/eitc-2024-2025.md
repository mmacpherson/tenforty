# EITC scenarios E1–E9, tax years 2024 and 2025

Evidence kind: EIC values are published table rows (EIC Table); other lines independent derivation from official IRS forms and instructions.

Derived by: Claude subagent, blind — did not read or run anything in the tenforty repo, and used no tax software.

Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-11-03. Both a 2024 and a 2025 Publication 596 are published, and both were used.

Line numbering differs between years. In 2024, AGI is Form 1040 line 11 and the EIC is line 27. In 2025, AGI is line 11a, carried to line 11b, and the EIC is line 27a. "Line 27" below means 2024 line 27 or 2025 line 27a.

## Summary

The EIC row is "at least – but less than". The column is S/HOH for Single or Head of household, or MFJ, followed by the number of children counted (capped at 3).

Line 24 is shown two ways:

- **24 (A)**: no line 19 credit (no child tax credit or credit for other dependents is claimed). This is the primary figure.
- **24 (B)**: every EITC child is also under 17 and qualifies for the child tax credit (CTC), claimed on Schedule 8812. Only the nonrefundable line 19 portion matters here, capped at the tax. See the Assumptions section.

In every scenario, the EIC on line 27 is a refundable credit in the Payments section. In 2024 it enters total other payments and refundable credits on line 32 ("Add lines 27, 28, 29, and 31"). In 2025 the same line 32 reads "Add lines 27a, 28, 29, 30, and 31". Line 32 then feeds total payments on line 33. The EIC does **not** reduce line 24 (total tax). Refund or amount owed comes from comparing line 33 with line 24, on lines 34 and 37.

## Worked arithmetic per scenario

The steps below are the Form 1040 instructions, "Lines 27a, 27b, and 27c—Earned Income Credit (EIC)" for 2025 and "Line 27—Earned Income Credit (EIC)" for 2024. All scenarios pass these steps unless noted otherwise:

- Step 1, questions 2–4: valid SSNs, no Form 2555, no nonresident alien.
- Step 2: investment income test.
- Step 3: qualifying child.
- Step 4: childless filers. Age 30 is at least 25 and under 65. Main home was in the U.S. more than half the year. Not a qualifying child of another person. Not a dependent.
- Step 5: earned income.

Unless noted, the filer has no Schedule E, no rental of personal property, no Form 8814 and no passive activity. So Step 2 question 4 is "No" and Pub 596 Worksheet 1 is not *required*. Its result is shown anyway where interest exists, and it matches Step 2.

## Rounding rules

**EIC Table, not the formula.** Both worksheets direct the filer to "Look up the amount … in the EIC Table … Enter the credit here." Neither tells the filer to compute the statutory formula, so the published table value is the EIC.

- Rows are $50 wide ("At least – But less than"), except the first row, which is 1–50.
- The IRS documents reviewed do not state how the table is built. As a consistency check only (not used as evidence), each value used here equals the statutory formula evaluated at the row **midpoint**, rounded to the nearest dollar. The formula uses phase-in rates of 7.65% / 34% / 40% / 45% and phase-out rates of 7.65% / 15.98% / 21.06% / 21.06%, per IRC §32(b)(1); that is background, not an irs.gov source. Examples:
  - E3 2025: 4,328 − 0.1598 × (25,025 − 23,350) = 4,060.3 → 4,060.
  - E5 2025: 8,046 − 0.2106 × (55,025 − 30,470) = 2,874.7 → 2,875.
  - E4 2024: 6,960 − 0.2106 × (30,025 − 29,640) = 6,878.9 → 6,879.
  - E7a 2024: 6,960 − 0.2106 × (31,625 − 22,720) = 5,084.6 → 5,085.
  - E8 2024: 4,213 − 0.1598 × (24,025 − 22,720) = 4,004.5 → 4,004.
  - E8 2025: 4,328 − 0.1598 × (24,025 − 23,350) = 4,220.1 → 4,220.
- A formula evaluated at the exact amount can differ from the table by up to half a row times the phase rate. That is about ±$5.27 at a 21.06% phase-out, or ±$11.25 at the 45% phase-in. Precision tolerance for EIC tests should be $0 against the table, and up to that half-row bound if comparing a formula engine.
- At the completed phase-out there are special footnote rows. 2025: "at least $19,100 but less than $19,104 … your credit is $0". 2024: "at least $18,550 but less than $18,591 … your credit is $2". None of the scenarios lands in one.

**Tax Table.** Line 16 values are Tax Table rows: $25 wide below $3,000 and $50 wide from $3,000. Each value equals the bracket tax at the row midpoint, rounded. For example, 2025 MFJ: 23,525 × 10% = 2,352.5 → 2,353.

**Whole dollars.** 2025 1040 instructions p. 23, "Rounding Off to Whole Dollars" (2024 p. 23, same text):

> "You can round off cents to whole dollars on your return and schedules. If you do round to whole dollars, you must round all amounts. To round, drop amounts under 50 cents and increase amounts from 50 to 99 cents to the next dollar… If you have to add two or more amounts to figure the amount to enter on a line, include cents when adding the amounts and round off only the total."

This creates the E9 ±$1 ambiguity in SE tax, 2,119.43 versus 2,120. It does not affect the EIC row.

## Assumptions and ambiguities

1. **Line 24 depends on line 19, which the scenarios do not fix.** The facts make each child an "EITC child" (under 19, or a student under 24) but give no age relative to 17.
   - **Primary (A)**: no CTC or credit for other dependents is claimed, so line 24 = line 16 (+ SE tax for E9).
   - **(B)**: all children are under 17 and qualify for the CTC. The nonrefundable CTC (2,000 per child in 2024, 2,200 in 2025) is at least line 18 in every child scenario, so line 24 = 0. E9 is the exception: line 24 = SE tax.
   - If the children qualify only for the $500 credit for other dependents (ages 17–18), the only scenario where it doesn't eliminate the tax is E5: line 24 = 1,135 (2024) / 853 (2025).
   - The EIC itself is unaffected by any of this.
2. **E9 filing status.** Single is kept, with the assumption that the filer is not HOH-eligible (did not pay more than half the cost of keeping up the home). With HOH the EIC, line 16 (0) and line 24 would all be the same.
3. **E9 rounding.** SE tax is 2,119.43 in cents. It becomes 2,120 when every Schedule SE line is rounded to whole dollars (line 4a 13,852.50 → 13,853), or 2,119 when cents are carried and only the final entry is rounded. AGI and earned income are 13,940.28 or 13,940 and fall in the same EIC row either way. A ±$1 tolerance on E9 line 24 is justified by the published whole-dollar rule.
4. **E9 assumptions:** there are no business expenses beyond those already netted into the 15,000 Schedule C line 31 profit, and no optional method is used (the filer is ineligible anyway). The QBI deduction is irrelevant because taxable income is already 0.
5. **Interest.** All interest is taxable interest on line 2b. Tax-exempt interest (line 2a), dividends, capital gains, Schedule E, Form 8814 and passive activity are all zero. So Step 2 investment income equals the taxable interest, and Pub 596 Worksheet 1 gives the same total.
6. **E7a/E7b "limit".** This is the year's statutory investment income limit: 11,600 for 2024 and 11,950 for 2025. The test is "more than", so exactly at the limit qualifies.
7. **E2.** The filer is ineligible at Step 1, because AGI 20,000 is not less than 18,591 / 19,104. The EIC Table row shows 0 as well. 20,000 is above the childless completed phase-out in both years.
8. **E8.** AGI is not equal to earned income and is above the threshold phase-out amount, so the table is read at both. The EIC is the smaller amount, which is the AGI lookup in both years.
9. **No other credits or taxes.** There is no additional Medicare tax and no AMT (Schedule 2 line 3 = 0). Withholding and refunds are outside the scope.
10. **Source caveat.** Pub 596 (2025) refers the reader to the 1040 instructions for EIC Worksheets A and B; it does not reproduce them. The worksheets were therefore taken from the 1040 instructions for each year.
11. **Minor source inconsistency, no effect here.** Pub 596 (2025) p. 21 says to see the optional methods "If your net earnings (without using the optional methods) are less than $7,240". The 2025 Schedule SE nonfarm optional method limit is net nonfarm profits "less than $7,840". E9 is above both.
