# Colorado individual income tax, TY2024 and TY2025: independent derivation

## Header

- **Scope:** Colorado full-year resident individual income tax (Form DR 0104) for tax years 2024 and 2025. The derivation computes Colorado taxable income and Colorado tax after nonrefundable credits for simple returns: wages only, plus one case with taxable interest and long-term capital gains. All returns take the federal standard deduction, claim no dependents, and all filers are under 65.
- **Method:** Blind and source-only. Every value is computed by hand from the official form lines, tax table and statute. No tax software or library was run, and nothing under the tenforty repository or its worktrees was read.
- **Derived by:** Claude (Anthropic), model Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`), on 2026-11-03.
- **Evidence kind for every value in this file:** Independent derivation. Tax-table lookups are transcribed table rows (cited below). A whole return that uses a table row is still an independent derivation.

## Rounding and tax-computation conventions

1. **Whole dollars.** The DR 0104 is headed "Round To The Nearest Dollar" (S1 form p. 3 of 8; the S2 form carries the same header). Every line in this file is a whole dollar. The stipulated federal figures are already whole dollars.
2. **Tax table below $50,000 is mandatory in practice.** The line 12 instruction (2024) and line 13 instruction (2025) say full-year residents "should refer to the tax table". The table covers taxable income up to $50,000 in $100 brackets, except the lowest rows ($0–10, 10–30, 30–50, 50–75, 75–100). The worksheet applies only to "taxable incomes over $50,000". For Colorado taxable income of $50,000 or less this file therefore uses the table row and cites it.
   - Row semantics: "Over X, But not over Y". Income exactly equal to a row's upper bound falls in that row. For example, $5,400 falls in "Over $5,300 But not over $5,400".
   - Each row's tax equals the rate times the bracket midpoint, rounded. I checked this against every row used here; for example, 2024 $5,350 × 0.0425 = $227.375, which matches the table's $227. Because of the midpoint convention, table tax can differ from rate × income by up to about $2.
3. **Over $50,000:** tax = Colorado taxable income × 0.0425 (2024) or × 0.044 (2025), rounded to the nearest dollar.
4. **Half-dollar results.** Three computations land exactly on $0.50: 2024 Single $150k, 2024 Single AGI $300,000 under the booklet reading, and 2025 HoH $75k. Colorado does not state a tie rule. **I round half up**, which is the general nearest-dollar convention. The alternative (round down or to even) is $1 lower; see the ambiguities section.
5. **Rates.** 2024: 4.25%, from S1 p. 11 and the table title. This is the temporary TABOR rate reduction under C.R.S. 39-22-627, which displaces the 4.40% base rate in 39-22-104(1.7)(c). 2025: 4.40%, from S2 p. 10 and the table title, which equals the statutory base rate.

## Stipulated federal inputs (shared by all cases)

- Federal AGI equals wages, plus taxable interest and long-term capital gain where a case has them. There are no adjustments to income.
- Federal taxable income (1040 line 15) = AGI − standard deduction, floored at 0. The standard deduction is the stipulated figure:
  - 2024: Single $14,600, MFJ $29,200, HoH $21,900.
  - 2025 (OBBBA): Single $15,750, MFJ $31,500, HoH $23,625.
- No QBI deduction and no Schedule 1-A deductions (tips, overtime, seniors, car-loan interest). Under 2025 1040 numbering, the standard deduction is the full deduction on line 12e as the CO booklet references it. Long-term capital gain is fully included in federal taxable income; preferential rates affect federal tax, not taxable income.

## Assumptions

1. **Residency and filing status.** Each return is a full-year Colorado resident, with Colorado filing status matching federal. No DR 0104PN.
2. **Federal starting figures.** Federal taxable income and AGI are the stipulated inputs computed above, with no above-the-line adjustments, QBI deduction or Schedule 1-A deductions. For 2025 the Colorado instruction cites 1040 lines 11a (AGI) and 12e (deduction). I took those line references from the CO booklet and did not separately verify them against the 2025 IRS Form 1040.
3. **Interest and gain.** Interest is ordinary taxable interest, not U.S. obligations (which would be a DR 0104AD subtraction) and not non-Colorado municipal bond interest (which would be an L8 or L9 addition). The LTCG is not from Colorado agricultural land, the only capital-gain subtraction left on the 2024 and 2025 DR 0104AD.
4. **No other subtractions or additions.** That means no Social Security, pensions, 529 or ABLE activity, charitable subtraction, business meals, or state income tax addback. The state income tax addback cannot arise under the standard deduction.
5. **Nonrefundable credits are $0.** No DR 0104CR nonrefundable credit applies to these fact patterns. Refundable credits (Colorado EITC, child tax credit, family affordability tax credit) and the TABOR sales-tax refund are excluded by scope; they do not change "tax after nonrefundable credits".
6. **HoH with "no dependents".** Federal HoH needs a qualifying person. I assume the qualifying person yields no Colorado nonrefundable credit, so the HoH result depends only on the HoH standard deduction. HoH is treated as "single" for the L4 addback limit ($12,000), per the booklet text and the statute's single/joint split.
7. **AMT and recapture are $0.**
8. **Rounding.** Results land on whole dollars; exact halves round up (see A3).

## Ambiguities

- **A1. Is AGI of exactly $300,000 inside the addback?**
  - The statute, C.R.S. 39-22-104(3)(p.5)(I), says "equal to or exceeding three hundred thousand dollars".
  - Both DOR booklets (S1 p. 9, S2 p. 9) say AGI "exceeds $300,000".
  - At exactly $300,000 the two readings differ by $110 in 2024 and $165 in 2025 (cases T1). I give the statute reading as primary because the statute controls. I also report the booklet reading, which tax software following DOR instructions may implement.
  - At $300,001 the readings agree.
- **A2. The 2025 worksheet label is stale.** The 2025 "Worksheet for taxable incomes over $50,000" (S2 p. 35) reads "Colorado Taxable Income from Form 104 line 11". Colorado taxable income is line **12** on the 2025 form; line 11 is subtractions. This reads as a carry-over typo from the 2024 numbering, and I used line 12. The table header on S2 p. 34 correctly says "line 12".
- **A3. Half-dollar ties.** Colorado says "Round To The Nearest Dollar" but gives no tie rule. Three results are exact halves, and I round them up:
  - 2024 Single $150k: 5,754.50 → 5,755.
  - 2024 T1 booklet reading: 12,129.50 → 12,130.
  - 2025 HoH $75k: 2,260.50 → 2,261.

  An engine that rounds half to even, or truncates, would give $1 less. A $1 tolerance on these three cases would be justified by the missing tie rule, not by any observed mismatch.
- **A4. Table vs rate below $50,000.** The instruction says full-year residents "should refer to the tax table". I treat the table as the prescribed computation for taxable income of $50,000 or less. A pure rate × income engine would differ:
  - 2024 Single $20k: 229.50 vs 227.
  - 2024 Single $60k: 1,929.50 vs 1,927.
  - 2024 MFJ $60k: 1,309 vs 1,307.
  - 2024 T4: 2,125 vs 2,123.
  - 2025 T4: 2,200 vs 2,198.
  - 2025 A, B and E coincide with the table.

  A test that accepts either should allow the table's midpoint error of up to about rate × $50. That bound comes from the table's construction, not from observation.
