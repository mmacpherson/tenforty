# Arizona Form 140 (full-year resident), tax years 2024 and 2025 — independent derivation

Derived 2026-11-11 by a blind Claude subagent from Arizona Department of Revenue
(ADOR) and IRS publications only. No tenforty code, spec, table, graph, test or
fixture was read (only `docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`, for the
evidence format). No tax software or third-party calculator was used.

**Evidence kind for every whole-return value below: Independent derivation.**
The parameters (standard deduction, exemption amounts, 2.5% rate, credit
amounts, phase-out thresholds) are transcribed **published instruction
amounts**; the scenarios and arithmetic are the deriver's. None is a published
worked example. Arizona publishes no tax table for 2023 and later, so there are
no published table rows.

### Rules transcribed (page numbers are the printed page of A24i / A25i; identical placement in both years unless noted)

- **Rounding** (p. 4, "Tips for Preparing Your Return"): "You must round dollar
  amounts to the nearest whole dollar. If 50 cents or more, round up to the next
  dollar. If less than 50 cents, round down. Do not enter cents." Applied to
  every line, so line 46 is rounded half-up to whole dollars.
- **Line 12** (p. 8): federal adjusted gross income from the federal return;
  "Your federal adjusted gross income is your Arizona gross income." Federal:
  F24 line 1z (wages) → line 7 (capital gain) → line 9 (total income) → line 11
  (AGI); F25 line 1z → 7a → 9 → 11a. With no adjustments, AGI = wages (+ capital
  gain in scenario 8).
- **Lines 13–19**: no small business election, no additions → line 19 = line 12.
- **Lines 20–24** (p. 13; worksheet p. 32): "You may subtract 25% (.25) of any
  net long-term capital gain included in your federal adjusted gross income that
  is derived from an investment in an asset acquired after December 31, 2011."
  Line 23 note: "Only include net long-term capital gains on this line if it can
  be verified that the asset was acquired after December 31, 2011. If the date
  of acquisition cannot be determined, the asset is considered to have been
  acquired before January 1, 2012." An asset acquired by gift or inheritance
  takes the date the donor/decedent acquired it. Line 24 = line 23 × 25%.
  Lines 20–23 must be completed to take line 24. Same text both years.
- **Line 35** (form): line 19 minus lines 24 through 34c. **Line 36**: Other
  Subtractions (page 6 schedule). **Line 37** = line 35 − line 36.
- **Line 36, item V "Other Adjustments", 2025 only** (A25i p. 20: "See MCTCP
  worksheet."; MCTCP worksheet line 5): "Taxpayers who qualify for the following
  federal deductions may also take these deductions on their 2025 Arizona state
  individual income tax return: … Enhanced deduction for seniors"; amount =
  IRS Schedule 1-A line 37. S1A Part V: line 32 threshold $75,000 ($150,000
  MFJ); line 33 "If zero or less, enter $6,000 on line 35"; line 36a requires a
  valid SSN and birth before January 2, 1961. The 2024 instructions have no
  such item.
- **Line 38** (p. 20): number in box 8 (age 65 or over, taxpayer/spouse) ×
  $2,100. **Line 39** blind × $1,500. **Line 40** other exemptions × $2,300.
  **Line 41** qualifying parents/grandparents × $10,000. There is no personal
  or dependent exemption; dependents are handled by the line 49 credit.
- **Line 42** Arizona adjusted gross income = line 37 − lines 38–41.
- **Line 43 standard deduction** (p. 21):

  | Filing status | 2024 | 2025 |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | Single | 14,600 | 15,750 |
  | Married filing joint | 29,200 | 31,500 |
  | Married filing separate | 14,600 | 15,750 |
  | Head of household | 21,900 | 23,625 |

  No additional standard deduction for age or blindness is listed (age is
  handled by line 38).
- **Line 44** (p. 21): increase equal to 33% (2024) / 34% (2025) of charitable
  contributions that would have been allowed if itemizing. Assumed
  contributions = 0, so line 44 = 0 in every scenario.
- **Line 45** Arizona taxable income = line 42 − line 43 − line 44, not below 0.
- **Line 46** (p. 21, and the form): "Multiply line 45 by 2.5% (.025) and enter
  the result." **The rate is required; there is no tax table.** OTT page:
  "For tax year 2023 and beyond, there are no optional or X&Y tax tables to post
  due to Arizona's flat tax rate of 2.5%."
- **Line 47** recapture = 0; **line 48** = line 46 + line 47.
- **Line 49 Dependent Tax Credit** (pp. 21–22): nonrefundable; $100 per
  qualifying dependent under 17 at year end (box 10a); $25 per qualifying
  dependent at least 17 (box 10b). Reduced only if federal AGI (line 12) is
  more than $200,000 (single/MFS/HOH) or more than $400,000 (MFJ): the excess
  over the limit selects a factor from Table V (.95 for $1–1,000, stepping down
  .05 per $1,000 band, .00 at $19,001 and over), and the Table I credit is
  multiplied by it. Same thresholds and table both years.
- **Line 50 Family Income Tax Credit** (pp. 22–23): available only if Worksheet
  I line 8 (line 42 plus lines 38–41) does not exceed $10,000 (single/MFS),
  $20,000–$26,575 (HOH), $20,000–$31,000 (MFJ). Every scenario exceeds its
  maximum, so line 50 = 0.
- **Line 51** Form 301 credits = 0 (none assumed).
- **Line 52 Balance of Tax** (p. 25): line 48 − lines 49, 50, 51; if the credits
  exceed line 48, enter 0. This is the "Arizona tax before withholding" reported
  here. The Increased Excise Tax Credit (line 56) is a refundable credit below
  line 52 and requires line 12 ≤ $25,000 (MFJ/HOH) or ≤ $12,500 (single/MFS);
  no scenario qualifies.

## Precision

Every amount is a whole dollar under the Form 140 rounding rule (p. 4); line 46
is rounded half-up. Expected values are exact to the dollar: tolerance 0 for a
whole-dollar implementation. An implementation that does not round line 46
would differ by under $1 (at most $0.50) on the rows marked with cents above;
that would be a rounding difference from the form, not a rate difference.

## Uncertainties

1. **2025 scenario 7 — MCTCP senior subtraction (material: 654 vs 804).** The
   ADOR worksheet and the 2025 instructions (item V, "See MCTCP worksheet")
   let a Form 140 filer subtract the federal enhanced senior deduction
   (Schedule 1-A line 37, here 6,000). The worksheet says the taxpayer "may"
   take it. I did not verify the underlying legal authority (statute or
   executive order) or whether ADOR treats the subtraction as optional or
   expected; a web search surfaced only non-official claims about 2026
   legislation, which I did not rely on. I also could not find 2025 text on
   ADOR's conformity page (it showed only 2024 and earlier when fetched). Both
   branches are derived; 7a follows ADOR's published form materials as they
   stand on 2026-11-11.
2. **2025 form revision dates.** The 2025 Form 140 PDF was modified 2026-11-02
   and the instructions 2026-08-21, i.e. revised after the filing season. I
   used the current versions and did not compare them with the originally
   released ones. The figures used (standard deduction 15,750 / 31,500 /
   23,625, rate 2.5%, exemptions, credit amounts) are as printed in the
   current versions.
3. **Federal eligibility assumptions for 7a**: taxpayer born before
   1961-01-02 with a valid SSN; MAGI equals AGI (50,000). The IRS Schedule 1-A
   form face was read; its separate instructions were not.
4. **Scenario 8** depends on the acquisition date, which the scenario does not
   state; both branches are given. The 10,000 is assumed to be a net long-term
   gain with no short-term amounts, no capital loss carryover, and not from a
   qualified small business or legal-tender exchange.
5. **Dependents** are assumed to qualify as federal dependents with the
   required identifying information; the instructions warn the credit can be
   lost otherwise.
6. **Line-reference discrepancy, no effect here**: the form says line 51 comes
   from Form 301 "Part 2, line 60" while the instructions say "line 62". Line
   51 is zero in every scenario.
7. The 2024 instructions contain a typo in a surviving-spouse note
   ("$29,2000"); the standard deduction table itself reads $29,200, which is
   what was used.
8. Federal AGI was taken as wages (plus the capital gain) directly from the
   Form 1040 line structure; no federal return was otherwise computed, and no
   federal tax figures are asserted.
