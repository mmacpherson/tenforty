## Part A: Published source transcription (verbatim)

### A1. Statute, tax year 2024: R.C. 5747.05(E), eff. 10/3/2023 (S1)

> (E)(1) On a joint return filed by a husband and wife, each of whom had adjusted gross income of at least five hundred dollars, exclusive of interest, dividends and distributions, royalties, rent, and capital gains, a credit equal to the lesser of six hundred fifty dollars or the percentage shown in column B that corresponds with the taxpayer's modified adjusted gross income, less exemptions for the taxable year, of the total amount of tax due after allowing for any other credit that precedes this credit as required under section 5747.98 of the Revised Code:

| A. IF THE MODIFIED ADJUSTED GROSS INCOME, LESS EXEMPTIONS, FOR THE TAX YEAR IS: | B. THE CREDIT FOR THE TAXABLE YEAR IS: |
|---|---|
| $25,000 or less | 20% |
| More than $25,000 but not more than $50,000 | 15% |
| More than $50,000 but not more than $75,000 | 10% |
| More than $75,000 | 5% |

> (2) The credit shall be claimed in the order required under section 5747.98 of the Revised Code.

### A2. Statute, tax year 2025: R.C. 5747.05(E), eff. 9/30/2025 (S2)

(E)(1) is word-for-word the same as A1, except that the lead-in ends "...as required under section 5747.98 of the Revised Code, subject to division (E)(2) of this section:". The table is identical to A1. Division (E)(2) now reads:

> (2) The credit shall be claimed in the order required under section 5747.98 of the Revised Code. No taxpayer shall claim this credit unless the taxpayer's modified adjusted gross income is less than seven hundred fifty thousand dollars for taxable years beginning in 2025 or less than five hundred thousand dollars for taxable years beginning in 2026 or thereafter.

### A3. Definitions: R.C. 5747.01 (S7)

> (A) "Adjusted gross income" or "Ohio adjusted gross income" means federal adjusted gross income, as defined and used in the Internal Revenue Code, adjusted as provided in this section:

> (II) "Modified adjusted gross income" means Ohio adjusted gross income plus any amount deducted under divisions (A)(28) and (34) of this section for the taxable year.

(A)(28) begins: "Deduct from the portion of an individual's federal adjusted gross income that is business income, ..." (the business income deduction). (A)(34) begins: "Deduct amounts as provided under section 5747.79 of the Revised Code related to the taxpayer's qualifying capital gains and deductible payroll."

(The 5747.01 text was retrieved from the current version, eff. March 5, 2026; these two definitions are quoted only for their meaning of "adjusted gross income" and "modified adjusted gross income".)

### A4. 2024 IT 1040 instructions, Schedule of Credits Line 12 (S3, pp. 29-30)

> **Line 12 – Joint Filing Credit**
> To qualify for this credit, you and your spouse must each have at least $500 of qualifying income and jointly file your return. "Qualifying income" is any amount included in Ohio adjusted gross income, other than the following:
> ● Interest;
> ● Dividends and distributions;
> ● Capital gains; AND
> ● Rents and royalties.
>
> Amounts deducted on the Ohio Schedule of Adjustments are not included in Ohio adjusted gross income, and thus are not "qualifying income." Examples of such amounts include business income, state and local tax refunds, Social Security and railroad retirement benefits, and certain military compensation and retirement benefits.
>
> The credit equals a percentage of your tax liability prior to the application of the credit. The maximum credit per return is $650. The percentage used is based on your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) less exemptions, as shown in the following table:

| MAGI less exemptions | Credit (max $650) |
|---|---|
| 0 - $25,000 | 20% of line 11 |
| $25,001 - $50,000 | 15% of line 11 |
| $50,001 - $75,000 | 10% of line 11 |
| $75,001 or more | 5% of line 11 |

> Example: Kevin and Krysten file jointly. Kevin has $50,000 of wage income and $5,000 of interest income. Krysten has $40,000 of business income and $20,000 of taxable Social Security benefits. While Kevin has $50,000 of qualifying income (his wages), Krysten has no qualifying income because her business income and taxable Social Security benefits were both deducted in calculating their Ohio adjusted gross income. Therefore, they are not entitled to the joint filing credit.
>
> Please include a separate statement listing the qualifying income for each spouse. See R.C. 5747.05(E).

### A5. 2025 IT 1040 instructions, Schedule of Credits Line 12 (S4, p. 29)

> **Line 12 – Joint Filing Credit**
> To qualify, you and your spouse must each have at least $500 of qualifying income, jointly file your return, and have an MAGI less than $750,000. "Qualifying income" is any amount included in Ohio adjusted gross income (AGI), other than the following:
> ● Interest;
> ● Dividends and distributions;
> ● Capital gains; AND
> ● Rents and royalties.
>
> Amounts deducted on the Ohio Schedule of Adjustments are not included in Ohio AGI, and thus are not "qualifying income." Examples of such amounts include business income, state and local tax refunds, Social Security and railroad retirement benefits, and certain military compensation and retirement benefits.
>
> The credit equals a percentage of your tax liability prior to the application of the credit. The maximum credit per return is $650. The percentage used is based on your MAGI less exemptions, as shown in the following table:

| MAGI less exemptions | Credit (max $650) |
|---|---|
| 0 - $25,000 | 20% of line 11 |
| $25,001 - $50,000 | 15% of line 11 |
| $50,001 - $75,000 | 10% of line 11 |
| $75,001 - $749,999 | 5% of line 11 |

> Example: Kevin and Krysten file jointly. Kevin has $50,000 of wage income and $5,000 of interest income. Krysten has $40,000 of business income and $20,000 of taxable Social Security benefits. While Kevin has $50,000 of qualifying income (his wages), Krysten has no qualifying income because her business income and taxable Social Security benefits were both deducted in calculating their Ohio AGI. Therefore, they are not entitled to the joint filing credit.
>
> Please include a separate statement listing the qualifying income for each spouse. See R.C. 5747.05(E).

### A6. Schedule of Credits forms (S5 2024, S6 2025; identical wording)

> 11. Tax less credits (line 1 minus line 10; if negative, enter zero).
> 12. Joint filing credit (see instructions for table). ____ % times line 11, up to $650

## Part B: Independent derivation (inference, not quotation)

Each point below is my reading of Part A and is labelled as inference.

1. **Filing status.** Only married filing jointly. The statute requires "a joint return filed by a husband and wife" (A1/A2); the instructions require that "you and your spouse ... jointly file your return" (A4/A5). Single, head of household, and married filing separately cannot claim it.

2. **Per-spouse income test.** The test applies to **each** spouse separately ("each of whom had"; "you and your spouse must each have"). Threshold: **at least $500**, so exactly $500 qualifies (inclusive, >= 500). The term the instructions use is **"qualifying income"**; the statute frames it as each spouse's "adjusted gross income ... exclusive of interest, dividends and distributions, royalties, rent, and capital gains."

3. **What qualifying income is.** It is measured **as included in Ohio AGI** (R.C. 5747.01(A)), attributed to each spouse:
   - **Excluded by name:** interest; dividends and distributions; capital gains; rents and royalties.
   - **Excluded because not in Ohio AGI:** anything deducted on the Ohio Schedule of Adjustments. Named examples are business income (the Kevin/Krysten example treats a spouse's business income as wholly deducted), state and local tax refunds, Social Security and railroad retirement benefits, and certain military compensation and retirement benefits. Anything excluded from federal AGI is also excluded, since Ohio AGI starts from federal AGI.
   - **Counts (inference, not named in the sources):** wages are confirmed by the worked example. Taxable pension, annuity, and IRA distributions, and taxable unemployment compensation, are in federal AGI and are not on the excluded list. So they count to the extent they remain in Ohio AGI, that is, unless an Ohio deduction removes them (e.g., the military retirement deduction).
   - **Self-employment / business income (inference, with a caveat):** the instructions say business income is "deducted on the Ohio Schedule of Adjustments" and so is not qualifying income. The Ohio business income deduction under R.C. 5747.01(A)(28) is capped, so business income above the deduction may arguably remain in Ohio AGI. Read literally, the "included in Ohio AGI" test would then count that excess. The sources do not address this case directly, and the published example treats the spouse's business income as entirely deducted.

4. **Credit amount.** Credit = min($650, pct x Schedule of Credits line 11), where line 11 is "Tax less credits" (tax after credits that precede this one under R.C. 5747.98). The cap is $650 per return. The percentage is chosen by **MAGI less exemptions**, not by qualifying income. MAGI = Ohio AGI plus the R.C. 5747.01(A)(28) business income deduction and the (A)(34) deduction. The brackets are: <= $25,000: 20%; > $25,000 to <= $50,000: 15%; > $50,000 to <= $75,000: 10%; > $75,000: 5%. The statute's "more than / not more than" wording and the booklet's whole-dollar ranges ("$25,001 - $50,000") agree for whole-dollar amounts.

5. **2024 vs 2025 difference.** Filing status, the per-spouse $500 qualifying-income test, the definition of qualifying income, the percentage table, and the $650 cap are the same in both years. **New for 2025:** the credit requires **MAGI less than $750,000** (R.C. 5747.05(E)(2), as amended by H.B. 96, "for taxable years beginning in 2025"). The 2025 booklet's top bracket reads "$75,001 - $749,999" to match. For tax years beginning in 2026 or later the statute lowers the cap to MAGI less than $500,000. Tax year 2024 has no MAGI ceiling.

## Plain-English definition (for an API field docstring)

`spouses_both_have_qualifying_income`: True when, on an Ohio married-filing-jointly return, **each** spouse separately has at least $500 of "qualifying income" for the Ohio joint filing credit (R.C. 5747.05(E); IT 1040 Schedule of Credits line 12, tax years 2024 and 2025). Qualifying income is that spouse's income as included in Ohio adjusted gross income, other than interest, dividends and distributions, capital gains, and rents and royalties. Amounts deducted on the Ohio Schedule of Adjustments are not in Ohio AGI and so do not count. These include business/self-employment income covered by the business income deduction, Social Security and railroad retirement benefits, state and local tax refunds, and certain military pay and retirement benefits. Wages count, as do other Ohio-AGI items such as taxable pensions and unemployment compensation. The flag is meaningless for other filing statuses, which cannot claim the credit. It does not cover the separate MAGI requirements: MAGI less exemptions selects the 20/15/10/5% rate, the credit is capped at $650, and for 2025 MAGI must be under $750,000.
