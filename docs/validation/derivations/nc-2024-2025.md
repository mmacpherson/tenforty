# North Carolina individual income tax, tax years 2024 and 2025: independent derivation

## Header

- **Scope:** NC Form D-400, Lines 6 to 15, for full-year NC residents, starting from a
  stipulated federal AGI. Filing statuses Single, MFJ and HoH; NC standard deduction
  (Line 11); child deduction (Lines 10a/10b) where qualifying children are stated; no
  Schedule S additions or deductions (Lines 7 and 9 = 0); no credits (Line 16 and below
  are out of scope). All filers are under 65 and not blind.
- **Method:** blind, source-only. No tenforty code, tests, fixtures, graphs or output were
  read or run, and no tax software or library was used. Every figure comes from the
  official sources below. Arithmetic was done by hand line by line, then re-checked as
  plain decimal multiplication (Python `Decimal` used only as a calculator, with no tax

  subagent, 2026-11-03.

## Parameters

### Form lines (identical structure in F24 and F25)

- L6 Federal AGI
- L7 Additions (Schedule S Part A); 0 in every case here
- L8 = L6 + L7
- L9 Deductions (Schedule S Part B); 0 in every case here
- L10a number of qualifying children; L10b child deduction = L10a × per-child amount
- L11 NC standard deduction
- L12a = L9 + L10b + L11
- L12b = L8 − L12a
- L13 part-year percentage; not used (full-year residents)
- L14 NC taxable income = L12b for full-year residents (may be negative; I24/I25 p.14)
- L15 NC income tax = L14 × rate; if L14 is zero or less, L15 = 0

### Tax rate (F24/F25 Line 15; I24/I25 p.7 and p.14; G7)

| 2025 | 4.25% (0.0425) |

## Rounding conventions (with source)

1. **Whole dollars on every line.** I24/I25 p.4 (general instructions for completing the
   return): "Round off to the nearest whole dollar. Drop amounts under 50 cents and
   increase amounts from 50 cents to 99 cents to the next whole dollar." F24/F25 page 1
   also says "Enter Whole U.S. Dollars Only" above Line 6. **Round half up**, so $0.50
   goes up.
2. **What gets rounded.** Lines 6–14 are whole dollars already: whole-dollar inputs, and
   deductions that are whole-dollar amounts from the table and chart. The only line that
   produces cents is **Line 15 (the tax)**. It is computed from the whole-dollar taxable
   income on Line 14, then rounded half up to a whole dollar. Taxable income itself is
   never rounded to a bracket or table interval, because NC has no tax table for these years.
3. **Floor.** Line 14 may be negative. It is entered with the negative indicator, not
   floored (I24/I25 p.14, Line 14 "Important"). Line 15 is floored at zero: "If North
   Carolina taxable income is zero or less, enter a zero on Line 15" (I24/I25 p.14;
   F24/F25 Line 15 text).
4. Consequence: several 2024 cases produce exactly $0.50 before rounding, and those
   round up. For example, 34,500 × 0.045 = 1,552.50, which rounds to 1,553. Under
   half-up rounding, any fractional part of .50 or more rounds up and anything below
   .50 rounds down.

## Assumptions

- **A1.** Federal AGI is stipulated and equals the sum of the stated income items. No
  above-the-line adjustments. All amounts are whole dollars.
- **A2.** Every taxpayer (and spouse) is a full-year NC resident. Line 13 does not apply
  and L14 = L12b.
- **A3.** Every taxpayer is eligible for the federal standard deduction: not a dependent
  with a limited standard deduction, not MFS with an itemizing spouse, not a dual-status
  alien. Each claims the NC standard deduction rather than NC itemized deductions. Under
  65 and not blind, which makes no difference for NC either way.
- **A4.** No Schedule S additions or deductions. In particular, the interest in Case 21
  is not interest on U.S. obligations (which would be a Schedule S Part B deduction).
  None of the income is Social Security, Bailey-exempt retirement income or military pay.
- **A5.** "Wages" for MFJ means combined wages of both spouses. NC MFJ uses the joint
  federal AGI.
- **A6.** For every case with qualifying children, each child is a qualifying child under
  IRC § 24 (under 17 at year end, with an SSN), and the taxpayer was "allowed a federal
  child tax credit" for that child. At these AGIs the federal CTC phase-out (which starts
  at $400,000 MFJ / $200,000 others) is not reached. In the lowest boundary case (MFJ
  AGI 40,000) the credit is allowed partly as nonrefundable CTC and partly as refundable
  ACTC. I treat that as "allowed a federal child tax credit" (see U2).
- **A7.** No tax credits (Line 16) and no consumer use tax. The derivation stops at
  Line 15.
- **A8.** Tax year 2025 uses 4.25% as printed on the 2025 D-400 and D-401, and as
  enacted in G.S. 105-153.7(a). The (a1) rate-reduction trigger first applies to taxable
  years beginning in 2027, so it does not affect 2024 or 2025.

## Ambiguities

- **U1. Rounding of Line 15.** No Line 15 instruction says "round". The rounding rule
  comes from the general instruction on I24/I25 p.4 ("Round off to the nearest whole
  dollar. Drop amounts under 50 cents and increase amounts from 50 cents to 99 cents") and
  from the form's "Enter Whole U.S. Dollars Only". I read these together as: compute
  L14 × rate exactly, then round half up to whole dollars. The alternative readings are
  truncation, or keeping cents. Under them the .50 cases in 2024 (Cases 5, 6, 7, 9, 10,
  12, 14, 17, 19) would be $1 lower, and any case with fractional cents would differ by
  under $1. Precision tolerance: ±$0 under the half-up reading. A test that has to cover
  the alternative readings needs ±$1.
- **U2. "Allowed a federal child tax credit."** The instructions (p.13 Line 10a) and
  G.S. 105-153.5(a1) key the deduction to the federal credit being *allowed*. It is
  unclear whether a child for whom only the refundable ACTC (not the nonrefundable CTC)
  is received counts. NCDOR's worksheet line 3 says "for whom a federal tax credit was
  claimed". I assume any § 24 credit counts, whether CTC or ACTC. None of the cases here
  has zero federal tax liability together with children, so no result depends on this
  except possibly Case 10.
- **U3. Band edges.** The table wording is "Up to $X" and "Over $X – Up to $Y". I read
  "Up to" as inclusive (≤) and "Over" as exclusive (>). This decides Cases 8, 9, 10,
  12, 14, 17 and 19, which sit exactly on an upper edge. The +$1 cases are unambiguous.
- **U4. The deduction is per child, not prorated.** The table gives a flat amount per
  child per band, with no phase-in within a band. The deduction drops by $500 per child
  at each edge (a cliff). I applied it that way, exactly as the table reads.
- **U5. The test is federal AGI, not NC-modified income.** The worksheet line 2 and the
  statute ("adjusted gross income, as calculated under the Code") both use federal AGI
  (Line 6), not Line 8. This makes no difference here because L7 = 0.
- **U6. The law could change after publication.** The 2025 parameters are from the
  2025 D-401 (PDF modified 2025-12-01) and the current statute text retrieved
  2026-11-03. I found no retroactive 2025 change in what I read, but I did not search
  2025–2026 session laws separately.
