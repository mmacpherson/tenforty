## 3. Rules as applied

### 3.1 Page 1 (taxable income)

| Line | 2024 (MT-F24) | 2025 (MT-F25) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Federal AGI, Form 1040 line 11 | Federal AGI, Form 1040 line 11b |
| 2 | "Federal standard deduction or adjusted federal itemized deductions" = Form 1040 line 12 | "Add Form 1040, lines 12e and 13b" (standard deduction plus Schedule 1-A deductions, including the enhanced senior deduction) |
| 3 | Line 1 - line 2, not below 0 ("federal taxable income for Montana") | same |
| 4 | Additions, Schedule I Part I line 9 | Schedule I Part I line 7 |
| 5 | Subtractions, Schedule I Part I line 27 | Schedule I Part I line 24 |
| 6 | $5,500 if 65 or older ($11,000 MFJ both) | $5,660 ($11,320 MFJ both) |
| 7 | Line 3 + line 4 - line 5 - line 6, not below 0 | same |

Wages and long-term capital gains trigger none of the Schedule I additions or
subtractions (other-state bond interest, state tax deducted, federal bond interest,
tribal, military, MSA, 529, railroad retirement, etc.), so lines 4 and 5 are 0.

There is no Montana personal or dependent exemption on the 2024 or 2025 Form 2: the base
is federal taxable income and the form has no exemption line. Dependents therefore do
not change line 7 or line 8 (they matter only for credits, which are out of scope), so
scenarios 5 and 6 are identical.

### 3.2 Page 2 worksheet (identical wording both years except the line 5 amounts)

1. Montana taxable income (page 1 line 7). If no net LTCG, skip lines 2-10, line 11 = 0.
2. Net long-term capital gains (2024 form: "from federal Schedule D, line 15"; instructions
   both years: generally the lesser of Schedule D line 15 or line 16).
3. Lesser of line 1 or line 2.
4. Line 1 - line 3. (Montana ordinary income.)
5. Threshold: 2024 $20,500 single/MFS, $41,000 MFJ/QSS, $30,750 HOH;
   2025 $21,100, $42,200, $31,700.
6. Line 5 - line 4, not below 0. (Room left in the 3% gain bracket.)
7. Lesser of line 3 or line 6.
8. Line 7 x 3%.
9. Line 3 - line 6, not below 0.
10. Line 9 x 4.1%.
11. Line 8 + line 10 = net long-term capital gains tax.
12. Ordinary income tax on line 4 (or line 1 if no gain) from the Ordinary Income Tax Table.
13. Residents: line 11 + line 12 -> page 1 line 8.

Ordinary Income Tax Table (MT-I24 p. 14; MT-I25 p. 12), "taxable income without
long-term capital gains":

| Status | Year | Below threshold | At or above threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single / MFS | 2024 | under $20,500: 4.7%, less $0 | 5.9%, less $246 |
| MFJ / QSS | 2024 | under $41,000: 4.7%, less $0 | 5.9%, less $492 |
| HOH | 2024 | under $30,750: 4.7%, less $0 | 5.9%, less $369 |
| Single / MFS | 2025 | under $21,100: 4.7%, less $0 | 5.9%, less $253 |
| MFJ / QSS | 2025 | under $42,200: 4.7%, less $0 | 5.9%, less $506 |
| HOH | 2025 | under $31,700: 4.7%, less $0 | 5.9%, less $380 |

Check of the "Less" amounts against 1.2% x threshold: 2024 gives exactly 246 / 492 / 369.
2025 gives 253.20 / 506.40 / 380.40, which the booklet prints as 253 / 506 / 380. So in
2025 the booklet table yields up to $0.40 more tax than the marginal-rate statement on
MT-W25. The primary values follow the booklet table, because that is what line 12 says
to use.

### 3.3 Rounding

Neither booklet nor form states a dollar-rounding rule (the only "round" instructions are
six-decimal ratios on Schedule II and the credit schedules). Every money line on the form
has `00` printed in the cents position, so entries are whole dollars. The booklets' own
example rounds to the nearest dollar in 2024 ($2,704 x 0.20 = 540.80 -> $541). Convention
used here: round each computed worksheet entry (lines 8, 10, 12) to the nearest whole
dollar, half up, then add. All inputs here are whole dollars, so page 1 needs no rounding.

## 4. Published worked examples (bonus anchors)

- **2024** (MT-I24 booklet p. 12, "Example: Marla"): single, Montana taxable income
  $50,000, no net LTCG -> "Her Montana Ordinary Income Tax is $2,704."
  Check: 50,000 x 5.9% = 2,950; 2,950 - 246 = 2,704.
- **2025** (MT-I25 booklet p. 13, same example): single, $50,000 -> "$2,697."
  Check: 50,000 x 5.9% = 2,950; 2,950 - 253 = 2,697. (The marginal form gives 2,696.80,
  which also rounds to 2,697.)

These are resident-tax figures computed "as if she were a resident" before the
nonresident ratio. No published worked example with a net long-term capital gain was
found in either booklet.

## 6. Uncertainties

1. **No stated rounding rule.** Neither year's form or booklet says how to round to whole
   dollars; the convention in 3.3 is inferred from the printed `00` cents and the 2024
   example. It changes the answer in one case: **2025 scenario 11, 2,475 (per-line) vs
   2,474 (round the total only)**. Two more sit exactly on a half dollar and depend on
   half-up: 2025 scenario 9 (277.50 -> 278) and 2025 scenarios 5/6 (3,535.50 -> 3,536).
   A tolerance of +/- $1 on line 13 covers every case; the "exact" column gives the
   unrounded figure. All 2024 values are the same under every convention tried.
2. **2025 "Less" amounts are rounded in the booklet.** The table's $253 / $506 / $380 are
   1.2% x threshold (253.20 / 506.40 / 380.40) rounded down, so the table gives $0.20-$0.40
   more than the marginal rates stated on the DOR web page. It changes the whole-dollar
   answer in one case: **2025 scenarios 5/6, 3,536 (booklet table) vs 3,535 (marginal
   rates)**. The primary value follows the table, which line 12 names; the statute was
   not consulted.
3. **The 2025 booklet's own example is internally inconsistent on rounding**: it prints
   $2,697 x 0.20 = $540, where the product is 539.40 (the 2024 text has 2,704 x 0.20 =
   540.80 -> $541). This looks like an incompletely updated example. It affects only the
   nonresident ratio step, not any value here, but it weakens "nearest dollar" as an
   inference from the booklet.
4. **2025 instructions vintage.** The 2025 booklet PDF is dated 2025-09-16, earlier than
   the 2025 form (`2025v3 12/2025`). The form and booklet agree on every line and amount
   used here (line 2 = 1040 lines 12e + 13b, $5,660, thresholds), but a later booklet
   revision was not found or checked.
5. **Scenario 8, 2025** depends on reading "Add Form 1040, lines 12e and 13b" as bringing
   the federal $6,000 enhanced senior deduction into Montana's base in addition to
   Montana's own $5,660 line 6. Both the form line and booklet p. 7 say so explicitly, so
   this is a low-risk reading, but it is the one place the result hinges on a 2025-only
   federal change. Without the $6,000, line 7 would be 26,590 and the tax 1,316 (26,590 x
   0.059 - 253 = 1,315.81). Assumes age 67 at the end of each tax year, not blind, valid
   SSN, MAGI = 50,000.
6. **Scenario 9 does not behave as its description suggests.** The gain is entirely in
   the 3% bracket in both years (nothing reaches 4.1%), and in 2025 only 9,250 of the
   10,000 gain is taxable because the worksheet caps the gain at taxable income.
7. **Net LTCG definition.** The scenarios stipulate "net long-term capital gains"; this
   is taken as worksheet line 2 directly (Schedule D line 15, with line 16 not smaller,
   i.e. no short-term loss). No qualified dividends are assumed; Montana treats those as
   ordinary income.
8. **Scope.** Values stop at page 1 line 8. Montana EITC, the federal child tax credit
   and any other credits are not computed; scenarios 6 and 7 would have credits that do
   not affect line 8.
