PDFs only. Nothing in the tenforty repository was read except
`docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`; no tax software or calculator was run.
A short Python loop re-added the hand arithmetic below (plain arithmetic on the
published parameters, no tax library).

"State tax" below is Form 740 line 22 (income tax after personal tax credits and
the family size tax credit). With lines 23-25 zero it also equals line 26, Income
Tax Liability, before use tax and withholding.

### Rules used

- **Family size** (packet p.26, Schedule ITC Section C instructions): size 1 is a
  single individual; size 2 is an individual with one qualifying child or a
  married couple; size 3 is a married couple with one qualifying child; size 4
  is a married couple with two or more qualifying children. Maximum is four
  (packet p.11).
- **Modified gross income** (worksheet, packet p.26): the greater of (e) federal
  AGI + non-Kentucky municipal bond interest + lump-sum distributions not in
  federal AGI, or (i) Kentucky AGI + lump-sum distributions. With no additions
  or subtractions, MGI = federal AGI = Kentucky AGI in every scenario here.
- **Filing statuses for married couples** (packet p.13; Form 740 page 1):
  - Status 2, "Married, filing separately on this combined return": one form,
    spouse's figures in Column A and the taxpayer's in Column B; each column
    gets its own standard deduction ("enter $[amount] in both Columns A and
    B"), its own 4% computation and its own credits, and line 19 adds the two
    line 18 amounts. Available only "if both you and your spouse had income".
  - Status 3, "Married, filing joint return": "you and your spouse add your
    incomes together and report in Column B"; "even if one spouse had no
    income"; "only one $[amount] standard deduction is allowed".
  - With a single earner, status 2 is not available (the form says "If both
    had income"), so scenarios 5-7 are status 3, Column B only, one standard
    deduction. Under a flat rate the only difference between statuses 2 and 3
    is the second standard deduction (worth 4% × the deduction: $126.40 in
    2024, $130.80 in 2025), which a non-earning spouse cannot use.
- **Additions and subtractions** (Schedule M, M24/M25): additions are other
  states' bond interest, K-1 resident adjustments, federal depreciation,
  federal NOL, other. Subtractions are state refund, U.S. obligation interest,
  retirement income exclusion, taxable Social Security, K-1 adjustments,
  Kentucky depreciation, active-duty military pay, other, Kentucky NOL. None
  applies to wages or to an ordinary long-term capital gain. Schedule M's
  "other subtractions" list names capital gains only for property taken by
  eminent domain; the packet's differences chart names only Kentucky Turnpike
  bond gains. Lines 6 and 8 are therefore 0 in all scenarios.
- **Capital gains**: Kentucky taxes line 11 at one flat rate; there is no
  separate capital gain rate or general exclusion on Form 740 or Schedule M.
- **Federal AGI**: Form 1040 line 1z wages + line 7 (2024) / 7a (2025) capital
  gain = line 9 total income; line 10 adjustments are 0; line 11 (2024) / 11a
  (2025) AGI. The full long-term gain is in AGI (preferential rates affect
  federal tax, not AGI). The $10,000 gain is stipulated as the net Schedule D
  amount.
- **Rounding**: every money line on Form 740, Schedule ITC and Schedule M has
  "00" preprinted in the cents position, so entries are whole dollars. The
  packets, forms, schedules and the KY-1345 handbooks contain no rounding
  instruction at all (text search for "round", "nearest", "cents"). All inputs
  here are whole dollars, so the only line where cents arise is line 12
  (and line 21 where the credit is 100%, giving back the whole line 19). The
  whole-dollar figures use round-to-nearest; no product lands on .50.

### Scenario notes

- **S1** (family size credit): single, family size 1. MGI 15,000 is not over
  the 100% threshold in either year (15,060 for 2024, 15,650 for 2025), so the
  credit is 100% of line 19 and line 22 is 0. The 2024 margin is only $60: a
  single filer with MGI 15,061 would fall to 90%.
- **S5**: joint, family size 2 (married couple). MGI 100,000 is over 27,185
  (2024) and 28,130 (2025): 0%.
- **S6**: joint, family size 4. MGI 100,000 is over 41,496 (2024) and 42,760
  (2025): 0%. Kentucky has no dependent exemption or dependent credit on Form
  740 (personal credits are only age 65, blind, National Guard), so S6 equals
  S5. The federal child tax credit does not enter Form 740. No child care
  expenses are stipulated, so line 24 is 0.
- **S7**: joint, family size 4. MGI 30,000 is not over 31,200 (2024) or 32,150
  (2025): 100%, line 22 is 0.
- **S8**: Schedule ITC Section B line 1 = 40, line 4 = 40, line 9 = 40 to Form
  740 line 17. Wages are not retirement income, so no pension exclusion
  (Schedule M line 9) applies. The federal additional standard deduction and
  the 2025 federal senior deduction are deductions from federal taxable income,
  not from AGI, so line 5 is unaffected. Family size 1, MGI 50,000: 0%.
- **S9**: the gain is in federal AGI and taxed at the same 4%; no Schedule M
  modification.

## Uncertainties

1. **Rounding rule not published.** The forms force whole-dollar entries
   (preprinted "00"), but no Kentucky document retrieved says whether to round
   to nearest or drop cents. Round-to-nearest gives the whole-dollar column.
   Dropping cents would change every nonzero 2024 result by −1 (all 2024
   products end in .60: 1,473; 3,273; 5,873; 3,873; 3,873; 1,833; 2,673) and
   leave 2025 unchanged (all end in .20). The exact cents column is the
   rule-independent value; a test tolerance of $1 for 2024, or a comparison
   against the exact figure, avoids depending on the unverified rule. No
   authority (statute, regulation) on rounding was searched beyond the forms,
   packets and e-file handbooks.
2. **S1 and S7 both land in the 100% row**, so they exercise only the top of the
   family size table. The intermediate rows are transcribed above and are
   confirmed only by the packet's four percentage examples, not by a derived
   return here.
3. **Family size credit rounding** for intermediate percentages (line 19 ×
   .90 etc.) is likewise unspecified; it does not arise in these scenarios.
4. **Filing status for the one-earner couple**: I read "If both had income" on
   the status 2 line and "if both you and your spouse had income" in the
   instructions as excluding status 2 for a one-earner couple. Even if it were
   allowed, the non-earning spouse's column would be zero income with an
   unusable deduction, so tax would be the same.
5. **Scenario assumptions supplied by the deriver, not by a source**: taxpayers
   are full-year residents, not blind, not National Guard, spouse in S5-S7 under
   65 and living in the same household, the two children are qualifying
   children under IRC 152(c), the scenario-9 gain is a net long-term gain with
   no Kentucky basis difference, and no one is claimed as a dependent elsewhere.
6. **2025 federal AGI**: taken as wages plus gain with no adjustments. The 2025
   packet states Kentucky's IRC conformity date is December 31, 2024; no 2025
   federal change alters AGI for wage or capital-gain income, so line 5 is not
   affected, but I did not review the 2025 Form 1040 instructions beyond the
   form face.
7. Source PDFs were read through `pdftotext`; table figures were transcribed
   from the extracted text, not from a visual read of the page. The four
   published percentage examples agree with the transcribed rows.
