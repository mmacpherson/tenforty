## Which method is "required": schedule or table

Nebraska does not switch method by income. The rule is by filing method (Form 1040N line 15 and its instruction, both years): "Paper filers may use the Nebraska Tax Table. Electronic filers must use the Nebraska Tax Calculation Schedule." (form wording: "All others must use Tax Calculation Schedule.")

- **Tax Calculation Schedule**: mandatory for e-filed returns at every income, and permitted for paper returns. It is the only method valid for every filer, so it is the **primary expected value** below.
- **Tax Table**: optional, paper filers only. It "calculates tax to the midpoint of the bracket" ($100-wide rows), so it can differ from the schedule by a few dollars. The table ends at $75,360 (2024) / $77,760 (2025); above that the table page prints an "Over $75,360 / $77,760" worksheet, whose base amount is already rounded to whole dollars and can differ from the schedule by $1.

Both are reported for every case. Where they differ, both are legally valid line 15 entries for a paper filer; only the schedule value is valid for an e-filer.

### Rounding rule

N24-B p.4 and N25-B p.6: "Enter All Amounts as Whole Dollars. Do not include cents on the return or schedules. ... Round any amount from 50 cents to 99 cents to the next higher dollar. Round any amount less than 50 cents to the next lower dollar." Applied: the schedule result is computed in cents and rounded half-up to whole dollars once, at line 15. All inputs here are whole dollars, so no other line needs rounding. Tolerance for the schedule method: exact whole dollars (0).

## Common treatment in every scenario

- Federal AGI (1040N line 5) = Federal Form 1040 line 11 (2024) / line 11a (2025). Wages go on 1040 line 1z; the long-term capital gain in scenario 9 goes on line 7 (2024) / 7a (2025); no adjustments to income (line 10 = 0). So AGI = wages (+ capital gain in scenario 9). The federal preferential rate on long-term gains affects federal tax only, not AGI.
- Nebraska adjustments (lines 12 and 13, Schedule I): 0. Wages have no Schedule I adjustment. The only Nebraska capital gains item is the "Special Capital Gains/Extraordinary Dividend Deduction" (Schedule I line 16 for 2024, line 19 for 2025; Form 4797N), an elective deduction for qualifying employer stock; the scenario stipulates an ordinary long-term gain, so it does not apply and the gain is taxed at ordinary Nebraska rates.
- Line 10 = line 6 (standard deduction; no federal itemizing). Line 11 = line 5 - line 10. Line 14 = line 11.
- Line 16 (other tax: 29.6% of federal lump-sum / early-distribution tax) = 0. Line 17 = line 15.
- Dependents are assumed to qualify for the federal child tax credit or credit for other dependents (required for line 4c); nobody can be claimed as a dependent by another taxpayer (line 2b unchecked).
- "Net" = line 17 - line 18. This is before the Nebraska child tax / child care / earned income credits and before withholding, as the task specifies.
- **High-AGI additional tax / recapture (scenario 10): does not apply.** Neither year's Form 1040N, Tax Calculation Schedule, tax table worksheet nor booklet contains an additional-tax or bracket-recapture line or schedule; a text search of both booklets for "additional tax" finds only an unrelated amended-return payment line in 2025. Line 15 for AGI 400,000 is the ordinary schedule amount.

## Uncertainties

1. **Two legal line 15 answers for paper filers.** The task asked which method is "required at that income"; Nebraska's rule turns on filing method, not income. The schedule is mandatory for e-filers and always allowed, so it is primary. The table / over-table worksheet differs in 9 of 20 cases: 2024 scenarios 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 2025 scenarios 2 and 7 (by $1 to $2). A test pinned to the schedule value should not treat the paper-table value as wrong law; a precision tolerance derived from the published rules would be $0 against the schedule, or the specific alternative value for the table.
2. **Rounding point for the schedule.** The booklet says to enter whole dollars and round half-up; it does not say separately whether the schedule result is rounded at line 15 or carried in cents. I rounded once at line 15, since the form has pre-printed "00" cents on every line. No case here sits within a cent of a .50 boundary (closest: 2024 scenario 7 at 1,754.945 and 2024 scenario 1 at 367.965, both clearly above .50), so the result does not depend on intermediate rounding of the product.
3. **Dependent qualification is assumed.** Line 4c counts only dependents who qualify for the federal child tax credit or credit for other dependents. The scenarios say "dependent children" / "dependents"; I assumed all qualify. If a dependent did not, line 18 falls by $166 / $171 each.
4. **"Before other credits" excludes credits the scenarios would otherwise attract.** Net tax here is line 17 - line 18 only. The 1040N has other credits these facts could trigger on a real return (e.g. the Nebraska child care credits and the refundable Nebraska earned income credit; scenario 7 may or may not qualify federally). None was computed, per the task.
