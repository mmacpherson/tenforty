- Retrieved: 2026-11-03

## Parameters

### 1. Standard deduction (line 17, before phase-out)

| Status | TY2024 | TY2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Single | 14,600 | 15,000 |
| MFJ | 29,200 | 30,000 |
| MFS | 14,600 | 15,000 |
| HoH | 21,900 | 22,500 |
| QSS | 29,200 | 30,000 |

**TY2024.** The 2024 deduction is the federal standard deduction.
- S5124C sub-§1-A: "For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2020 and before January 1, 2025, the standard deduction of a resident individual is equal to the federal standard deduction, subject to the phase-out under subsection 2."
- R24: "The Maine standard deduction amount is equal to the federal standard deduction amount." It also prints "Standard Deduction: Single - $14,600 Married Filing Jointly - $29,200 Head of Household - $21,900 Married Filing Separately - $14,600."
- I24 p.10 Quick Facts gives the same base amounts. I24 p.4, line 17: "enter the amount from federal Form 1040, line 12."
- Conformity for TY2024: L24 p.3 says the Code reference was updated to "as amended through December 31, 2023. Applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2023" (P.L. 2023, c. 619). L25 p.3 then updated it to "December 31, 2024. Applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024" (P.L. 2025, c. 432, effective July 1, 2025). Neither change alters the 2024 federal basic standard deduction, so the amounts above hold.

**TY2025: the pre-OBBBA amounts, not 15,750 / 31,500 / 23,625.**
- I25 PDF p.1, Important Update: "Under current law, Maine conforms to the Internal Revenue Code (the "Code") as of December 31, 2024 ... Federal Public Law 119-21, the federal One Big Beautiful Bill Act ("OBBBA"), enacted on July 4, 2025, made several federal law changes, some of which would require changes to Maine income tax law".
- I25 p.5, Maine Standard Deduction Chart for line 17 (no boxes checked): Single $15,000; MFJ or QSS $30,000; MFS $15,000; HoH $22,500.
- I25 p.11 Quick Facts: "Maine standard deduction base amounts: $15,000 single or married filing separately; $22,500 head of household; $30,000 married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse."
- R25, prepared Feb 3, 2026: "Standard Deduction: Single - $15,000 Married Filing Jointly - $30,000 Head of Household - $22,500 Married Filing Separately - $15,000."
- Statute as in force now (S5124C sub-§1-B, as amended by P.L. 2025, c. 650, Pt. K, §13 and c. 752, §1): "For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025 and before January 1, 2026, the standard deduction of a resident individual is equal to the sum of the basic standard deduction and the additional standard deduction, subject to the phase-out under subsection 2. A. The basic standard deduction is: (1) For single individuals and married persons filing separate returns, $15,000; (2) For individuals filing as heads of households, the amount allowed under subparagraph (1) multiplied by 1.5; and (3) For individuals filing married joint returns or surviving spouses, the amount allowed under subparagraph (1) multiplied by 2."
- S111 sub-§1-A now reads: "'Code' means the United States Internal Revenue Code of 1986 and amendments to that Code as of December 31, 2025" (P.L. 2025, c. 650, Pt. K, §1). L26 p.2: this "Generally applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025 ... Effective July 29, 2026."
- So the IRC conformity date for TY2025 moved from 12/31/2024 (the law under which the forms were printed) to 12/31/2025 (since July 29, 2026). That would ordinarily pull in OBBBA's federal amounts. The same 2026 session, however, wrote the 2025 Maine basic standard deduction into statute as a fixed $15,000 / $22,500 / $30,000 (L26 p.4). Under either conformity date the TY2025 amount is therefore 15,000 / 30,000 / 15,000 / 22,500 / 30,000.

## Rounding

1. Whole dollars on the form. I24 p.3 and I25 p.4: "Use whole dollar amounts. Round down to the next lower dollar any amount less than 50 cents. Round up to the next higher dollar any amount 50 cents or more." Line 17, line 18 and worksheet line 7 are therefore taken as whole dollars, half-up.
2. Worksheet line 5 (both worksheets, both years). The instructions say only "Divide line 3 by line 4. If one or more, enter 1.0000." The entry field has exactly four decimal places (`___ . ___ ___ ___ ___`). **No instruction says whether to round or truncate to four places.**
   - Primary reading here: round half-up to four decimals.
   - Each case where the choice changes the result also shows (i) truncation to four decimals and (ii) no rounding of line 5 or line 7 (the statutory pure fraction; S5124-C(2) and S5126-A(2) specify no rounding).
   - Case (d) and case (f) were chosen so that round ≠ truncate ≠ unrounded. Where the base is $10,000 (2024 MFJ exemption), rounding the ratio to four decimals is equivalent to rounding line 7 to whole dollars.
3. Tax table (T24/T25; booklet portion in I24/I25 covers TI < 52,000).
   - Line 20 instruction (F24/F25): "Find the tax for the amount on line 19 in the tax table in this booklet or compute your tax using the tax table or tax rate schedules available at maine.gov/revenue/tax-return-forms." Either method is allowed; neither is mandated below any threshold.
   - The table has $100 brackets up to 100,000 (the 2024 table has 0-50 and 50-100 first rows; the 2025 full table has 0-100).
   - I checked every transcribed row from 100 to 99,900 (999 rows × 3 columns × 2 years): each equals the **printed** rate schedule evaluated at the bracket midpoint, rounded half-up to whole dollars. There were 0 mismatches against the printed schedule and 1,240 against the pure marginal integral.
   - For TI ≥ 100,000 the table prints, for Single/MFS and HoH: "6,653 [2024 single] / 6,638 [2025 single] plus 7.15% of excess over 100,000" and "6,406 [2024 HoH] / 6,384 [2025 HoH] plus 7.15% of excess over 100,000". For MFJ/QSS it prints "See the tax rate schedule below."
   - The "100,000 and over" formula equals the schedule tax at TI − 50, so it is about $3.58 below the rate schedule for every TI ≥ 100,000 (Single/HoH). Both are official, and the form permits either. Neither the table nor the instructions say how to round that formula; the general whole-dollar rule (half-up) is applied to the line 20 entry.
