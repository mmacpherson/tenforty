after exemption credits (line 7 − line 11; line 6, 9 and 10 are zero here), i.e.
Iowa income tax before other credits, surtax and withholding. Whole-dollar
figures follow Iowa's optional rounding rule (every line rounded, half up); the
cent-exact figures are what the Department computes on adjustment.

## Rules as published

**Starting point.** IA 1040 line 2 = federal taxable income, federal Form 1040
line 15. Line 3 = net Iowa modifications (Schedule 1, line 21). Line 4 = line 2 +
line 3 (IA24-F, IA25-F p.2). If federal taxable income is zero, line 2 is instead
federal AGI less lines 1d and 1e (and, for 2025, federal line 13b) and "can be
negative" (IA24-I p.9; IA25-I p.9).

**Iowa modifications.** None apply: wages only, no Iowa-exempt income, no
retirement income, no 529 or health-insurance deduction. For scenario 9 the Iowa
capital gain deduction (Schedule 1 line 16) requires one of the IA 100 forms
(livestock, farm/non-farm business real property, timber, business sale, ESOP,
retired farmer); a generic long-term gain (assumed here: publicly traded
securities) does not qualify, so the gain is fully taxed at ordinary Iowa rates
and line 3 = 0.

**2024 rates (IA24-W).** No tax table is published for 2024; the form says
"Iowa tax from tax rate schedule" and the instructions require the Tax
Calculation Worksheet (41-026) at every income level.

Tax = (line 4 − Col. A) × Col. B + Col. C. Note head of household uses the
non-joint schedule.

**2025 rate.** Flat 3.8% of line 4, all statuses (IA25-F line 5; IA25-I pp.3, 12).
No table and no schedule.

**Exemption credits, both years (Step 3; IA24-I p.8, IA25-I p.8).** Personal
credit $40 × 1 (single, MFS, QSS) or × 2 (MFJ **and head of household**);
$20 for each taxpayer 65 or older and/or blind; $40 per dependent. Total goes to
line 8 and is subtracted from line 7; line 12 is floored at zero (IA24-I p.18).

**Low-income exemption (p.10 both years).** Single: exempt if Iowa taxable
income, with the listed add-backs (standard deduction up to the amount actually
used, QBI, etc.; for 2025 also the senior deduction), is $9,000 or less ($24,000
if 65 or older). MFJ/HOH/QSS: $13,500 or less ($32,000 if either spouse 65+).

**Alternate tax (statuses 2–5 only).** 2024: (line 4 + federal standard/itemized
deduction + QBI + … − $13,500 [or $32,000]) × 5.7%; use the smaller of that and
the schedule tax (IA24-A). 2025: same base × **4.3%**, compared with line 4 ×
3.8% (IA25-A lines 4–5; IA25-I p.12).

**Tax reduction (single only; 41-146).** Line 12 is limited to (line 4 +
add-backs) − $9,000 (or $24,000 if 65+).

**Rounding (p.3 both years).** Rounding to whole dollars is optional; if used,
all amounts must be rounded, dropping under 50 cents and raising 50–99 cents to
the next dollar ("$2.50 becomes $3"). The Department computes adjustments to the
cent. The worksheet examples carry cents.

## Per-scenario arithmetic — tax year 2024

All: line 3 (Iowa modifications) = 0; lines 6, 9, 10 = 0; line 7 = line 5;
line 11 = line 8.

**1. Single, wages 12,000.**
Federal AGI 12,000; standard deduction 14,600; federal line 15 = 0 (floored).
IA line 2: federal taxable income is zero, so 1c − 1d − 1e = 12,000 − 14,600 − 0
= −2,600 (IA24-I p.9). Line 4 = −2,600.
Line 5: no tax on taxable income at or below zero = 0.
Credits: 1 × 40 = 40. Line 12 = 0 − 40 → 0 (floored).
Low-income exemption check: add-back total = line 4 + standard deduction "not to
exceed the amount included in line 2". Using the deduction actually absorbed,
the total is 12,000 (= AGI), which is more than 9,000, so on the add-back reading
the taxpayer is **not** under the exemption threshold and has an Iowa filing
requirement (IA24-I p.3 filing requirement a); on the literal reading of
condition (a) ("IA 1040, line 4, is $9,000 or less") the taxpayer is exempt.
Either way the tax is 0 (see U3).
Tax reduction worksheet: line 3 = 12,000 − 9,000 = 3,000; line 4 = 0; smaller = 0.
Alternate tax: not available to single filers.
**Iowa tax = 0.**

**2. Single, wages 40,000.**
Federal taxable income 40,000 − 14,600 = 25,400. Line 4 = 25,400.
Bracket 6,210–31,050: (25,400 − 6,210) = 19,190 × .0482 = 924.958 → 924.96;
+ 273.24 = **1,198.20**. Whole-dollar line 5 = 1,198.
Credits 40. Line 12 = 1,198 − 40 = **1,158** (cents: 1,158.20).
Tax reduction: 40,000 − 9,000 = 31,000 > line 12; not binding.

**3. Single, wages 85,000.**
FTI 85,000 − 14,600 = 70,400.
Over 31,050: (70,400 − 31,050) = 39,350 × .057 = 2,242.95; + 1,470.53 =
**3,713.48**. Line 5 = 3,713. Credits 40. Line 12 = **3,673** (3,673.48).

**4. Single, wages 150,000.**
FTI 150,000 − 14,600 = 135,400.
(135,400 − 31,050) = 104,350 × .057 = 5,947.95; + 1,470.53 = **7,418.48**.
Line 5 = 7,418. Credits 40. Line 12 = **7,378** (7,378.48).

**5. MFJ, wages 100,000, one earner, no dependents.**
FTI 100,000 − 29,200 = 70,800. Joint schedule, over 62,100:
(70,800 − 62,100) = 8,700 × .057 = 495.90; + 2,941.06 = **3,436.96**.
Alternate tax: 70,800 + 29,200 = 100,000; − 13,500 = 86,500; × .057 = 4,930.50 >
3,436.96, so the schedule tax applies.
Line 5 = 3,437. Credits 2 × 40 = 80. Line 12 = **3,357** (3,356.96).
One earner vs two makes no difference on a joint Iowa return (status 2 is a
single combined computation since 2023).

**6. MFJ, wages 100,000, two dependent children.**
Line 4 and line 5 as scenario 5: 70,800; **3,436.96** → 3,437.
Credits 2 × 40 + 2 × 40 = 160. Line 12 = **3,277** (3,276.96).

**7. Head of household, wages 60,000, one dependent child.**
FTI 60,000 − 21,900 = 38,100. Non-joint schedule, over 31,050:
(38,100 − 31,050) = 7,050 × .057 = 401.85; + 1,470.53 = **1,872.38**.
Alternate tax: 38,100 + 21,900 − 13,500 = 46,500 × .057 = 2,650.50 > 1,872.38;
schedule tax applies.
Line 5 = 1,872. Credits: personal 2 × 40 = 80 (HOH enters 2) + dependent 40 = 120.
Line 12 = **1,752** (1,752.38).

**8. Single, age 67, wages 50,000.**
Standard deduction 14,600 + 1,950 = 16,550 (chart). FTI 50,000 − 16,550 = 33,450.
(33,450 − 31,050) = 2,400 × .057 = 136.80; + 1,470.53 = **1,607.33**.
Line 5 = 1,607. Credits 40 + 20 = 60. Line 12 = **1,547** (1,547.33).
Low-income / tax reduction: 33,450 + 16,550 = 50,000; − 24,000 = 26,000 > line 12;
not binding.

**9. Single, wages 60,000 + long-term capital gain 10,000.**
Federal AGI 70,000. FTI 70,000 − 14,600 = 55,400 (the federal preferential rate
changes federal tax, not federal taxable income). No Iowa capital gain
deduction (see Rules). Line 4 = 55,400.
(55,400 − 31,050) = 24,350 × .057 = 1,387.95; + 1,470.53 = **2,858.48**.
Line 5 = 2,858. Credits 40. Line 12 = **2,818** (2,818.48).

**5. MFJ, wages 100,000, no dependents.**

## Uncertainties

- **U1 — negative line 4 in scenario 1.** Iowa instructs that when federal
  taxable income is zero, line 2 is AGI less deductions and "can be negative"
  (−2,600 for 2024, −3,750 for 2025). An implementation that floors federal
  taxable income at zero would show 0. The tax is 0 either way; only the
  taxable-income figure differs. The instructions do not print "tax on a
  negative line 4 is zero"; I take it as the only sensible reading of the
  worksheet (2024 lowest bracket starts "Over $0.00") and of a 3.8% rate, and the
  tax reduction worksheet independently caps line 12 at min(3,000, line 12).
- **U2 — scenario 8, 2025 senior deduction.** The brief says "federal standard
  deduction". The $6,000 enhanced deduction for seniors is a separate federal
  line (13b), but it is not elective in any practical sense for an eligible
  67-year-old with a valid SSN and MAGI of 50,000, and it reduces federal line
  15, which is Iowa's starting point. I used 26,250. The no-senior-deduction
  variant (32,250 → 1,226 → 1,166) is given so the caller can pick deliberately;
  it is not what a correctly prepared return would show.
- **U3 — low-income exemption wording.** Page 10 lists add-backs "to determine
  if you qualify", but condition (a) for single filers then refers to "Iowa
  taxable income … IA 1040, line 4, is $9,000 or less" without repeating "with
  the additions above" (the MFS conditions do repeat it). The filing-requirement
  text (p.3) and the tax reduction worksheet both use the added-back total, so I
  read the threshold as applying to the added-back amount. This does not change
  any number here: scenario 1 has zero tax on either reading, and every other
  scenario is far above the thresholds on either reading.
- **U4 — 2025 alternate tax rate.** The 2025 worksheet (41-145, 10/17/2025) and
  the 2025 expanded instructions both print 4.3% for the alternate tax against a
  3.8% regular rate. I used 4.3% as printed. The instruction copy of the
  worksheet still says "12/31/2024" on line 2 (a carry-over typo; the standalone
  form says 12/31/2025). In scenarios 5–7 the alternate tax exceeds the regular
  tax at either 4.3% or 3.8% (the base is larger than line 4), so the result is
  not sensitive to this.
- **U5 — rounding presentation.** Whole-dollar rounding is optional. I report
  both. If rounding is used, each line is rounded (line 5 first, then credits
  subtracted), which gives line 12 values $0.50 above cent-exact for the five
  2025 rows whose tax ends in .50, and differences under $0.50 elsewhere.
- **U6 — 2024 "tax tables" wording.** The alternate-tax worksheet copy inside the
  2024 expanded instructions says "Using the tax tables"; the standalone 2024
  worksheet says "Using the tax rate schedule", and I found no 2024 Iowa tax
  table on the Department's site (form line 5 reads "Iowa tax from tax rate
  schedule"). I treated the rate schedule worksheet as required at all incomes.
  I did not exhaustively crawl the forms library, so I cannot prove that no
  table exists.
- **U7 — scenario 9 gain type.** Assumed a gain that does not qualify for any
  Iowa capital gain deduction (e.g. listed securities). A qualifying gain
  (IA 100 series) would be subtracted on Schedule 1 line 16.
- **U8 — scope.** Line 12 excludes the school district / EMS surtax (line 19,
  depends on residence), the Iowa child and dependent care, early childhood
  development and earned income credits (refundable, Step 6), and withholding.
  Scenarios 6 and 7 could have refundable Iowa credits that are outside the
  requested figure.
- **Source access.** All cited PDFs were fetched successfully on 2026-11-11. The
  Department's own online "IA 1040 Tax Calculator" was not used.
