# PA-40 rental income and loss, tax years 2024 and 2025

- Scope: Pennsylvania full-year resident, Form PA-40, tax years 2024 and 2025, four rental scenarios.
- Method: blind derivation from official Pennsylvania Department of Revenue publications only. No tenforty files or tools were opened or run. The only computation is multiplication done on transcribed numbers.
- Deriver: Claude Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5).
- Retrieved: 2026-11-25 (PDFs fetched from pa.gov, the current host of revenue.pa.gov documents, and read as text).

## 1. Summary table

Line 6 = net income or loss from rents, royalties, patents, copyrights.
Line 9 = total PA taxable income. Line 11 = adjusted PA taxable income. Line 12 = PA tax liability.
Values are identical for 2024 and 2025 (same rules, same 3.07% rate).

| # | Scenario | Line 6 | Line 9 | Line 11 | Line 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single, comp 60,000, rental net income 10,000 | 10,000 | 70,000 | 70,000 | 2,149 |
| 2 | Single, comp 60,000, rental net loss 5,000 | (5,000) loss, oval filled | 60,000 | 60,000 | 1,842 |
| 3 | MFJ, comp 100,000, jointly owned rental net income 20,000 | 20,000 | 120,000 | 120,000 | 3,684 |
| 4 | MFJ, comp 100,000, primary's rental +20,000, spouse's separate rental loss 8,000 | 20,000 | 120,000 | 120,000 | 3,684 |

Each row holds for both 2024 and 2025.

## 3. Rules as applied (quoted)

Rate and Line 12 (PA-40 form, both years): "12. PA Tax Liability. Multiply Line 11 by 3.07 percent (0.0307)." There is no standard deduction or personal exemption on the PA-40. Line 10 allows only MSA, HSA, 529 and 529A contribution deductions, none of which apply here.

Line 11: "Subtract Line 10 from Line 9." Line 10 = 0.

Rounding (PA-40 IN): "On the PA-40 form and schedules, show money amounts in whole-dollars. Eliminate any amount less than $0.50 and increase any amount that is $0.50 or more to the next highest dollar." All results here are exact whole dollars, so rounding does not change any number.

Line 9 (form): "Add only the positive income amounts from Lines 1c, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8. DO NOT ADD any losses reported on Lines 4, 5, or 6." Instructions: "Add only the positive income amounts from Lines 1c through 8. Do not add, subtract, or take losses into consideration. IMPORTANT: You cannot use a loss that you report in one income class on your tax return to reduce income in any other class."

Between classes (p. 15): "Under PA law, losses may only be reported on Lines 4, 5, and 6. If entering a loss, fill in the oval next to the line. REMEMBER: You may not offset income in one PA income class with a loss in any other PA income class. You cannot carry forward or carry back gains or losses to other tax years."

Within the Line 6 class (Line 6 instructions, p. 19): "You may only offset your own net income and losses in determining the amount to report on this line. ... If you realize a total net loss, fill in the oval next to Line 6 on your PA-40." Schedule E instructions: "If property is rented or leased to realize a profit and also not operated as a business activity, losses may be offset against other rental or royalty income." Also: "Pennsylvania does not follow federal At-Risk Rules or Passive Activity Loss Rules." (Passive-loss limits therefore do not apply.) Not applicable here: the CAUTION that for a rental with no profit intent "your rental expenses are limited to your rental income, and you may not use a loss". The scenarios are stated as net rental results from one residential rental, treated as held for profit and not a business (rental of 30+ day periods is classed as rents, reported on Line 6, Schedule E Line c, "Complete the 'No' oval").

Between spouses on a joint return (p. 15): "Spouses, whether filing jointly or separately, may not use each other's expenses to reduce income or offset each other's income and losses. ... PA law does not allow spouses to offset income and losses with each other, even when both have activity in the same income class. You must follow these rules: 1. If each realizes a net profit, gain, or income, add the net income amounts together and report that total on the appropriate line. 2. If each realizes a net loss, add the net losses and report that total on the appropriate line. Fill in the oval next to that line. 3. If one spouse has a net profit, income or gain and the other spouse has a net loss, report only the net income on the appropriate line. Do not take into account the spouse's loss."

Schedule E, Line 23/25 IMPORTANT: "a taxpayer's income may not be offset by a spouse's loss and vice versa. All joint income and losses must be split equally when calculating net income (loss) for the taxpayer and spouse. ... If the taxpayer has net income and the spouse has a net loss (or vice versa), the net income amount separately calculated for the taxpayer or spouse must be entered on Line 6 of the PA-40."
Also Line a: "J (joint) ... was owned by and the net income (loss) reported is from both the taxpayer and spouse"; "IMPORTANT: One spouse may not use a loss to reduce the income of the other. Each spouse must determine his/her net income (loss) from rents, royalties, patents, and copyrights separately."

## 4. Per-scenario arithmetic (identical for 2024 and 2025)

1. Single. Line 1a/1c compensation 60,000; Line 6 = +10,000 (positive). Line 9 = 60,000 + 10,000 = 70,000. Line 10 = 0. Line 11 = 70,000. Line 12 = 70,000 x 0.0307 = 2,149.00 -> 2,149.
2. Single. Line 6 = -5,000 (loss, oval filled). Line 9 adds only positive amounts: 60,000; the loss is not added or subtracted and cannot reduce compensation (different class). Line 11 = 60,000. Line 12 = 60,000 x 0.0307 = 1,842.00 -> 1,842.
3. MFJ. Rental owned jointly (Schedule E Line a = J), 20,000 split equally: taxpayer 10,000, spouse 10,000. Both net income, so rule 1: add -> Line 6 = 20,000. Line 9 = 100,000 + 20,000 = 120,000. Line 11 = 120,000. Line 12 = 120,000 x 0.0307 = 3,684.00 -> 3,684.
4. MFJ. Taxpayer net rental income 20,000; spouse net rental loss 8,000 (separately owned property, Line a = S). Rule 3: report only the net income, spouse's loss disregarded -> Line 6 = 20,000, oval not filled. Line 9 = 100,000 + 20,000 = 120,000. Line 11 = 120,000. Line 12 = 3,684.

## 5. Uncertainties

- No substantive ambiguity found in the sources for these four scenarios; 2024 and 2025 text on every rule quoted is the same.
- Scenario 4 reading A (applied): Line 6 = 20,000, tax 3,684. Reading B (federal-style netting, rejected by the quoted rule 3 and the Schedule E IMPORTANT): Line 6 = 12,000, Line 9 = 112,000, tax 3,438 (112,000 x 0.0307 = 3,438.40 -> 3,438). Not a supported reading.
- Scenario 2 reading B (loss against wages, rejected by "cannot use a loss ... to reduce income in any other class"): Line 9 = 55,000, tax 1,689 (1,688.50 rounds to 1,689). Not a supported reading.
- Scenario 3: if the property were instead treated as wholly the primary filer's, the result is the same (income only), so the joint-ownership split does not change any figure.
- Scenario 1/3/4 assume the rental is held for profit and is not a "business activity" (Schedule C, Line 4). If it were classed as a business (average customer use under 30 days etc.), income would go on Line 4 rather than Line 6 but Line 9 and tax would be unchanged for the income scenarios; for scenario 2 a Line 4 loss would likewise not reduce wages.
- Assumed the "compensation" is entirely Line 1a taxable PA compensation with no unreimbursed employee business expenses or other adjustments.
- Printed page numbers for 2025 were not separately verified; the 2025 booklet cross-references the same pages (15 and 17) as 2024.
