# Michigan MI-1040, tax years 2024 and 2025: independent derivations

Blind derivation, 2026-11-11. Sources: Michigan Department of Treasury forms and
instruction books and IRS Form 1040 only, fetched from the web on 2026-11-11.
No tenforty code, specs, tables, tests or fixtures were read (only
`docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`, for the evidence format). No tax
software or calculator was run; the arithmetic below is by hand.

"State tax" throughout is **MI-1040 line 17** ("Tax"), before nonrefundable
credits (lines 18-19), use tax, refundable credits (including the Michigan
EITC) and withholding.

Evidence kind for every row: **Independent derivation** (deriver supplies the
scenario and arithmetic; the publications supply the rules). Michigan publishes
no tax table and no whole-return worked example for these inputs, so none of
these is a published worked example or a published table row. The parameters
($5,600 / $5,800 exemption, 4.25% rate, $20,000 Michigan Standard Deduction)
are **published instruction amounts** printed on the form face or in the book.

Scenario 7 is the one row that depends on a fact the scenario states only
loosely (see the scenario and "Uncertainties"): a single filer who has reached
67 by the end of the tax year gets a Michigan Standard Deduction against wages.
Without it the figures would be 44,400 / 1,887 (2024) and 44,200 / 1,879 (2025).

### Rules used, with locations

**Rate.** 4.25% in both years.
- 2024: F24 page 1, line 17: "Tax. Multiply line 16 by 4.25% (0.0425)". B24
  page 3: "For tax year 2024, the Michigan income tax rate is 4.25%"; B24 page 5:
  "The income tax rate for 2024 is 4.25 percent"; B24 page 10, Line 17.
- 2025: F25 page 1, line 17: "Tax. Multiply line 16 by 4.25% (0.0425)". B25
  page 3: "For tax year 2025, the Michigan income tax rate is 4.25%"; B25 page 5:
  "The income tax rate for 2025 is 4.25 percent"; B25 page 10, Line 17.

**Personal exemption (line 9a).** $5,600 per exemption for 2024 (F24 page 1 line
9a; B24 page 10, Line 9a); $5,800 for 2025 (F25 page 1 line 9a; B25 page 10,
Line 9a). Count, both years (B24/B25 page 10): "Enter the number of exemptions
for you, your spouse (if filing jointly), and your dependents. Dependents
include both qualifying children and qualifying relatives under the Internal
Revenue Code. You may claim an exemption for these dependents even if your AGI
exceeds the limits to claim federal tax credits for these dependents." There is
no income phase-out of the exemption on the form or in the instructions.

**Special exemptions (lines 9b-9e)** assumed zero: deaf/blind/disabled ($3,300
in 2024, $3,400 in 2025), qualified disabled veteran ($500), stillbirth, and the
$1,500 claimed-as-dependent amount. Age alone gives **no** exemption on line 9;
there is no age-65 exemption on the 2024 or 2025 form.

**Filing status.** B24/B25 page 10, Line 7: "If you filed your federal return as
head of household or qualifying surviving spouse, you must file your Michigan
return as single." Filing status does not change the rate or the per-exemption
amount; it matters here only for the Michigan Standard Deduction base
($20,000 single / $40,000 joint).

**Form flow (F24/F25 page 1).** Line 10 federal AGI; line 11 additions
(Schedule 1 line 9); line 12 = 10 + 11; line 13 subtractions (Schedule 1 line
31); line 14 = 12 - 13, not below 0; line 15 = line 9f; line 16 = 14 - 15, not
below 0; line 17 = line 16 x 0.0425.

**Federal AGI.** I24: wages on line 1a, total on 1z; capital gain on line 7;
line 9 total income; line 10 adjustments; line 11 AGI. I25: same, with capital
gain on line 7a and AGI on line 11a. With wages (and, in scenario 8, a
long-term capital gain reported in full on line 7 / 7a) and no adjustments,
AGI = wages + gain. The 2025 federal deductions for seniors, tips and overtime
are taken after AGI and do not change line 11a.

**Additions and subtractions.** S24/S25 list additions on lines 1-8 (out-of-state
municipal interest, deducted income taxes, MI-1040D/MI-4797 gains, other-state
losses, oil and gas expense, federal NOL, other) and subtractions on lines 10-30
(U.S. obligations, military and railroad retirement, MI-1040D/MI-4797 gains,
other-state income, Social Security and military pay, Renaissance Zone, state
refunds, 529/MET, oil and gas income, tribal, home-buyer savings, marihuana
expenses, miscellaneous, Tier 2 and Tier 3 Michigan Standard Deduction,
retirement benefits, the born-before-1946 dividend/interest/capital gains
deduction, Michigan NOL). None applies to W-2 wages of a filer under 67. None
applies to an ordinary long-term capital gain either: lines 3/12 concern the
Michigan adjustment of gains (property acquired before 1967-10-01, U.S.
obligations, out-of-state property; B24/B25 Schedule 1 line 3 instructions),
assumed not present, and line 28 is limited to taxpayers born before 1946.
Michigan has no preferential rate for capital gains: the gain is in AGI and is
taxed at 4.25%.

**Rounding.** B24 page 5 and B25 page 5, "Completing Michigan Forms": "Report all
amounts in whole dollars. Round down amounts of 49 cents or less. Round up
amounts of 50 cents or more. If cents are entered on the form, they will be
treated as whole dollar amounts." Every input here is a whole dollar, so the
only rounding is on line 17, and exact-half results round **up**.

**Tier 3 Michigan Standard Deduction (Schedule 1 line 26)**, used in scenario 7.
- 2024: S24 line 26: "Complete this line if the older of you or your spouse (if
  married) was born during the period January 1, 1953 through January 1, 1958,
  and reached age 67 on or before December 31, 2024." B24 page 17, Line 26:
  deduction "against all income types"; "You are considered to turn 67 the day
  before your 67th birthday"; "up to $20,000 for a return filed as single";
  "Exemption(s) claimed on MI-1040, lines 9a and 9d ... may reduce the amount".
  B24 page 18, Worksheet 2.
- 2025: S25 line 26: same, with the window "January 1, 1953 through January 1,
  1959" and "on or before December 31, 2025". B25 page 17, Line 26; B25 page 18,
  Worksheet 2.
- Worksheet 2 (both years, page 18): line 1 $20,000 single ($40,000 joint);
  line 2 $15,000 / $30,000 increase only with SSA-exempt-employment boxes 24C/24D
  (24G/24H); line 3 = 1 + 2; lines 4-6 military pay and military/railroad
  retirement; line 7 = 3 - 6; line 8 taxable Social Security in AGI; line 9
  "the amounts from MI-1040, lines 9a and 9d"; line 10 = 8 + 9; line 11 =
  7 - 10 (not below 0), carried to Schedule 1 line 26.

### 7. Single, age 67, wages 50,000

What age gives, per the 2024 and 2025 forms and books:

- **No extra exemption.** Line 9 has no age-based exemption; the filer claims one
  exemption on line 9a like anyone else.
- **Tier 3 Michigan Standard Deduction, Schedule 1 line 26, against all income
  including wages.** It needs the **birth date**, not just "age 67": the filer
  must be born 1953-01-01 through 1958-01-01 and have reached 67 by 2024-12-31
  (2024), or born 1953-01-01 through 1959-01-01 and have reached 67 by
  2025-12-31 (2025). A filer "age 67" at the end of the tax year (born
  1957-01-02 through 1958-01-01 for 2024; 1958-01-02 through 1959-01-01 for
  2025) qualifies. This derivation **assumes age 67 means attained by
  December 31 of the tax year**. A filer who turns 67 only after year end gets
  nothing from line 26 for that year.
- **Tier 2 treatment (line 25) instead**, worth $35,000 with no exemption
  offset, needs further facts: retired as of 2013-01-01 **and** received
  retirement benefits from employment exempt from Social Security (boxes 24C
  and 24D; Worksheet 2 lines 2b and 7b). Assumed not the case.
- **Retirement/pension subtraction (line 27, Form 4884)** needs retirement or
  pension income in AGI, the birth year, and the source of the benefits (public
  or private, SSA-exempt employment). There is none here, so line 27 is 0 and no
  election between line 26 and line 27 arises.
- **Reductions to the standard deduction** need: taxable Social Security in AGI
  (Worksheet 2 line 8), military pay, military/National Guard retirement,
  railroad retirement (lines 4-5). All zero here.
- **Line 28** (dividend/interest/capital gains deduction) is only for filers
  born before 1946: not available at 67.

## Uncertainties

1. **Scenario 7 depends on the birth date.** "Age 67" is read as attained by
   December 31 of the tax year, which puts the filer inside the Tier 3 window in
   both years. If the fixture means anything else (for example a filer who turns
   67 during the following filing season), the no-deduction alternatives apply
   (44,400 / 1,887 and 44,200 / 1,879). Also assumed: not retired as of
   2013-01-01 with SSA-exempt-employment benefits (which would give the larger
   Tier 2 amount), no Social Security, military or railroad income.
2. **Line 26 is elective in form** ("may be eligible to claim"). The derivation
   assumes the filer claims it, as nothing in the instructions makes it
   disadvantageous with wages only.
3. **Half-dollar rounding.** Six of the eighteen results land on exactly 50 cents
   (1/2025, 2/2024, 3/2025, 8/2025, 9/2025, and the scenario 7 2025
   alternative). They are rounded up under the book's stated rule ("Round up
   amounts of 50 cents or more"). An implementation using banker's rounding or
   truncation will differ by $1 on exactly these rows.
4. **Document versions.** The 2024 book was fetched from the current web URL
   (PDF modified 2025-02-19) and the 2025 book likewise (modified 2026-02-11);
   earlier or later revisions were not compared. The rate and exemption amounts
   in each book agree with the corresponding form face. The 2024 book's URL
   (`MI-1040-Instructions.pdf`) differs in name from the 2025 one
   (`MI-1040-Book.pdf`); the title page reads "2024 MICHIGAN MI-1040 Individual
   Income Tax Forms and Instructions". Page numbers cited are the printed page
   numbers, which match the PDF page index for pages 3-18.
5. **Statute not consulted.** The rate and exemption amounts come from Treasury's
   forms and instructions, not from MCL 206.30 / 206.51 or Treasury's annual
   rate and exemption notices. The 2025 rate of 4.25% is as printed on the 2025
   form and book.
6. **Scope.** Line 17 only. The Michigan EITC (relevant to scenario 6 and
   possibly 5), homestead property tax credit, use tax, city income tax and
   withholding are not derived. Dependents are assumed to be federal qualifying
   children or relatives, and nobody qualifies for a special exemption.
7. **Scenario 8.** The capital gain is assumed to need no Michigan adjustment
   (MI-1040D not required). The federal preferential rate on the gain is
   irrelevant to Michigan; only its inclusion in AGI matters.
8. **Federal AGI** is taken as wages (plus the gain) with no adjustments to
   income; this was checked against the line structure of the 2024 and 2025
   Form 1040, not against the Form 1040 instructions.
