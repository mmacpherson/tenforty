Dakota Office of State Tax Commissioner, IRS). No tenforty code, spec, table,
graph, test or fixture was read (only `docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`
for the evidence format); no tax software or calculator was run. Arithmetic was
done by hand and re-checked with exact decimal arithmetic.

"Tax" below is **Form ND-1 line 20** (tax before the line 21–23 credits and
before withholding).

## Rules as transcribed

### Form ND-1 line flow (both years; ND-2024-F, ND-2025-F)

- Line 1a federal AGI; line 1b federal taxable income (Form 1040 line 15).
- Lines 2–3 additions; 4a total additions; 4b = 1b + 4a.
- Lines 5–16 subtractions (line 6 = net long-term capital gain exclusion); 17 total subtractions.
- Line 18 ND taxable income = 4b − 17 (not less than 0); line 19 = line 18.
- Line 20 tax: "If full-year resident, enter tax on amount on line 19 from Tax Table in instructions."
- Lines 21–24 credits (incl. line 22 marriage penalty credit); line 25 net tax liability. **Not applied here** — the fixtures are line 20.

For every scenario, lines 2, 3, 5, 7–16 are 0 (wages only; no ND additions or
other subtractions apply). Line 6 is nonzero only in scenario 10.

### Federal standard deduction

| | 2024 (IRS-2024-I) | 2025 (IRS-2025-I) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Single | 14,600 | 15,750 |
| Married filing jointly | 29,200 | 31,500 |
| Head of household | 21,900 | 23,625 |
| Single, age 65+ (chart, 1 box) | 16,550 (= 14,600 + 1,950) | 17,750 (= 15,750 + 2,000) |

2025 only: Schedule 1-A Part V enhanced deduction for seniors — $6,000 less 6%
of MAGI over $75,000 (single); entered on Form 1040 line 13b and therefore
reduces line 15. ND-2025-BK p. 2 states that this flows into the ND starting
point. Dependents do not change federal taxable income in either year (the
child tax credit is a credit), so scenarios 6 and 7 are identical on ND-1 line 20.

### Table versus schedule

- ND taxable income under $100,000: the Tax Table is required for a full-year resident (line 20 instruction; table runs to the "99,950 – 100,000" row, and the 2025 table margin reads "If $100,000 or over — use the Tax Rate Schedules").
- ND taxable income of $100,000 or more: the Tax Rate Schedule is required (p. 27 heading).
- Table rows are $50 wide, "At least / But less than". Every row I checked equals the schedule tax at the row midpoint, rounded to the nearest whole dollar (checks shown per scenario; they are structural confirmation of my reading, not the source of the values).

### Rounding

- Tax Table amounts are printed in whole dollars; the table value is the tax.
- Rate-schedule tax: **neither ND booklet states a rounding rule** (text search of both booklets and both forms for "round", "whole", "cents", "nearest" found no instruction). The schedules themselves carry cents (base amounts 3,725.48, 3,828.83, etc.; e.g. 2024 single (238,200 − 47,150) × 1.95% = 3,725.475, printed 3,725.48, i.e. half-up to the cent). I therefore report the schedule tax to the cent, half-up, and also give the whole-dollar figure under the federal convention (IRS "Rounding Off to Whole Dollars": drop under 50 cents, raise 50–99 cents). See U1.
- Inputs here are all whole dollars, so no rounding arises before line 20, except line 6 in scenario 10 (10,000 × 40% = 4,000 exactly).

## Uncertainties

- **U1 — rounding of rate-schedule tax (scenarios 4 and 5, both years).** I found no rounding instruction in either ND booklet or on either form. The cent figures follow the schedules' own printed precision; the whole-dollar figures follow the federal convention. Which one ND expects on line 20 is not established by the sources I read. Do not set a tolerance from this: pick the convention from an ND source, or treat these four as accurate to within the cents/whole-dollar difference only (at most $0.50). The 2025 scenario 5 value is an exact half-cent (4,814.455); 4,814.46 assumes half-up, which matches how ND's printed base amounts were rounded (3,725.475 → 3,725.48; 3,828.825 → 3,828.83).
- **U2 — 2025 Tax Table has no row below 40,250.** Confirmed on the rendered page, not just in extracted text: the first row is "40,250 – 40,300". The 2024 table begins "0 – 38,950". For 2025 scenarios 1, 8 and 9 the tax of 0 is therefore a derivation from the 0.00% bracket (and from all four statuses being 0 in the first printed row), not a published table row. The value 0 is not in doubt; only the evidence kind is weaker.
- **U3 — 2025 scenario 9 depends on the federal senior deduction.** Federal taxable income of 26,250 assumes the filer claims the $6,000 Schedule 1-A Part V deduction (valid SSN, born before 1961-01-02, MAGI 50,000 ≤ 75,000). Without it, line 1b would be 32,250. ND tax is 0 either way.
- **Scenario 10 assumption.** "Net long-term capital gain 10,000" is taken to mean Schedule D line 15 = line 16 = 10,000 (no short-term loss, no capital gain distribution special case), none of it already subtracted on ND-1 line 7 or 16, and no qualified dividends (line 13 = 0).
- **Scenarios 6 and 7 are the same return on line 20.** Dependents enter ND-1 only through credits, which are out of scope.
- **Not checked.** Schedule ND-1SA additions were not read line by line; I relied on the ND-1 form's line list and the wages-only fact pattern to conclude additions are 0. Line 25 (after the marriage penalty and other credits) was not derived.
- **Source defects noted.** 2025 MFJ schedule prints "$ 80.975" for 80,975. The 2024 standalone table PDF and the 2024 booklet agree on every row I compared.
