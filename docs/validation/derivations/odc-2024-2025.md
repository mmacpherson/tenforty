## Common derivation steps (all scenarios)

- Form 1040 line 1a = 1z = 9 = wages; line 10 = 0; line 11 (2024) / 11a and 11b (2025) = wages.
- Standard deduction, from the margin of each Form 1040. **2024:** Single $14,600, MFJ $29,200, HOH $21,900 (line 12). **2025:** Single $15,750, MFJ $31,500, HOH $23,625 (line 12e). 2024 line 13 = 0 and line 14 = line 12. 2025 lines 13a (QBI) and 13b (Schedule 1-A) are 0, so line 14 = line 12e.
- Line 15 = line 11(b) − line 14.
- Line 16 comes from the Tax Table when line 15 < $100,000 and from the Tax Computation Worksheet when it is $100,000 or more. The 1040 instructions for line 16 say: "If your taxable income is less than $100,000, you must use the Tax Table … If your taxable income is $100,000 or more, use the Tax Computation Worksheet right after the Tax Table." (2025 i1040gi PDF p. 36; 2024 i1040gi PDF p. 33.) Tax Table columns, left to right: Single, MFJ, MFS, HOH.
- Line 17 (Schedule 2 line 3) = 0. There is no excess APTC, and AMT is 0 (Form 6251 checks below), so line 18 = line 16.
- Schedule 8812: line 1 = 1040 line 11 (2024) / 11a (2025); lines 2a–2d = 0; line 3 = AGI.
- **Credit Limit Worksheet A**: line 1 = 1040 line 18 = line 16; line 2 = 0 (no Schedule 3 lines 1, 2, 3, 4, 5b, 6d, 6f, 6l, 6m); line 3 = line 16; line 4 = 0 (Worksheet B is required only if you claim the mortgage interest, adoption, residential clean energy or DC homebuyer credit; none applies); line 5 = line 16. **So Schedule 8812 line 13 = Form 1040 line 16 in every scenario.**
- Line 20 (Schedule 3 line 8) = 0, so line 21 = line 19 and line 22 = max(0, line 18 − line 19).
- Line 23 = Schedule 2 line 21 = Schedule 2 line 11 (Form 8959 line 18 = line 7 here, since there is no SE or RRTA income).
- **ACTC (Part II-A)**: line 16a = line 12 − line 14 (stop if 0); line 16b = (line 4 count) × $1,700 (stop if 0); line 17 = min(16a, 16b); line 18a = earned income = wages (see below); line 18b = 0; line 19 = 18a − 2,500; line 20 = 15% × line 19. If line 16b < $5,100 (not a bona fide Puerto Rico resident), skip Part II-B and enter min(line 17, line 20) on line 27. **No scenario has 16b ≥ $5,100**, since at most 2 CTC children gives 16b ≤ $3,400. Part II-B is therefore never reached, and **no FICA (W-2 boxes 4/6) assumption affects any result.**
- Line 18a earned income: the Earned Income Chart (i1040s8 2025 p. 7; 2024 p. 3) routes a non-EIC filer to the Earned Income Worksheet, where line 1a = Form 1040 line 1z = wages and lines 2–6 = 0, so line 7 = wages. An EIC filer instead uses earned income from EIC Worksheet B line 4b / Step 5, which is also wages here. Either route gives 18a = wages.

## Rounding rules applied

- **Schedule 8812 line 10** round-up to the next multiple of $1,000, quoted above. It matters for S4a (4,200 → 5,000) and S4c (9,001 → 10,000); S3 (30,000) and S4b (9,000) are already multiples.
- **Whole-dollar rounding** (1040 instructions, "Rounding Off to Whole Dollars", PDF p. 23 in both years): "You can round off cents to whole dollars on your return and schedules. If you do round to whole dollars, you must round all amounts. To round, drop amounts under 50 cents and increase amounts from 50 to 99 cents to the next dollar. For example, $1.39 becomes $1 and $2.50 becomes $3. If you have to add two or more amounts to figure the amount to enter on a line, include cents when adding the amounts and round off only the total."
- Cents arise only from the TCW (S4a 2024: 38,546.50; S4b 2024: 39,894.50; S4c 2024: 39,894.82; S4c 2025: 39,227.24) and from Form 8959 line 7 (S4a: 37.80; S4c: 81.009). The summary shows both consistent returns. The ".50" results round **up** (38,546.50 → 38,547; 39,894.50 → 39,895). The S4c Form 8959 product 81.009 is shown as 81.01 on a dollars-and-cents return and as 81 on a rounded return.
- On the rounded return, L22 and L24 are formed from the already-rounded entries on lines 18, 19 and 23. The resulting figures equal the cents-kept totals rounded to the nearest dollar in every case but one. In S4a 2024, the cents-kept L24 of 38,334.30 would round to 38,334, but the rounded return gives 38,335. That is because L16 38,546.50 rounds up by 0.50 and L23 37.80 rounds up by 0.20, and the two add +0.70. Both columns are internally consistent returns; a consumer should accept exactly 38,334.30 (cents) or exactly 38,335 (every line rounded), not 38,334.
- Tax Table rows (all scenarios with L15 < 100,000) are already whole dollars.

## Assumptions and ambiguities

1. **Dependents.** Every CTC child is under 17 at year end, has a valid-for-employment SSN issued by the return due date, lived with the filer more than half the year, and meets dependency Steps 1–3. Every ODC dependent is a U.S. citizen with an SSN/ITIN/ATIN issued by the due date, is claimed as the filer's dependent, and is not counted on line 4. Examples are a 17- or 18-year-old child, or a qualifying relative such as a parent.
2. **Filing status as given.** S4 and S6 file Single although they have a dependent. We assume they do not qualify for, or do not use, HOH, for example because the ODC dependent is a qualifying relative not living with them for whom they do not maintain a household. For S7, the HOH qualifying person is assumed to be the CTC child.
3. **2025 OBBBA eligibility change.** For 2025 the filer (one spouse on a joint return) must have a valid SSN to claim the CTC/ACTC, and the other spouse needs an SSN or ITIN issued by the due date. The brief says the filers have valid SSNs, so this is satisfied. The ODC requires only that the filer have an SSN or ITIN and the dependent have an SSN/ITIN/ATIN. The ODC stays $500 per dependent in both years, not inflation-indexed per the form text. The CTC rises from $2,000 (2024) to $2,200 (2025); the ACTC cap is $1,700 in both years. No other 2025 change affects these scenarios: Schedule 1-A is 0, and Form 6251 now starts from line 14 − Schedule 1-A line 37, which yields the same AMTI here.
4. **Additional Medicare Tax beyond S3.** S4a/b/c (Single, wages > $200,000) owe Additional Medicare Tax on Form 8959, so L23 > 0 (see the flag after the summary table). Employer withholding of Additional Medicare Tax (Form 8959 Part V, flowing to 1040 line 25c) depends on per-employee wages above $200,000. For S3 it depends on how the 430,000 is split between the spouses. It affects payments, not L23/L24, so it is not computed.
5. **S4c line 12 / 13 / 14 entries.** The form stops at line 12 ("No"). We report L12 as 0 (left blank), L13 not completed, and L14 = 0. The 2024 form explicitly says "Enter -0- on lines 14 and 27". The 2025 form's line 12 says only "Stop here", but the result is the same: no CTC, ODC or ACTC.
6. **ACTC includes the ODC portion of line 12 up to the per-child cap.** By the form's arithmetic, line 16a = line 12 − line 14 includes unused ODC, and line 16b caps the refund at $1,700 per CTC child. In S5 2024 the refundable 1,417 therefore exceeds the "unused CTC" (2,000 − 1,083 = 917). This is the form as printed, followed literally. With zero CTC children (S6) line 16b = 0 and nothing is refundable.
7. **Part II-B / FICA.** No scenario has line 16b ≥ $5,100, so Part II-B is never reached. Results do not depend on W-2 boxes 4/6 or on any FICA assumption.
8. **EIC.** S5, S6 and S7 may be eligible for the EIC (1040 line 27a). It is a refundable payment: it does not change L16–L24, Schedule 8812 Part I, or Part II-A lines 16a–20/27. It would enter only Part II-B line 24 / Credit Limit Worksheet B, neither of which is reached. Not computed. Line 18a = wages whether or not the EIC is claimed.
9. **AMT.** It was checked explicitly for S3 and S4a/b/c (zero in both years). All other scenarios have AMTI (= AGI) below the AMT exemption, so AMT = 0 trivially.
10. **2024 line 15 checkbox** (opt out of ACTC) is assumed unchecked. In 2025 that line is "Reserved for future use".
11. **Form 8959 cents for S4c** (81.009): treated as 81.01 on a dollars-and-cents return and 81 on a whole-dollar return. The instructions do not address sub-cent products explicitly.
