# Alabama Form 40, tax years 2024 and 2025: independent derivations

Blind derivation, 2026-11-11. Sources: Alabama Department of Revenue and IRS
PDFs only, fetched today. Nothing in the tenforty repository was read except
`docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`; no tax software or calculator was
run. A small script was used only to pull printed rows out of the PDFs' text
layer (see Uncertainties 5).

Evidence kind for every whole-return value below: **Independent derivation**.
The Alabama tax figures at or below $100,000 of taxable income, and the federal
tax figures below $100,000, are **published table rows** used inside an
independent derivation. None is a published worked example.

## Rules used

### Alabama (identical in both booklets unless noted)

| Rule | Text relied on | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Rounding | "Round off cents to the nearest whole dollar on your return and schedules. You can drop amounts under 50 cents. Increase amounts from 50 to 99 cents to the next dollar." | AL-24-BK / AL-25-BK p. 7, "Rounding Off to Whole Dollars" |
| Line 5b wages; line 7 other income; line 8 total; line 10 AGI | Line 7 "includes rents, royalties, gains from sale of property"; capital gains go on Schedule D. No preferential rate or exclusion for ordinary long-term gains (the 2025 booklet adds an exclusion only for precious-metal bullion gains, Act 2024-447). | Booklet p. 8 (lines 5-10), Schedule D instructions; AL-25-BK p. 2 and p. 7 |
| Line 11 standard deduction | Standard Deduction chart, by filing status and line 10. Single: $3,000 at $0-$25,999, falling to $2,500 at "$35,500 and above". MFJ: $8,500 falling to $5,000 at "$35,500 and above". Head of Family: $5,200 falling to $2,500 at "$35,500 and above". Chart is the same in both years. | Booklet p. 9 |
| Line 12 federal income tax deduction | Federal Income Tax Deduction Worksheet: line 1 = "the tax as shown on line 22" of Form 1040; line 2 = net investment income tax (Form 8960 line 17); line 3 = sum; line 4 subtracts EIC (1040 line 27 / 27a), additional child tax credit (line 28), American opportunity credit (line 29), [2025 only: refundable adoption credit, line 30], and Form 2439 credits; line 6 = line 3 less line 5, not below zero. | AL-24-BK p. 31; AL-25-BK, worksheet after the tax table |
| Line 13 personal exemption | $1,500 Single; $3,000 Married filing joint; $1,500 Married filing separate; $3,000 Head of Family | Form 40 page 1, filing status boxes 1-4, both years |
| Line 14 dependent exemption | Per dependent, by page 1 line 10: 0-50,000: 1,000; 50,001-100,000: 500; over 100,000: 300 | Booklet p. 10 (line 14 instructions), both years |
| Lines 15-16 | Line 15 total deductions = lines 11 + 12 + 13 + 14; line 16 taxable income = line 10 less line 15 | Form 40 page 1 |
| Line 17 method | "You must figure your tax from the Tax Tables unless you are claiming a carryover or carryback Net Operating Loss from another year." The table ends at $100,000; "If taxable income is over $100,000, use the following worksheet": (taxable income - 100,000) x .05 + 4,958.00 for Single / MFS / Head of family, + 4,918.00 for Married filing jointly. | Booklet p. 10 (line 17), Tax Table pp. 25-30, worksheet p. 30 |

So the federal figure Alabama deducts is **Form 1040 line 22**: tax (line 16
plus Schedule 2 line 3) after all nonrefundable credits, including the
nonrefundable child tax credit on line 19. It is then increased by net
investment income tax and reduced by the listed refundable credits. In every
scenario here net investment income tax is zero (income below the Form 8960
thresholds) and each line-4 credit is zero, so line 12 equals Form 1040 line 22.

The Alabama Tax Table is identical in the 2024 and 2025 booklets (all 1,001
rows compared). The table is mandatory, so no rate-schedule figure is given for
the table cases.

Alabama has no additional standard deduction or exemption for age: scenario 8's
age changes only the federal tax.

## Uncertainties

1. **Children's ages (scenarios 6 and 7) were not given.** The headline rows
   assume children under 17 with valid SSNs. The 17-or-older variant is shown
   beside each. A fixture should state the ages.
2. **The Alabama Standard Deduction chart has a printed error** in both
   booklets: the third row of the MFJ column reads "$25,500 - $26,999" where the
   pattern requires $26,500. No scenario here falls in the phase-down band
   (26,000-35,499), so no value depends on it.
3. **Alabama wages are assumed equal to federal wages**, with no state
   retirement-contribution difference, and all wages are assumed to be ordinary
   pay. The 2025 booklet's "What's New" and line 5 instructions, as extracted,
   mention no overtime exclusion, but I did not read the Schedule W-2
   instructions for 2025 in full.
4. **Scenario 9 assumes ordinary securities gains.** Alabama taxes them in full
   on line 7. The 2025 exclusion for precious-metal bullion gains would change
   the result if the gain were of that kind.
5. **Table rows were read from the PDF text layer**, not from the rendered
   page. The extraction returned 1,001 Alabama rows per year, identical across
   years, and the federal rows match a 10%-of-midpoint check where one is easy
   (5,425 x 10% = 542.50, printed 543). I did not eyeball each row on the page.
6. **Page numbers for the 2025 booklet** are taken from its table of contents
   and page markers and match 2024 for the chart (p. 9); I did not confirm the
   page number of the 2025 Federal Income Tax Deduction Worksheet. Its text is
   quoted above.
7. **2025 IRS instructions are the 2026-02-26 revision** on irs.gov's
   prior-year path. Any later revision was not checked.
8. **Head of Family eligibility** in scenario 7 is assumed (unmarried, child
   lived in the home more than six months, Schedule HOF completed). Federal
   head of household status is assumed on the same facts.
9. Scenario 8 assumes the taxpayer is 67 at year end, has a valid SSN, is not
   blind, and has no Social Security or pension income.
