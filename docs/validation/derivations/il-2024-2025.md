# Illinois IL-1040 resident: independent derivation, tax years 2024 and 2025

## Header

- **Scope:** Illinois resident individual income tax (Form IL-1040) for tax years 2024 and 2025.
  The derivation starts from a stipulated federal AGI and runs through Illinois base income (Line 9),
  the exemption allowance (Line 10), net income (Line 11), tax (Lines 12 and 14) and tax after
  nonrefundable credits (Line 19). The property-tax and K-12 credits (Line 16) are excluded by
  instruction. No Schedule CR, Schedule 1299-C or Schedule 4255 items apply, so Lines 13, 15, 17 and 18 are 0.
  Refundable credits (IL EITC, IL Child Tax Credit, Lines 29-30), use tax and payments are out of scope.
- **Method:** blind, source-only. I read no tenforty code, data or output, and ran no tax software
  or library. I did all arithmetic by hand from the form lines. Afterwards I checked each Line 11 x 0.0495
  product with exact decimal multiplication in a Python one-liner, which is plain arithmetic and not tax software.
  Every product matched.
- **Derived by:** Claude (Anthropic), model Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`), 2026-11-03.
- **Evidence kind (every value in this file):** Independent derivation.
- **Retrieval date for all sources:** 2026-11-03.

## Rounding conventions

Source: S24-I and S25-I p.4, "Should I round?". The text is the same in both years: "You must round cents to whole dollars on
Form IL-1040 and most schedules, as directed. To round you must drop amounts under 50 cents and increase amounts
of 50 to 99 cents to the next dollar. ... If you have to add two or more amounts to figure the amount to enter on a
line, include cents when adding the amounts and round only the total."

Applied here:
- All inputs are whole dollars, so Lines 1-11 are whole dollars with nothing to round.
- Line 12 = Line 11 x 0.0495, rounded half-up to whole dollars: under $0.50 is dropped, $0.50 through $0.99 goes up.
  This is the only line where rounding acts.
- Line 9 and Line 11 floor at zero (S24-I / S25-I p.7 and p.8). Line 12 "Cannot be less than zero" (form). Neither floor binds in these cases.

## Common line structure for every case

For every case below: Line 2 (tax-exempt interest/dividends) = 0, Line 3 (Schedule M additions) = 0, Line 5 = 0,
Line 6 = 0, Line 7 = 0, so Line 8 = 0. Line 10b = 0, Line 10c = 0, Line 13 = 0, Line 15 = 0, Line 16 = 0 (excluded),
Line 17 = 0, so Line 18 = 0 and Line 19 = Line 14 = Line 12. Each case lists those lines in short form, in full order.

## Assumptions

1. Full-year Illinois residents. No Schedule NR, so the L-204(a) allocation fraction is 1.
2. Federal AGI is stipulated as wages + interest + gains. There are no federal above-the-line adjustments, and the
   federal Form 1040 Line 11 (2024) / Line 11a (2025) figure equals that sum.
3. No Schedule M additions or subtractions (Lines 3 and 7 = 0). No tax-exempt interest (Line 2 = 0). No retirement or
   Social Security income (Line 5 = 0). No prior IL refund in AGI (Line 6 = 0).
4. Nobody can claim the filer or spouse as a dependent, so Line C is unchecked and the "No" rows of the p.8 chart apply.
5. All filers and spouses are under 65 and not blind (L10b = L10c = 0).
6. Dependents are dependents properly claimed on the federal return and listed on Schedule IL-E/EITC Step 2. The HoH case's one dependent is that filer's qualifying child.
7. No investment-credit recapture (L13 = 0), no credit for tax paid to other states (L15 = 0), no Schedule 1299-C credits
   (L17 = 0). The property-tax/K-12 credit (L16) is excluded by scope. Line 19 is therefore the tax after nonrefundable credits.
8. Interest in Case 14 is ordinary taxable interest. LTCG is net long-term gain reported on federal Schedule D and included in
   AGI. No capital loss carryover, and no employer-securities gain under the Line 5 subtraction.
9. The "Single" status in the boundary cases stands for all non-MFJ statuses for the $250,000 limit (S24-I/S25-I p.8 box).

## Ambiguities

1. **Rounding of Line 12.** The form says only "Multiply Line 11 by 4.95%". The whole-dollar rule on p.4 ("You must round
   cents to whole dollars ... drop amounts under 50 cents and increase amounts of 50 to 99 cents") is read here as applying
   to Line 12, because every IL-1040 line is printed with ".00". A filer who keeps cents, or who truncates, would differ by up
   to $1. No case here lands at exactly $0.50. Products ending .55, .6375, .70, .775, .85 and .925 round up. Products ending
   .0495, .275, .35 and .425 round down. Truncation would therefore differ from these figures by $1 in every round-up case
   (for example 2024 Single 20k: 852 truncated against 853 rounded).
2. **Limit test basis.** The instructions (p.8) test "federal AGI", and L-204(g) says "the taxpayer's adjusted gross income".
   I read these as the same number, IL-1040 Line 1. Base income (Line 9) equals AGI in every case here, so the choice
   does not matter for these cases.
3. **"Greater than" / "exceeds".** Both sources use a strict inequality. AGI exactly at $250,000 / $500,000 keeps the full
   exemption, and $1 over loses all of it, including dependent exemptions (Line 10 = "zero"). There is no phase-out.
4. **Dependent exemption authority after TCJA.** L-204(c) grants one basic amount per IRC sec. 151 exemption beyond one. The
   federal sec. 151 exemption amount is $0 for 2018-2025 and later. I follow IDOR's form mechanics: 10d = number of
   dependents x the basic amount (S24-E/S25-E Step 2 Line 1). I do not treat the federal $0 amount as removing the Illinois count.
5. **Exemption amounts.** $2,775 (2024) and $2,850 (2025) are IDOR's published figures. L-204(b)(7) defines them as $2,050 plus
   a CPI adjustment under (d-5), and I did not recompute that adjustment from CPI data.
6. **Rate citation.** I took the 4.95% rate from the IDOR instructions (p.1) and the form (Line 12). I did not retrieve the
   statutory rate section, 35 ILCS 5/201(b), for this derivation.
7. **2024 source provenance.** IDOR's "currentyear" URLs now serve 2025 documents, and I found no stable prior-year URL
   (several `prioryear/...` patterns returned 404). The 2024 documents are April 2025 Internet Archive captures of the
   official URLs, and each PDF identifies itself as the 2024 edition.
