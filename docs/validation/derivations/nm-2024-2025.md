# New Mexico PIT-1, tax years 2024 and 2025: independent derivation

Blind derivation, 2026-11-11. Sources: New Mexico Taxation and Revenue
Department (TRD) and IRS PDFs only, fetched on 2026-11-11. No tenforty code,
spec, table, graph, test or fixture was read (only
`docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`, for the evidence format). No tax
software or calculator was run. The only code used was a text search over the
official PDFs to locate table rows; every row quoted below was then read back
from the raw PDF text.

**Evidence kinds** (README definitions):

- Line 17 (New Mexico taxable income) in every case: **independent derivation**
  from the official instructions.
- Line 18 at taxable income of $100,000 or less: **published table row**
  (TRD Tax Rate Table), applied to an independently derived line 17. The whole
  return is therefore an independent derivation, not a published worked example.
- Line 18 above $100,000: **independent derivation** from the published
  over-$100,000 schedule printed on page T-7 of the Tax Rate Table.
- No value here is a published worked example.

"Tax" below is PIT-1 line 18, which in these scenarios equals line 22 (net New
Mexico income tax): lines 19, 20 and 21 are zero. It is before PIT-RC rebates
and credits (line 24), the working families tax credit (line 25) and
withholding (line 27).

Three results that may surprise a reader, each explained below:

- Scenario 7 gets **no** deduction for certain dependents: the worksheet
  subtracts one from the dependent count, so one dependent yields zero.

- Scenario 8 gets **no** 65-or-older exemption: single AGI of $50,000 is above
  the $28,500 ceiling.

## Rules used, with citations

### Federal inputs

- AGI: wages, plus the long-term capital gain in scenario 9; no adjustments.
  PIT-1 line 9 takes it from Form 1040 line 11 (NM24-F, NM25-F line 9).
- Standard deduction, 2024 (IRS24-F page 1 margin, "Standard Deduction for"):
  Single $14,600; Married filing jointly $29,200; Head of household $21,900.
  Single born before January 2, 1960, one box checked: $16,550 (IRS24-I,
  "Standard Deduction Chart for People Who Were Born Before January 2, 1960,
  or Were Blind").
- Standard deduction, 2025 (IRS25-F page 2 margin): Single $15,750; Married
  filing jointly $31,500; Head of household $23,625. Single born before
  January 2, 1961, one box checked: $17,750 (IRS25-I, "Standard Deduction
  Chart for People Who Were Born Before January 2, 1961, or Were Blind").
- PIT-1 line 12 is "Federal standard or itemized deduction amount (from
  federal Form 1040, line 12)" in both years (NM24-F, NM25-F; NM24-I and
  NM25-I "LINE 12", page PIT-1-25). On the 2025 Form 1040 that amount is on
  line 12e; line 13b (Schedule 1-A, including the $6,000 enhanced deduction
  for seniors) is a separate line and is not referenced by PIT-1. See
  Uncertainties 1.

### PIT-1 line 17

- 2024 (NM24-F line 17; NM24-I "LINE 17", page PIT-1-29): add lines 9, 10 and
  11, then subtract lines 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16; if negative, zero.
- 2025 (NM25-F line 17; NM25-I "LINE 17", page PIT-1-27): add lines 9, 10 and
  11, then subtract lines 12, 13, 14 and 15; line 16 is "Reserved for Future
  Use".
- Lines 10, 11 and (2024) 16 are zero in every scenario.

### Line 13, deduction for certain dependents (same text both years)

NM24-I and NM25-I, "LINE 13" and "Line 13. Worksheet for Calculating Deduction
for Certain Dependents", page PIT-1-25. Conditions:

- the taxpayer is not a dependent of another individual;
- the return is filed as head of household or married filing jointly;
- "Deduction is valid beginning tax year 2019, as long as the exemption amount
  pursuant to Section 151 of the Internal Revenue Code is zero (0)."

Worksheet: (1) dependents and other dependents on PIT-1 line 8; (2) those on
PIT-S; (3) total; (4) "Qualified dependents. Subtract '1' from total
dependents"; (5) line 4 x $4,000.

### Line 14, low- and middle-income exemption (same text both years)

NM24-I and NM25-I, "LINE 14" (page PIT-1-25) and "New Mexico Low- and
Middle-Income Tax Exemption Worksheet" (page PIT-1-26).

- Eligible only if line 9 is at or below: Single $36,667; Married filing
  jointly, surviving spouse or head of household $55,000; Married filing
  separately $27,500.
- Worksheet: (1) line 9; (2) Single $20,000, MFJ/HOH $30,000, MFS $15,000;
  (3) line 1 - line 2, not below zero; (4) Single 0.15, MFJ/HOH 0.10, MFS
  0.20; (5) line 3 x line 4; (6) $2,500 - line 5; (7) exemptions from PIT-1
  line 5; (8) line 6 x line 7.
- Line 5 exemptions (worksheet on page PIT-1-23 in 2024): 1 for yourself,
  1 for spouse if MFJ, plus dependents and other dependents.

### PIT-ADJ line 13, exemption for persons 65 or older or blind (same both years)

NM24-A and NM25-A, "LINE 13" and "TABLE 1. Exemptions for Persons 65 or Older
or Blind", pages ADJ-4/ADJ-5. Up to $8,000 per qualifying taxpayer, by line 9
AGI. Single column of Table 1:

| Single AGI over | but not over | Exemption |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 18,000 | 8,000 |
| 18,001 | 19,500 | 7,000 |
| 19,501 | 21,000 | 6,000 |
| 21,001 | 22,500 | 5,000 |
| 22,501 | 24,000 | 4,000 |
| 24,001 | 25,500 | 3,000 |
| 25,501 | 27,000 | 2,000 |
| 27,001 | 28,500 | 1,000 |
| 28,501 | — | 0 |

Ceilings for the other columns: Married filing jointly, head of household and
surviving spouse, $51,000 (the $8,000 band ends at $30,000, then $3,000 steps);
Married filing separately, $25,500 (the $8,000 band ends at $15,000, then
$1,500 steps). NM25-I page PIT-1-27 restates the ceilings: "$51,000 for a joint
return; $28,500 for a single taxpayer; or $25,500 for married taxpayers filing
separately."

### PIT-ADJ line 16, net capital gains deduction (changed for 2025)

- 2024 (NM24-A "LINE 16", page ADJ-5): "You may deduct the greater of: 100% of
  your net capital gains, not to exceed $1,000; or 40% of your net capital
  gains."
- 2025 (NM25-A "LINE 16", page ADJ-5): "You may deduct the greater of: 100% of
  your net capital gains, not to exceed $2,500; or 40% of up to $1,000,000 of
  your net capital gain income from the sale of a business that is allocated
  or apportioned to New Mexico pursuant to Section 7-2-11 NMSA 1978".
- Both years define net capital gains by IRC 1222(11) (net long-term gain over
  net short-term loss) and exclude short-term gains.
- The PIT-ADJ total (line 28) goes to PIT-1 line 15.

### Line 18, tax: table or schedule

- NM24-I "LINE 18" (page PIT-1-29) and NM25-I "LINE 18" (page PIT-1-27): a
  full-year resident with no out-of-state income uses "the rate tables from
  the PIT-1 instructions, starting on page 1T", applied to line 17, and enters
  R on line 18a.
- The Tax Rate Table (NM24-T, NM25-T, pages T-1 to T-6) lists tax in $100
  bands, "More Than ... But Not Over", up to $100,000, with columns Single,
  Married Filing Jointly (also surviving spouse), Married Filing Separately,
  Head of Household. An amount exactly on a band edge belongs to the band it
  closes ("but not over").
- Page T-7 of each: "If line 17 of Form PIT-1 (Taxable Income) is over
  $100,000 use the following table to compute your tax."

### Rounding

NM24-I page PIT-1-21 and NM25-I page PIT-1-21 ("Round all numbers"): "Round all
numbers and enter only whole dollar amounts. For example, enter $10.49 as $10
and $10.50 as $11." Table amounts are already whole dollars. For the
over-$100,000 schedule the computed tax is rounded to the nearest dollar, with
50 cents rounding up.

## Per-scenario arithmetic

Line numbers are PIT-1 lines. Lines not shown are zero.

### Scenario 1 — Single, wages 20,000

Line 5 exemptions: 1. Line 14 worksheet (both years, AGI 20,000 ≤ 36,667, so
eligible): L1 20,000; L2 20,000; L3 20,000 − 20,000 = 0, so L5 = 0; L6 2,500 −
0 = 2,500; L7 1; L8 2,500 × 1 = **2,500**.

2024:
- L9 20,000
- L12 14,600
- L14 2,500
- L17 = 20,000 − 14,600 − 2,500 = **2,900**
- L18: table (NM24-T), band "more than 2,800 but not over 2,900", Single = **48**
  (2,900 is the top edge of the band, so it is in this band, not 2,900–3,000,
  which reads 50)

2025:
- L9 20,000
- L12 15,750
- L14 2,500
- L17 = 20,000 − 15,750 − 2,500 = **1,750**
- L18: table (NM25-T), band 1,700–1,800, Single = **26**

Rebates/credits excluded but potentially applicable: Low Income Comprehensive
Tax Rebate (PIT-RC Section 2; modified gross income $36,000 or less, NM24-R and
NM25-R). Not computed: it depends on modified gross income and exemptions
facts outside this scenario.

## Structural cross-check (not value evidence)

Using only the published documents: the 2024 Single table entry for the last
band, 99,900–100,000, and the T-7 base of $4,618 at $100,000 should differ by
about 4.9% × $50 if the table is computed at band midpoints. From the 70,300–
70,400 row (3,168) at 4.9% per dollar, the midpoint 99,950 gives 3,168 +
0.049 × 29,600 = 4,618.40, which agrees with the T-7 base. This only shows the
table and the schedule are mutually consistent; it does not independently
establish any row.

## Uncertainties

1. **2025 federal Schedule 1-A deductions (scenario 8).** The 2025 PIT-1 and
   its instructions still say line 12 is "from federal Form 1040, line 12",
   but the 2025 Form 1040 has lines 12a–12e and a new line 13b for Schedule
   1-A deductions (including the $6,000 enhanced deduction for seniors). The
   2025 NM instructions and PIT-ADJ instructions contain no mention of
   Schedule 1-A, line 12e or line 13b. I read line 12 as line 12e only, so the
   senior deduction is not subtracted and 2025 scenario 8 is 32,250 / 1,112.
   If New Mexico in fact allows the line 13b amount, taxable income would be
