Blind derivation, 2026-11-11. Official Connecticut DRS and IRS PDFs only. No
tenforty source, spec, tables, graphs, tests, other fixture files, software
output or third-party calculators were consulted; the only repository file read
was `docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md` (evidence format).

"State tax" below is **Form CT-1040 Line 6, Income Tax** (= Tax Calculation
Schedule Line 10), before the credit for taxes paid to other jurisdictions,
AMT, property tax credit, other credits, EITC and withholding.

## Connecticut AGI

Form CT-1040 Lines 1-5 (CT24-F / CT25-F p. 1; instructions p. 2): Line 1
federal AGI; Line 2 additions (Schedule 1, Line 38); Line 3 = 1 + 2; Line 4
subtractions (Schedule 1, Line 50); Line 5 = 3 - 4 = Connecticut AGI.

Assumptions for every scenario: no Schedule 1 additions or subtractions (wages
only; no Social Security, pension, IRA, military, municipal-bond or other
modification items). The capital gain in scenario 8 is assumed to be an
ordinary federal long-term gain with no Connecticut modification. So Line 5 =
Line 1 in all eighteen returns.

Things that do NOT enter Line 6 on this form:

- **Dependents.** The TCS has no dependent exemption or credit line; Tables
  A-E are keyed only on filing status and Connecticut AGI. Schedule
  CT-Dependent is an attachment listed on the form front. Scenarios 4 and 5
  therefore have the same Line 6.
- **Age.** Tables A-E carry no age-based amount. Scenario 7's age matters
  only for items it does not have (Social Security / pension subtractions).

## Method and rounding

**Which method.** Instructions p. 2, Line 6 (identical wording both years):

> If your Connecticut adjusted gross income is less than or equal to $102,000,
> you may use the Tax Tables on the DRS website ... If your Connecticut
> adjusted gross income is more than $102,000, you may: 1. Calculate your tax
> liability instantly by visiting myconneCT ...; or 2. Use the Tax Calculation
> Schedule beginning on Page 19.

The wording is "may" in both branches. The Tax Tables stop at $102,000, so the
TCS is the only paper method for scenarios 3 and 9. For AGI <= $102,000 the
Tax Tables are the method the Line 6 text designates, and they are used as the
primary value; nothing in the instructions forbids a <= $102,000 filer from
using the TCS, so the TCS figure is also shown.

**Tax Table bands.** $50 bands, "More Than / Less Than or Equal To". An AGI of
exactly 40,000 falls in the row "39,950 - 40,000", not the first row of the
next block. The published amounts reproduce exactly when the TCS is evaluated
at the band midpoint (e.g. 39,975) and the final result rounded to the nearest
dollar; that was verified by hand for all six rows used and is why the table
value is $1 below the exact-AGI TCS value in each case. This midpoint
construction is my inference from the numbers; the tables do not state it.

**Rounding.** The form front says "Whole Dollars Only" (CT24-F / CT25-F p. 1)
and every TCS money line is pre-printed with ".00"/"00" cents. I found no
sentence in the instructions that states the rounding direction. The Table B
examples (p. 20) round a half dollar up at the intermediate step:
"$25,000 x .0699 = $1,748" (exactly 1,747.50) and "$2,500 x .045 = $113"
(exactly 112.50). TCS figures below therefore round each line to whole
dollars, half up. This matters in exactly one place in this file: scenario 1,
TCS Line 9 (132.50 -> 133).

**Table lookup convention** (Tables A, C, D, E): "More Than / Less Than or
Equal To", so an AGI exactly on a boundary takes the lower row.

### 1. Single, wages 40,000

- Federal AGI: 40,000 (1040 line 11 / 11a). Connecticut AGI (Line 5): 40,000.
- Method: AGI <= 102,000 -> Tax Tables.
- **Tax Table row** (CT24-T p. 4, CT25-T p. 4; block "$39,000", row More Than
  39,950 / Less Than or Equal To 40,000; column Single): **1,191**.
- TCS at exact AGI:
  - TCS 1: 40,000
  - TCS 2 (Table A, Single, more than 39,000 <= 40,000): 5,000
  - TCS 3: 40,000 - 5,000 = 35,000
  - TCS 4 (Table B, Single, 10,000-50,000): 35,000 - 10,000 = 25,000;
    25,000 x .045 = 1,125; 200 + 1,125 = 1,325
  - TCS 5 (Table C, Single, 0-56,500): 0
  - TCS 6 (Table D, Single, 0-105,000): 0
  - TCS 7: 1,325
  - TCS 8 (Table E, Single, more than 33,300 <= 60,000): .10
  - TCS 9: 1,325 x .10 = 132.50 -> 133
  - TCS 10: 1,325 - 133 = **1,192** (unrounded: 1,192.50)
- Midpoint check of the table row: 39,975 - 5,000 = 34,975;
  200 + .045 x 24,975 = 1,323.875; x .90 = 1,191.49 -> 1,191. Matches.

## Uncertainties

1. **Table vs. TCS for AGI <= $102,000 ($1 difference in all seven table
   scenarios).** The instructions say "may use" the Tax Tables; they do not
   say the tables are mandatory or that the TCS is barred below $102,000. I
   treated the tables as the designated method and report both figures. A test
   pinned to the exact-AGI formula should expect the TCS column; a test pinned
   to the filed-return figure by the Line 6 text should expect the table
   column. I could not find a DRS statement resolving which governs when they
   differ.
2. **Rounding direction is inferred, not stated.** "Whole Dollars Only" is
   printed on the form, but I found no sentence in CT24-I / CT25-I giving the
   rounding rule; half-up comes from the two Table B examples. It affects only
   scenario 1's TCS figure (1,192 with per-line rounding; 1,192.50 unrounded,
   which would be 1,193 if rounded only at the end). The scenario 1 table
   value (1,191) is unaffected.
3. **Tax Tables are image-only PDFs.** The table rows were read visually from
   220-dpi renders, not extracted as text. Each row read agrees with an
   independent midpoint recomputation to the dollar, which makes a misread
   unlikely, but the midpoint rule itself is my inference.
4. **The "**" marker** beside "If CT AGI is ..." in the Tax Tables header: I
   did not locate its footnote text on the pages I inspected (pp. 1 and 10
   show only the "*" qualifying-surviving-spouse note).
5. **2024 standalone TCS form is a draft-watermarked PDF** at the official
   URL; not used. 2024 tables come from the Rev. 01/25 instructions. I did not
   compare the draft's tables with the instructions.
6. **Schedule 1 modifications not exhaustively reviewed.** I assumed none
   apply to plain wages or to an ordinary long-term capital gain, and that
   nothing age-based applies to a 67-year-old with wages only. I did not read
   every Schedule 1 line instruction to prove the negative.
7. **Scope.** Values are Line 6 only. Property tax credit (Schedule 3),
   Connecticut EITC (possibly relevant to scenario 6 depending on federal
   EITC eligibility), AMT, and withholding are outside this derivation. The
   IRS PDF hashes were not recorded.
8. **2025 = 2024.** The equality of the two years rests on my comparison of
   Tables A-E text and of the six table rows used; I did not diff the full
   Tax Tables between years.
