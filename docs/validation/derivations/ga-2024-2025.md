# Georgia individual income tax, TY2024 and TY2025: blind derivation

## Header

- **Scope:** Georgia full-year resident Form 500, starting from federal AGI (Form 500 Line 8) through Georgia taxable income (Line 15c), tax (Line 16), and balance after credits (Line 23). Cases have wages only, plus one case with taxable interest and long-term capital gain. All cases use the Georgia standard deduction, all filers are under 65, and dependents are as stated.
- **Method:** blind, source-only. I did not read any tenforty code, data, tests or output, and I did not run any tax software or library. Every figure below is hand arithmetic from the official form lines.

- **Evidence kind (every value in this document):** Independent derivation.

## Rounding conventions and tax table

- **Rate, not table.** Both years compute tax as Line 15c × flat rate. The 2024 rate is 5.39% and the 2025 rate is 5.19% ([S24] p. 17 and Form 500 Line 16; [S25] p. 17 and Form 500 Line 16). Neither booklet contains a tax table; a text search of both for "tax table" finds nothing. No table is required at any income level.
- **Whole dollars.** Every entry is in whole dollars ([S24]/[S25] p. 8: "Round to the nearest dollar"). Line 16 is rounded to the nearest dollar after the multiplication. All inputs here are whole dollars, so only Line 16 needs rounding. No case lands on exactly $0.50, so the tie-breaking rule (which the booklets do not state) never matters here.

- L11 = 12,000. L13 = 20,000 − 12,000 = 8,000.
- L14 = 0 × 4,000 = 0. L15a = 8,000. L15b = 0. **L15c = 8,000.**

- **2025:** L16 = 8,000 × 0.0519 = 415.20, rounded to **415**. L22 = 0. **L23 = 415.**

## Assumptions

1. All filers are full-year Georgia residents (no Schedule 3 proration) and are **under 62**, not just under 65. Under 62 with no disability means no retirement income exclusion (Schedule 1 line 7; $35,000 at ages 62 to 64). See ambiguity 1.
2. The federal standard deduction is used, so the Georgia standard deduction is mandatory ([S24]/[S25] p. 16). No itemizing, so the Line 19 itemizer credit ($300 per taxpayer) does not apply.
3. Schedule 1 adjustments (L9) are 0. The wages are ordinary taxable wages: no military pay, no first-responder or PTSD exclusions, no 529 or other subtractions. The interest is ordinary taxable interest, not U.S. obligation interest (which would be subtracted on Schedule 1 line 10) and not non-Georgia municipal interest (which would be an addition).
4. The LTCG is a federal net long-term capital gain already in FAGI. Georgia taxes it at the same flat rate as other income.
5. No NOL (L15b = 0). No other-state credit, IND-CR credits or Schedule 2 credits.
6. No filer is claimed or claimable as a dependent by another taxpayer.
7. "Dependents" means qualified dependents on L7a. There are no unborn dependents (L7b = 0).
8. One-time surplus refunds or rebates that Georgia enacted outside the return are not a Form 500 line, so they are excluded. Neither booklet puts one on Lines 16 to 23.
9. The figures for Single 400k and MFJ 400k assume no Georgia high-income surtax, phase-out or recapture. None appears on Form 500 or in the booklets for either year.

## Ambiguities

1. **"Under 65" versus Georgia's age-62 threshold.** The case spec says filers are under 65, but Georgia's retirement income exclusion starts at **62**. For the wages-only cases this does not matter, because the exclusion covers retirement income plus up to $5,000 of earned income, and wages would count. Case 10 is different: a filer aged 62 to 64 could exclude the $5,000 interest and $10,000 gain (up to $35,000 combined, including $5,000 of earned income). The exclusion would be min(35,000, 5,000 interest + 10,000 LTCG + 5,000 earned income) = 20,000. That would drop L15c to 95,000 − 20,000 − 12,000 = 63,000. I resolved this as under 62; the alternative is not computed in the summary table.
2. **HoH with no stated dependent.** Federal HoH requires a qualifying person, but that person need not be a Georgia "dependent" on L7a (for example, a parent who is not claimed). I followed "dependents as stated" (0) and also gave the 1-dependent alternative (8b).
3. **Half-dollar tie rule.** "Round to the nearest dollar" does not specify how ties at exactly $0.50 break. No case lands on .50, so this does not affect any value here.
4. **Low income credit boundary at FAGI exactly $20,000 (Case 1).** The text says "less than $20,000", and the p. 35 table's top bracket ends at $19,999. Both say FAGI of exactly $20,000 gets no credit. With 1 exemption the credit would have been only $5 even if available.
5. **2025 Low Income Credit worksheet extraction.** In the 2025 booklet's p. 35 worksheet, part of the text (the table body) did not come through in text extraction. The governing eligibility test (FAGI < $20,000) is stated in prose on [S25] p. 17, and no case is below $20,000, so the 2025 credit table values are not needed.
6. **Rate source.** The rates are taken from the DOR booklets and form lines. I did not retrieve the underlying statute (O.C.G.A. § 48-7-20 as amended for 2024 and 2025). If the statute and the DOR form ever disagree, these values follow the DOR form.
