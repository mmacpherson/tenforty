## Assumptions

- A1. The filing status is Married Filing Separately. The taxpayer is not 65 or older, not blind, cannot be claimed as a dependent, has no dependents, and the spouse does not itemize (Form 1040 line 12a/12b boxes are unchecked). The taxpayer takes the standard deduction, has no QBI deduction, no Schedule 1-A deductions, no credits, and no Schedule 2 line 1z or Schedule 3 line 1 amounts.
- A2. The only income items are wages (Form 1040 line 1a = 1z = $80,000) and Schedule 1 line 8z = −$15,000. Schedule 1 has no line 1 (taxable refund) and no line 8a (NOL). Schedule 1 line 26 adjustments are $0.
- A3. As the task instructs, the −$15,000 line 8z amount flows to Schedule 1 line 9 (−15,000), then line 10 (−15,000), then Form 1040 line 8. This derivation does not decide whether such a negative entry is permissible. It takes the amount as given.
- A4. There are no capital gains or qualified dividends, so Form 6251 line 7 uses the "All others" bullet, not Part III. There is no Form 2555 and no Form 4972.
- A5. The 2025 standard deduction is the post-OBBBA amount of $15,750 printed on the 2025 Form 1040 (S6) and in the 2025 instructions (S11). It supersedes the $15,000 in Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §3.15 (S13), which was issued before OBBBA.

## The 2b/2e question

**Form 6251 line 2b** reads: "Tax refund from Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 1 or line 8z" (S1, S2). It is a parenthesized entry, meaning a negative adjustment. The instructions, "Line 2b—Refund of Taxes" (S3, S4), limit it to two things. The first is a Schedule 1 line 1 refund attributable to state or local income taxes. The second is "refunds received in [2024/2025] and included in income on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8z, that are attributable to state or local personal property taxes or general sales taxes; foreign income taxes; or state, local, or foreign real property taxes." Any line 8z amount included must also be described next to the entry. Line 2b therefore reaches only the part of line 8z that is a **refund of taxes**. A negative "other income" amount that is not a refund falls outside it.
**Line 2b = $0** for both years.

**Form 6251 line 2e** reads: "Net operating loss deduction from Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8a. Enter as a positive amount" (S1, S2). Schedule 1 line 8a is captioned "Net operating loss" (S7, S8). Line 2e takes its value from line 8a alone, not from line 8z. The amount here is not an NOL and does not appear on line 8a.
**Line 2e = $0** for both years.

As a result, the −$15,000 receives no AMT adjustment. It reduces AGI, and through Form 6251 line 1 (2024) or line 1b (2025) it reduces AMTI by the same amount.

## Rounding rules applied

- All inputs are whole dollars, so no line-level rounding was needed.
- Line 16 is read directly from the published Tax Table, since taxable income is under $100,000. The rate-schedule midpoint check is not the source of the value.
- Line 7 was not reached because line 6 is $0. The 26% rate would otherwise apply up to $116,300 (2024) or $119,550 (2025) for MFS (S1, S2 line 7 text).

## Sensitivity: what a misclassification would produce (not the correct answer)

- If the −15,000 were wrongly entered on line 2e as +15,000, AMTI would be 80,000. For 2024, line 6 = 13,350 and TMT = 26% × 13,350 = 3,471, which is less than 6,147, so AMT = 0. For 2025, line 6 = 11,500 and TMT = 2,990, which is less than 5,755, so AMT = 0. TMT and AMTI would be wrong, but AMT would still be 0.
- If it were wrongly entered on line 2b as a negative 15,000, AMTI would be 50,000. That is below the exemption, so TMT and AMT would both be 0.
