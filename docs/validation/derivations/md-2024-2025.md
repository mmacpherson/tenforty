## Parameters

### Standard deduction, 2024: Worksheet 16A (2024 booklet, printed p. 12)

> STANDARD DEDUCTION WORKSHEET (16A) ... **Worksheet 1** [Single, Married filing separately or Dependent taxpayer] ... "$12,000 or less ... 1,800" / "If your income is at least $12,001 but not more than $17,999 ... Multiply by 15 percent (.15)" / "$18,000 or over ... 2,700".
>
> **Worksheet 2** [Married filing Jointly, Head of Household or Qualifying Surviving Spouse] ... "$24,333 or less ... 3,650" / "If your income is at least $24,334 but not more than $36,332 ... Multiply by 15 percent (.15)" / "$36,333 or over ... 5,450".
>
> "Enter your income from Line 16 of Form 502" ... "Enter your standard deduction on Line 17 of Form 502."

| Status | Minimum | 15% band (line 16) | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single, MFS | 1,800 if line 16 <= 12,000 | 12,001 - 17,999: 15% x line 16 | 2,700 if line 16 >= 18,000 |
| MFJ, HoH, QSS | 3,650 if line 16 <= 24,333 | 24,334 - 36,332: 15% x line 16 | 5,450 if line 16 >= 36,333 |

The input is Form 502 line 16 (Maryland AGI), not federal AGI. In these cases the two are equal.

### Standard deduction, 2025: flat amounts (2025 booklet, Instruction 16, printed p. 13)

> "If your filing status is single, dependent, or married filing separately, your standard deduction amount is **$3,350**. If your filing status is married filing jointly, head of household, or qualifying surviving spouse, your standard deduction amount is **$6,700**. Write the amount on Line 17 of Form 502."

The "What's new" note on p. 2 says the BRFA (HB 352) "increases the standard deduction". Current TG §10-217(b) matches: $3,350 for individuals other than heads of household, surviving spouses and joint filers, and $6,700 for heads of household, surviving spouses and spouses filing jointly. §10-217(c) indexes the amounts to inflation only "for each taxable year beginning after December 31, 2025" (rounded down to a multiple of $50), so 2025 uses the flat amounts as stated. The 15% calculation no longer applies in 2025.

### Personal exemption (Exemption Amount Chart 10A; identical in the 2024 and 2025 booklets, printed p. 4)

> "The personal exemption is $3,200. This exemption is reduced once the taxpayer's federal adjusted gross income exceeds $100,000 ($150,000 if filing Joint, Head of Household, or Qualifying Surviving Spouse with Dependent Child)."

| Federal AGI | Single or MFS: each exemption | Joint, HoH or QSS: each exemption |
|---|---|---|
| $100,000 or less | $3,200 | $3,200 |
| Over $100,000, not over $125,000 | $1,600 | $3,200 |
| Over $125,000, not over $150,000 | $800 | $3,200 |
| Over $150,000, not over $175,000 | $0 | $1,600 |
| Over $175,000, not over $200,000 | $0 | $800 |
| In excess of $200,000 | $0 | $0 |

TG §10-211(b)(1) gives "$3,200 for each exemption". §10-211(c)(1) (other individuals) sets $1,600 if FAGI "does not exceed $125,000", $800 if "greater than $125,000 but not greater than $150,000", and $0 above $150,000. §10-211(c)(2) (joint, head of household, surviving spouse) sets $1,600 / $800 / $0 at $175,000 and $200,000. The statute and the chart agree.

**Number of exemptions.** Form 502 page 2, Exemptions line A, has two boxes: "Yourself" and "Spouse". MFJ checks both, giving 2 exemptions. Single, MFS, HoH and QSS check "Yourself" only, giving 1, because these returns have no dependents. Line D (total exemptions) goes to line 19. The 2025 booklet, in its passage on married couples filing separate Maryland returns (printed p. 12), says: "Each spouse must claim their own personal exemption."

### Rate schedules (Instruction 17; 2024 booklet p. 13-14, 2025 booklet p. 14)

**Schedule I** (Single, MFS, dependent): 2% on 0-1,000; 3% on 1,000-2,000; 4% on 2,000-3,000; 4.75% on 3,000-100,000 (base $90.00); 5.00% on 100,000-125,000 (base $4,697.50); 5.25% on 125,000-150,000 (base $5,947.50); 5.50% on 150,000-250,000 (base $7,260.00).
- 2024: 5.75% over $250,000 (base $12,760.00).
- 2025 (BRFA): 5.75% on 250,000-500,000 (base $12,760.00); **6.25%** on 500,000-1,000,000 (base $27,135.00); **6.50%** over $1,000,000 (base $58,385.00).

**Schedule II** (MFJ, HoH, QSS): 2%, 3% and 4% on the first $3,000 as above; 4.75% on 3,000-150,000 (base $90.00); 5.00% on 150,000-175,000 (base $7,072.50); 5.25% on 175,000-225,000 (base $8,322.50); 5.50% on 225,000-300,000 (base $10,947.50).
- 2024: 5.75% over $300,000 (base $15,072.50).
- 2025: 5.75% on 300,000-600,000 (base $15,072.50); **6.25%** on 600,000-1,200,000 (base $32,322.50); **6.50%** over $1,200,000 (base $69,822.50).

Current TG §10-105(a)(1) and (a)(2) list the same brackets as the 2025 schedules. Below $100,000 of taxable income, Schedules I and II are identical. That is why the booklet prints a single tax table column for all statuses.

**Mandatory tax table** (2024 p. 13 and 2025 p. 14, Instruction 17):

> "You must use the tax tables if your taxable income is less than $100,000. The [year] Maryland tax rate schedules are shown so you can see the tax rate that applies to all levels of income; however, do not use them to figure your tax. Instead, use the tax tables if your income is under $100,000; otherwise, use the appropriate row in the MARYLAND TAX COMPUTATION WORKSHEET SCHEDULES (17A) ..."

Table header: "At least / But less than / Your Maryland tax is". Rows run in $50 bands (below $100 they are 0-50, 50-75 and 75-100). The 0-50 row is $0. Every table row used below was transcribed from the booklet PDF. Each one equals the schedule tax at the band midpoint, rounded half-up to a whole dollar (for example 3,000-3,050 gives 90 + 25 x .0475 = 91.19, printed as 91). For $100,000 and over, the 17A worksheet computes (line 20 - subtraction amount) x rate + addition amount, which is the exact schedule tax in cents.

## Rounding

- Booklet, "Completing the Return" (same in 2024 and 2025, printed p. 1): "You may round off all cents to the nearest whole dollar. Fifty cents and above should be rounded to the next dollar. State calculations are rounded to the nearest cent."
- The 2024 Worksheet 16A gives no rounding rule for the 15% product. Below, it is kept in cents. Where whole-dollar rounding would change line 17, the per-case notes give that figure too. In these cases it never changes the table tax.
- Line 21 = the tax table amount (whole dollars) when line 20 < $100,000. Otherwise it is the 17A result, exact to the cent. The "exact schedule" column gives the unrounded rate-schedule tax for every case.

## Per-case arithmetic

### 2024 Single, wages/AGI 10,000
- Line 16 (MD AGI) = 10,000.00
- Standard deduction: Worksheet 1, min (<=12,000): 1,800.00
- Line 17 = 1,800.00; line 18 = 10,000 - 1,800.00 = 8,200.00
- Line 19: chart 10A row "$100,000 or less", Single/MFS column = 3,200 each x 1 exemption = 3,200.00
- Line 20 = 8,200.00 - 3,200.00 = 5,000.00
- Exact schedule I: 90.00 + (5,000.00 - 3,000) x .0475 = 185 (cents: 185.00)
- Tax table: row at least 5,000 but less than 5,050 gives **line 21 = 186**

### 2024 Single, wages/AGI 15,000
- Line 16 (MD AGI) = 15,000.00
- Standard deduction: Worksheet 1, middle band: 15,000 x .15 = 2,250.00
- Line 17 = 2,250.00; line 18 = 15,000 - 2,250.00 = 12,750.00
- Line 19: chart 10A row "$100,000 or less", Single/MFS column = 3,200 each x 1 exemption = 3,200.00
- Line 20 = 12,750.00 - 3,200.00 = 9,550.00
- Exact schedule I: 90.00 + (9,550.00 - 3,000) x .0475 = 401.125 (cents: 401.13)

## Ambiguities and notes

1. **2024 joint thresholds against the plain 15% rule.** Worksheet 2 sets the minimum at "$24,333 or less" and the maximum at "$36,333 or over". The exact crossover points are 24,333.33 and 36,333.33. At line 16 = 36,333 the worksheet gives 5,450, while 15% x 36,333 = 5,449.95. Here the worksheet (the form's instruction) is used. The $0.05 difference does not change the tax table result. The same applies at 24,333 (3,650 against 3,649.95).
2. **2024 rounding of the 15% product.** The worksheet gives none. Cents are kept here, per "State calculations are rounded to the nearest cent". Optional whole-dollar rounding does not change line 21 in any case here.
3. **Table versus schedule.** Below $100,000 the booklet makes the table mandatory, so line 21 is the table amount. The exact schedule figure differs from it by up to about $1.19 (midpoint rounding).
4. **Exactly $100,000 of taxable income (2025).** The 2025 table ends at "less than 100,000" and the 2025 17A schedule begins "At least $100,001". In 2024 it begins "At least $100,000". A taxable income of exactly 100,000 is covered by neither 2025 row as printed. The schedule gives 4,697.50 either way. No case here lands on it.
5. **2025 17A Schedule I misprint.** The 6.25% row reads "At least $250,001 but not over $1,000,000", where the rate schedule says $500,001. No case here reaches it.
6. **HoH and QSS exemptions.** Each gets one personal exemption ("Yourself"). Dependents would add more, but none are assumed. The QSS status is labelled "Qualifying surviving spouse with dependent child". Taking it without a dependent is a hypothetical input.
7. **MFS spouse exemption.** Only the filer's own exemption is used. The 2025 booklet says "Each spouse must claim their own personal exemption".
8. **Basis for the exemption tiers.** The tiers key on **federal** AGI, not Maryland AGI. The two are equal here.
