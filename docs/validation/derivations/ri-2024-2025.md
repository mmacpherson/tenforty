Derived 2026-11-11 by a blind Claude subagent from Rhode Island Division of
Taxation publications only. No tenforty code, spec, table, graph, test or other
fixture file was read (only `docs/validation/state-fixtures/README.md`, for the
evidence format). No tax software or calculator was run. The only tooling was
`curl`, `pdftotext -layout` and a regex that pulled "at least / but less than /
tax" triples out of the official tax-table PDF text; each row used below was
then re-matched verbatim against the raw PDF text.

**Evidence kinds** (per the README):

- Taxable income (line 7) of every scenario: **independent derivation**.
- Line 8 for scenarios 1-3 and 5-9 (taxable income under $100,000): the tax is
  a **published table row**; the whole return that reaches that row is still an
  **independent derivation**, not a published worked example.
- Line 8 for scenarios 4 and 10 (taxable income over $100,000): **independent
  derivation** using the official Tax Computation Worksheet.
- Nothing here is a published worked example.

Rhode Island does not allow federal itemized deductions (I24/I25, line 4), so
the standard deduction is the only deduction.

### Exemptions (RI Schedule E, in F24/F25; I24 page I-7, I25 pages I-7 to I-8)

Line 1a "Yourself", line 1b "Spouse" (joint return), lines 2a-2m dependents;
line 3 = boxes checked on 1a and 1b; lines 4a-4c count dependents; line 5 = sum,
carried to RI-1040 line 6. Schedule E has **no** box for age 65 or older or for
blindness, and the standard deduction chart has no age-based addition. So
scenario 8 (age 67) has one exemption and the ordinary Single deduction, the
same as a younger filer.

### Modifications (RI Schedule M; I24 pages I-8 on, I25 pages I-9 on)

None applies. The scenarios have only wages (and, in scenario 9, a long-term
capital gain). I searched both instruction booklets for "capital gain": Schedule
M has no modification for long-term capital gains generally, so the gain stays
in modified federal AGI and is taxed at the ordinary schedule rates. The
age-related modifications (Social Security, pension/annuity income for those at
full retirement age) apply to those kinds of income, not to wages, so scenario
8 has no modification.

2025 only: new RI Schedule HR1 adds back certain P.L. 119-21 (H.R.1) federal
deductions (I25 pages I-12 to I-13): line 1a business interest (§163(j)), 1b
§174A research amortization, 1c §179(b) depreciation, 1d §181 sound recording
production, 1e reserved. All are business deductions; none arises on a
wages-only return, so the 2025 line 2 is 0.

### Which tax method is required (T24/T25 page T-1 caution and page T-2 header)

Page T-1: "DO NOT use [the Tax Rate Schedule] to figure your Rhode Island tax.
Instead, if your taxable income is less than $100,000 use the Rhode Island Tax
Table located on pages T-2 through T-7. If your taxable income is larger than
$100,000, use the Rhode Island Tax Computation Worksheet located on page T-1."
Page T-2: "Use if your Rhode Island taxable income is less than $100,000. If
your taxable income is $100,000 or more, use the Tax Rate Schedules located on
page T-1."

So the Tax Table is required below $100,000 and the Computation Worksheet above
it. Scenarios 4 and 10 use the worksheet; the other eight use the table.

Computation Worksheet (T24/T25 page T-1; also I24 page I-11, I25 page I-13):
(a) line 7 amount; (b) multiplication amount; (c) = (a) × (b); (d) subtraction
amount; tax = (c) − (d), "Enter here and on RI-1040, line 8".

Tax Table: rows are $50 wide ("At least / But less than"), tax in whole
dollars. The page T-2 example: taxable income $25,300.00 → row 25,300-25,350 →
tax $950.00.

### Rounding

- I found **no** whole-dollar rounding instruction in I24, I25, W24, W25, T24
  or T25 (text search for "round", "nearest", "cents", "whole dollar"). The
  form's amount boxes have a dollars column and a cents column.
- The only stated rounding rule is in the Standard Deduction and Exemption
  Worksheets, line 6: "If the result is not a whole number, increase it to the
  next higher whole number (for example, increase 0.0004 to 1)."
- Tax Table amounts are printed in whole dollars; I take them as printed.
- Computation Worksheet amounts carry cents (the subtraction amounts are
  $774.50, $2,957.52, $799.00, $3,051.46). I report the worksheet result to the
  cent, rounding half-up at the third decimal only where one appears.
- All inputs here are whole dollars, so lines 1-7 are whole dollars with no
  rounding needed in any scenario.

### Federal AGI

Federal AGI (Form 1040 line 11 for 2024, line 11a for 2025, as F24/F25 line 1
cite) is total income less adjustments to income. Each scenario has wages only
(scenario 9: wages plus a long-term capital gain, which is included in total
income in full) and no adjustments, so AGI equals the stated income. The
federal standard deduction, the age-65 additional deduction and the 2025 senior
deduction all come after AGI and do not enter the Rhode Island return.
Evidence kind: independent derivation; no IRS table row was needed.

### Structural cross-checks (not value evidence)

- Each table row used equals 3.75% of the row midpoint, rounded to the nearest
  dollar (for example 2024 row 69,500-69,550: 69,525 × 0.0375 = 2,607.19 →
  2,607). All sixteen rows agree. This only shows the transcription is
  self-consistent with the published rate.
- Worksheet against the printed rate schedule ("Pay + % on excess"):
  - 2024 #4: 2,904.38 + 0.0475 × (134,500 − 77,450) = 2,904.38 + 2,709.875 = 5,614.255 (worksheet: 5,614.25)

## Uncertainties

1. **Cents on worksheet results.** No Rhode Island publication I fetched states
   a whole-dollar rounding rule, and the form has a cents column. I therefore
   report the worksheet results to the cent: 5,614.25, 12,724.90, 5,566.00,
   12,110.43. If a consumer rounds line 8 to whole dollars these become 5,614,
   12,725, 5,566 and 12,110. A tolerance of $0.50 on the two worksheet
   scenarios per year covers both readings and follows from that rule gap, not
   from any observed output. For 2024 #4 the rate-schedule form gives 5,614.255,
   half a cent above the worksheet's exact 5,614.25; an implementation built on
   the schedule could show 5,614.26. The worksheet is the method the
   publication requires.
2. **Third-decimal rounding** of 12,724.899 and 12,110.428 to the cent is my
   assumption (ordinary rounding); the worksheet does not say.
3. **Tax table versus formula.** For scenarios under $100,000 the table is
   required, and the table tax differs from exact 3.75% of taxable income by up
   to about a dollar (for example 2024 #1: table 170, exact 168.75). An
   implementation that computes the formula without the $50-row midpoint will
   not match the published rows.
4. **Exactly $100,000** of taxable income is worded inconsistently (page T-1:
   table if "less than $100,000", worksheet if "larger than $100,000"; page
   T-2: schedules if "$100,000 or more"). No scenario lands there.
5. **Provenance of the final PDFs.** The Division's HTML form index pages
   returned HTTP 403, so I could not confirm that the PDFs used are the ones
   currently linked there. They are on `tax.ri.gov`, carry no DRAFT stamp, and
   the 2024 and 2025 parameters agree across the form, instructions, worksheet
   page, tax table page T-1 and Advisory 2024-26. The 2024 final table differs
   from the October 2024 draft only in the draft watermark. I did not diff the
   2025 final table against its draft row by row.
6. **Instruction typo.** I24/I25 line 4 says to "see the Exemption Worksheet"
   when line 3 exceeds the threshold; the form itself and the worksheet page
   make clear that the Standard Deduction Worksheet is meant.
7. **Scenario assumptions I supplied:** no filer can be claimed as a dependent
   by someone else (otherwise the exemption is zero); the children in scenarios
   6, 7 and 10 qualify as dependents under the federal rules Schedule E points
   to; no tips, overtime or business items; no Rhode Island credits, use tax,
   or individual mandate penalty. In scenario 10 "two dependents" is read as 4
   exemptions (two spouses plus two dependents).
8. **Scenario 9.** I found no capital-gain modification or preferential rate in
