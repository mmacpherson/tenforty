### Line 20 method and tolerances

Form 1040ME line 20 (S1, S7) reads: "Find the tax for the amount on line 19 in
the tax table in this booklet or compute your tax using the tax table or tax
rate schedules". Neither year has a mandatory table cutoff, so the rate schedule
is an equally legal method.

- **Expected values.** They remain the deriver's: table rows below $100,000 and
  schedule values above it.
- **The graph** uses the schedule.
- **Below $100,000.** The table-versus-schedule gap is a table-method
  compatibility difference, not a tax-law defect. Its signatures are owned by
  tenforty-tj2.20.

Each signature is centred on its exact, source-derived residual, with a
half-width of $0.50 for line 20's whole-dollar rounding only. The residual has
three terms:

- **Table term:** rate × (taxable income − row midpoint).
- **Printed-base offset.** The published schedules print whole-dollar bracket
  bases, and the graph uses exact bracket arithmetic. Graph minus printed:
  - 2024 Single: 6.75% −0.10; 7.15% −0.475.
  - 2024 MFJ 7.15%: −0.575.
  - 2025 Single: 6.75% +0.40; 7.15% +0.275.
  - 2025 MFJ 7.15%: −0.45.
  - Every 5.8% bracket: 0.

  This term is a separate strict signature reason, owned provisionally by
  tenforty-b72.42.
- **Deduction shortfall:** rate × (tenforty-b72.43 / b72.44).

Case 11's cell rounds an exact half (2,885.50 → 2,886) and sits on the bound, so
the bound carries a 1e-6 float guard.

**Line 18.** The $0.50 line-18 tolerance covers these 18 fixtures only. It is
not a global bound (see ratio quantization below).

### Implementation notes

  statute's unrounded fraction (5126-A(2)), capped at 1, and keeps cents on line
  18. It deliberately models neither worksheet precision rule. Both residuals
  are newly introduced by this line and are owned by tenforty-b72.42.
  1. **Ratio quantization.** Worksheet line 5 is entered to 4 decimal places.

- **Ratio rounding in the 18 cases.** The 4-place ratio either terminates or
  its rounding does not move line 20.
- **Compatibility rule.** An explicit `dependent_exemptions` total is treated
  as the **pre-phase-out** amount: worksheet line 6 (line 13 x the per-exemption
  amount), including the base. A caller must not pass an already-phased line-18

  the status base, then phases out the result.

## Rule summary

### Filing status
Form 1040ME lines 3-7 use the filing status from the federal return (S2 and S8, p.3, "Lines 3-7"). The five statuses are Single, MFJ, MFS, HoH and QSS.

- QSS uses the MFJ standard deduction and phase-out thresholds (S8 p.3-4 chart and worksheet; S2 worksheet).
- QSS uses the MFJ tax column ("*This column must also be used by a surviving spouse with dependent child", S3/S9).
- QSS uses the "Married Individuals and Surviving Spouses" rate schedule.

### Personal exemption: line 13 count, line 18 amount
Line 13 counts exemptions (S2/S8 p.3):

| Filing status | Line 13 | Exceptions |
|---|---|---|
| Single, MFS, HoH, QSS | 1 | 0 if you can be claimed as a dependent. MFS is 2 if you would claim a federal exemption for your spouse (spouse has no gross income, per S14). |
| MFJ | 2 | 0 or 1 if one or both spouses can be claimed as dependents |

**Dependents get no personal exemption.** Line 13a counts qualifying children and dependents only for the Dependent Exemption Tax Credit (Schedule A). The credit is a tax credit, not a deduction, so it does not affect line 20. Quick Facts give it as $300 per dependent for 2024, and $305 (age 6+) or $610 (under 6) for 2025, subject to phase-out.

Line 18 = line 13 × the exemption amount:

| Year | Per exemption | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | $5,000 | S2 p.4; S4 |
| 2025 | $5,150 | S7 line 18; S8 p.4; S10 |

The statutory base is $4,150 (S14), indexed by 1.215 for 2024 and 1.25 for 2025 (S4/S10 note).

### Tax table
The form gives no mandatory cut-over. Line 20 says: "Find the tax for the amount on line 19 in the tax table in this booklet or compute your tax using the tax table or tax rate schedules available at maine.gov/revenue/tax-return-forms" (S7 line 20; same in S1).

- **Booklet table:** covers $0 to $52,000.
- **Full table (S3/S9):** $100 rows from $100 to $100,000. Columns are Single/MFS, MFJ (also QSS), HoH.
- **"100,000 and over" row:**

  | Year | Single/MFS | HoH | MFJ |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 2024 | 6,653 + 7.15% of excess over 100,000 | 6,406 + 7.15% of excess over 100,000 | "See the tax rate schedule below" |
  | 2025 | 6,638 + 7.15% of excess over 100,000 | 6,384 + 7.15% of excess over 100,000 | "See the tax rate schedule below" |

- **Row values:** every row I checked equals the schedule evaluated at the row midpoint, rounded to whole dollars. Example: 2025 Single row 32,000-32,100 = 1,554 + 0.0675 × (32,050 − 26,800) = 1,908.375, and the table shows 1,908.
- **Lowest rows:** the 2024 table (S2, S3) and the 2025 booklet (S8) have rows 0-50 → 0 and 50-100 → 3. The 2025 standalone table (S9) has a single row 0-100 → 3 (see Ambiguities).

## Ambiguities / open questions

- **A1. Rounding of the line 5 ratio.** The worksheet prints the ratio with 4 decimal places but does not say whether to round or truncate. The statute (S13 sub-§2; S14 sub-§2) states an unrounded fraction capped at 1. Among these scenarios only #7 and #16 have a non-terminating ratio:
  - #7: the deduction moves by ±1 to 2 dollars (10,151 / 10,152 / 10,153).
  - #16: the deduction is 11,000 rounded or exact, 11,001 truncated.
  - Line 20 is the same under all three methods in both cases. I recommend tests pin **tax** and keep a $2 tolerance on the deduction, or pin the 4-place round-half-up value.
- **A2. Taxable income at or above $100,000: table "and over" row vs. rate schedule.** Line 20 allows either "the tax table or tax rate schedules", with no stated precedence.
  - For Single/MFS and HoH, the full table's "$100,000 and over" row starts from the midpoint value of the last row (6,653 / 6,406 in 2024; 6,638 / 6,384 in 2025). The schedule at exactly 100,000 is about $3.30 higher for Single: 2025 schedule at 100,000 = 6,641.33.
  - The two methods differ by about $3 to $4: #7 7,003 vs 7,000; #8 24,249 vs 24,245; #16 6,917 vs 6,913; #17 24,197 vs 24,194.
  - I chose the **rate schedule** as primary because it is the statutory instrument (§5111 as indexed in S4/S10) and the MFJ column itself defers to it. A test may reasonably accept either, so a tolerance of about $5 is justified by the published table/schedule discrepancy, not by observed output.
  - Below $100,000 the table rows are primary. Exact-schedule values differ by up to about $3.
- **A3. Lowest row of the 2025 table.** The standalone 2025 table (S9) shows "0 – 100 → 3". The 2025 booklet (S8) and both 2024 tables show "0 – 50 → 0" and "50 – 100 → 3".
  - For TI = 0 (#15) I used 0. The form instructs that tax is found for the line-19 amount, and zero tax on zero income is consistent with the booklet and the schedule.
  - S9's printed row would literally give $3. This looks like a typesetting issue in S9 and should be confirmed with MRS.
- **A4. 2025 standard deduction vs. OBBBA.** §5124-C(1-A) says "equal to the federal standard deduction", but the Code is fixed as of 2024-12-31 (§111(1-A)), so Maine uses the pre-OBBBA $15,000 / $22,500 / $30,000 (S8 chart, S10). Anyone wiring Maine to the computed federal standard deduction would get $15,750 / $23,625 / $31,500 for 2025. **That would be wrong for Maine.**
- **A5. Negative taxable income.** Line 19 is "Line 16 minus lines 17 and 18". The form does not say to floor it at zero. I floored #15 at 0, the conventional reading, and the tax is 0 either way.
- **A6. QSS exemption count is 1, not 2** (S2/S8 line 13 table). QSS gets the MFJ standard deduction, MFJ thresholds and MFJ tax column, but only one exemption.
- **A7. Dependents.** Line 13a dependents do not increase line 18. They feed the Dependent Exemption Tax Credit on Schedule A, which is not part of line 20. The "/2" and "/1" dependent counts in the scenarios therefore leave line 20 unchanged.
- **A8. The 2024 standard deduction is taken from federal Form 1040 line 12** (S2 line 17). For this domain that is 14,600 / 21,900 / 29,200, which matches the S4 Quick Facts.
