None. The booklet fixes line 20 as the table row at or below $100,000, so the
record's schedule cross-checks are not alternatives.

### Comparison with the graph (2026-11-25, after the record was committed)

Two cases (2024 scenarios 3 and 9, Calculation I at whole-dollar income) match
exactly. The other 18 depart by one of four mechanisms:

- `tenforty-tj2.20`: the graph prices the Calculation I schedule at line 19
  where the booklet requires the $50 table row (2024 s1, s2, s4, s5, s6, s8).
  Bound: from -(rate x $50 + $0.50) to +$0.50. The record shows the printed
  rows are the schedule evaluated inside the $50-wide row (up to $60,000, the
  schedule at the row midpoint; above, about $1 under it), so the schedule at
  the row's lower edge cannot differ by more than the rate times the width. The
  same bound is carried by every other table case below.
- `tenforty-b72.63` (2025, every case
  except s7 and s10, which carry it inside their own shifts): `TablesDC2025.hs` carries the
  federal OBBBA amounts $15,750 / $31,500 / $23,625. The 2025 D-40 booklet
  (p. 4, p. 13) and the CFO letter keep DC at $15,000 / $30,000 / $22,500 and
  state DC does not follow the OBBBA increase, so the record is right. Taxable
  income is low by exactly $750 (Single), $1,500 (MFJ) or $1,125 (HoH) (the
  federal amounts are the IRS 2025 figures; the DC amounts are the record's).
  Tax bound: rate times that shift +/- $0.50 (the graph keeps cents), widened
  by rate x $50 on the table-row cases. Calculation I cases (s3, s9) carry no
  table widening.
- `tenforty-b72.64` (s7, both years): the graph
  has no additional standard deduction for age 65, so line 19 is high by
  exactly $1,950 (2024) / $2,000 (2025), less the 2025 standard-deduction
  excess above ($1,250 in 2025). Tax bound: 6% of the shift, with the table-row
  widening.
- `tenforty-s48h.73` (s10, both years): the graph takes the standard deduction
  and ignores itemizing, so line 19 is the record's Calculation F deduction
  (12,000) less the graph's standard deduction ($14,600 in 2024, $15,750 in
  2025): exactly -$2,600 and -$3,750. Tax bound: 6.5% of the shift with the
  table-row widening.

Every departure is a strict, bounded signature in `DEFECTS`: the test fails if
the graph leaves the bound, and also if it starts matching. Bounds come from
the mechanism and the record's own figures, never from the observed residual.
The s7 and s10 2025 shifts include the graph's $15,750 deduction, so they move
with the 2025 standard-deduction defect.
