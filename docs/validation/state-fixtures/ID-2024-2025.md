### Quantities pinned

The graph's "L42" is its line 20 tax less an "other credits" input that is zero
here. It excludes the $10 permanent building fund tax, which the record keeps
separate, so the record's PBF column is not pinned.

### Inputs

Wages are `w2_income`; the stock gain is `long_term_capital_gains`; the
children are `num_dependents`; the age-67 filer is `filer_65_or_older=True`.
The record's 2025 age-67 figure assumes a valid SSN, birth before January 2,
1961, and no tips, overtime or car-loan interest (record U1).

### Permitted alternatives

None. The instructions fix the rounding (whole dollars, 50 cents and above up)
and the worksheet; no scenario is near a half-dollar tie (record U4). The
record's 2025 age-67 alternative ($32,250 / $1,454, "if line 18 were zero") is
not listed as an outcome: the 2025 instructions for line 18 say to add federal
Form 1040 lines 13a and 13b, and no Form 39R addition reverses it (record U1,
which found no contrary source).

### Not pinned

None.

### Comparison with the graph (2026-11-25, after the record was committed)

Line 19 matches in 16 of 18 cases; line 27/26 matches in one (2025 Single
$20,000, tax $0 on both sides). The other 17 are signed.

- `tenforty-b72.42` (eleven cases, tax only): the graph keeps cents where line 20
  is entered in whole dollars. The bound is +/- $0.50 (the rounding rule).
- `tenforty-s48h.53` (existing bug, ID ignores `num_dependents`, age):
  - Child tax credit, four cases (MFJ with two children, HoH with one, both
    years): `STATE_GRAPH_CONFIGS[ID]` in `src/tenforty/mappings.py` has an
    empty `natural_to_node`, and `tenforty-spec/forms/IDFORM40_2024.hs` and
    `IDFORM40_2025.hs` take credits only as a raw input (line 26), so the
    $205-per-child credit never applies. The graph's tax is high by the credit:
    $410 or $205, +/- $0.50 for the cents. Taxable income matches.
  - Age 65 additional standard deduction, 2024 Single age 67: the graph's line
    16 is the status base only, so taxable income is high by the record's
    $1,950 and tax by $1,950 x 5.695% = $111.05, +/- $0.50.
- `tenforty-b72.67` (new, 2025 Single age 67 only):
  line 18 is a raw input in the spec and the senior deduction never reaches it.
  With the missing $2,000 age addition, taxable income is high by $8,000 and tax
  by $8,000 x 5.3% = $424.00, +/- $0.50.

How each bound was derived. Rounding departures use the form's whole-dollar rule
alone. The credit bound is the record's per-child amount times the number of
children. The age bounds are the record's omitted deduction amounts (taxable
income exactly; tax at the record's worksheet rate), plus the rounding half
dollar. No bound uses the observed residual.

Every departure is a strict, bounded signature in `DEFECTS`: the test fails if
the graph leaves the bound, and also if it starts matching.

### Doubts about the record

U1 (2025 line 18 and the Schedule 1-A deduction) rests on the instructions
heading and not on the printed form label or the statute; I take it as the
source. Under it, the 2025 Form 40 deducts a federal Schedule 1-A amount that
the graph also does not carry.
