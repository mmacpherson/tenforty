## Rule summary

### Filing statuses (S1/S2 pp.7–8; rate-schedule headers p.48)
N-11 has five filing-status ovals: 1 Single, 2 Married filing jointly, 3 Married filing separately, 4 Head of Household, 5 Qualifying Surviving Spouse. They mirror the federal statuses one to one, and the HoH and QSS tests are the federal ones restated. Tax computation:
- Schedule I / table column "Single or Married filing separately": ovals 1 and 3.
- Schedule II / table column "Married filing jointly": ovals 2 and 5. The table footnote reads "*This column must also be used by qualifying surviving spouse".
- Schedule III / table column "Head of a household": oval 4.
- QSS (2025 text, p.8): the spouse died in 2023 or 2024, there is a dependent child who lived in the home all year, and so on. For 2024 the years are 2022 or 2023.

### Standard deduction (line 23, p.20; identical in 2024 and 2025)
| Status | Amount |
|---|---|
| Single | $4,400 |
| MFJ | $8,800 |
| MFS | $4,400 |
| HoH | $6,424 |
| QSS | $8,800 |

The text reads: "Hawaii did not adopt the federal provision that increases the standard deduction amounts for tax years 2018 through 2025." There is **no additional standard deduction for age 65 or blindness**. Age 65 is handled through the exemption instead.

**Dependent filer:** "If you can be claimed as a dependent by someone else and you do not itemize … your standard deduction is limited to the greater of $500 or your earned income (up to the full standard deduction for your filing status)." Worksheet lines A–E: E = min(max(earned income, 500), full std deduction). Earned income here means wages plus Schedule 1 lines 3 and 6, less line 15.

### Personal exemptions (lines 6a–6e p.9; line 25 p.20; identical in 2024 and 2025)
- "Residents are allowed **$1,144** for each exemption they can claim. Multiply $1,144 by the total number of exemptions you claimed on line 6e." Hawaii did not adopt the federal suspension of exemptions for 2018–2025. There is no phase-out.
- 6a Yourself: one exemption, plus one more if age 65 or over as of Jan 1 of the following year ("Fill in the oval for 'Age 65 or over'", then "Enter the number of ovals filled on lines 6a and 6b").
- 6b Spouse: one exemption on MFJ if the spouse cannot be claimed as a dependent. On **MFS**, the spouse counts only if the spouse had no income, is not filing, and cannot be claimed as a dependent. Plus the spouse's 65+ oval.
- 6c/6d: one per dependent child or other dependent.
- **Taxpayer who can be claimed as a dependent:** "If you can be claimed as a dependent on another person's tax return, do not fill in the ovals on lines 6a and 6b. Instead, fill in the oval above line 21." Line 25 caution: "If you can be claimed as a dependent on another person's tax return, you may not claim an exemption for yourself." The test is *can be claimed*, whether or not the other person actually claims the filer. So such a filer gets $0 for self and spouse. Their own dependents on 6c/6d are discussed under Ambiguities, item 2.
- Blind, deaf, or totally disabled (Form N-172 certified): a $7,000 Disability Exemption replaces the $1,144 personal exemption, and the filer then cannot take dependent or 65+ exemptions. Maximums: one individual $7,000; with a non-disabled spouse under 65 $8,144; with a non-disabled spouse 65+ $9,288; both disabled $14,000. This is outside the input domain.

### Taxable income and tax (lines 24, 26, 27, p.20)
- Line 24 = HI AGI (line 20) − standard deduction. Line 26 = line 24 − line 25 (exemptions), **but not less than zero**.
- "If your taxable income is less than $100,000, you MUST use the Tax Table on pages 36 through 47." The table uses **$50 intervals** ("At least / But less than"). Taxable income of $100,000 or more must use the Tax Rate Schedules.
- Rounding (p.11): every entry is rounded to whole dollars, with under 50 cents dropped and 50–99 cents rounded up. The rate-schedule result is therefore rounded half-up.
- Structural check, not tax-law evidence: for every row from $50 to $99,950 (5,997 cells per year), the published table value equals the published rate schedule (with its printed, rounded base amounts) evaluated at the interval midpoint (lower bound + $25) and rounded half-up. All cells matched exactly in both years. Using exact cumulative bracket amounts instead of the printed bases matches only 4,216 cells in 2024 and 4,887 in 2025, so the printed bases are what DOTAX used.

## Arithmetic per scenario

The exemption is $1,144 per exemption in both years, and the standard deduction amounts are the same in both years. The 2024 and 2025 scenarios therefore have identical taxable income and differ only in the tax table or schedule.

- **A (Single $50,000, 0 deps):** exemptions = 1 (6a) × 1,144 = 1,144. TI = 50,000 − 4,400 − 1,144 = 44,456. 44,456 falls in [44,450, 44,500). 2024 S/MFS column = 2,936; 2025 = 2,285.
- **B (MFJ $90,000, 2 deps):** exemptions = (6a 1 + 6b 1 + 6c 2) = 4 × 1,144 = 4,576. TI = 90,000 − 8,800 − 4,576 = 76,624, in [76,600, 76,650). 2024 MFJ column = 4,896; 2025 = 3,683.
- **C (HoH $45,000, 1 dep):** exemptions = 2 × 1,144 = 2,288. TI = 45,000 − 6,424 − 2,288 = 36,288, in [36,250, 36,300). 2024 HoH = 2,051; 2025 = 1,308.
- **D (MFS $40,000, 0 deps):** the spouse is assumed to have income or file their own return, so 6b is not allowed. Exemptions = 1 × 1,144. TI = 40,000 − 4,400 − 1,144 = 34,456, in [34,450, 34,500). 2024 S/MFS = 2,150; 2025 = 1,571.
- **E (QSS $70,000, 1 dep):** exemptions = self 1 + child 1 = 2 × 1,144 = 2,288. No 6b, because the spouse died in a prior year (6b applies only to death in the current year). TI = 70,000 − 8,800 − 2,288 = 58,912, in [58,900, 58,950). MFJ column (QSS footnote): 2024 = 3,537; 2025 = 2,461.
- **F (Single $20,000):** TI = 20,000 − 4,400 − 1,144 = 14,456, in [14,450, 14,500). 2024 = 687; 2025 = 292.
- **G (Single $150,000):** TI = 150,000 − 4,400 − 1,144 = 144,456, which is ≥ 100,000, so the rate schedule applies.
  - 2024 Schedule I, bracket 48,000–150,000: 3,214 + 0.0825 × (144,456 − 48,000) = 3,214 + 0.0825 × 96,456 = 3,214 + 7,957.62 = 11,171.62 → **11,172**. See Ambiguities, item 1.
  - 2025 Schedule I, bracket 125,000–175,000: 8,391 + 0.079 × (144,456 − 125,000) = 8,391 + 0.079 × 19,456 = 8,391 + 1,537.024 = 9,928.024 → **9,928**. Using the exact cumulative base 8,391.20 gives 9,928.22, which also rounds to 9,928, so there is no ambiguity in this case.

## Ambiguities / open questions

1. **Rate-schedule base rounding (affects G24 by $1).** The printed base amounts are rounded to whole dollars. For example, the 2024 Schedule I base at $48,000 is printed as $3,214, while the exact cumulative amount is $3,213.60. The instructions tell filers to apply the printed schedule, which gives 11,171.62 → **$11,172**. A computation from exact cumulative brackets gives 11,171.22 → $11,171.
   - I recommend $11,172, because the instructions direct use of the published schedule. The table is also built on the printed bases: all 5,997 cells per year matched.
   - The statute (HRS §235-51) could not be retrieved (Cloudflare 403) to confirm whether it prints the same rounded bases. A test tolerance of ±$1 above $100k would be defensible if the statute differs.
2. **Dependent filer who has dependents of their own.** The instructions bar only lines 6a and 6b. They are silent on whether 6c/6d dependents may still be claimed. Under federal rules (IRC §152(b)(1)), a person who can be claimed as a dependent cannot claim dependents, but N-11 does not say so explicitly. This is untested here.
3. **HI AGI equals wages.** This assumes no Hawaii additions or subtractions apply to plain W-2 wages. Line 20 is derived from federal AGI through Hawaii adjustments, and none apply to ordinary wages. Examples of adjustments that could apply: Hawaii-exempt pension amounts, and HI's non-conformity on items such as HAS treatment, though HI generally conforms for HSAs. Wages that include Hawaii-specific excluded pay would break the assumption, for example National Guard/Reserve pay or certain exempt amounts.
4. **Statute not consulted (S3 blocked).** All parameters rest on the DOTAX instructions alone. I believe the 2025 bracket widening comes from Act 46, SLH 2024, but this is from memory and unverified against the session law. The 2025 instructions still print a $4,400 single standard deduction. If a later standard-deduction increase exists, it starts after 2025 per these instructions.
5. **The MFS spouse exemption depends on facts outside the stated domain.** It is allowed only if the spouse has no income and does not file. D24/D25 assume the spouse has income.
6. **Age 65 is out of domain.** It adds one exemption per 65+ oval, and there is no extra standard deduction. No values are derived for it.

## Parameters re-checked against the primary source

All of the following were confirmed in the implementer's own `pdftotext -layout` extraction
of both instruction books:

- **$1,144 per exemption.** Printed p.20, line 25, "Regular Exemptions" in both years. The
  same section says there is no federal-style suspension. No phase-out appears.
- **Lines 6a and 6b.** Printed p.9. Fill in 6a "if no one can claim you as a dependent".
  6b applies on MFJ if the spouse cannot be claimed as a dependent, and on MFS only if the
  spouse had no income, is not filing, and cannot be claimed as a dependent.
- **Standard deductions.** Printed p.20, line 23: S/MFS $4,400, MFJ/QSS $8,800, HoH $6,424
  in both years.
- **Table rows.** Every row used in the scenario table, plus the Brown example rows, matches
  the extraction exactly in both years.
- **Rate schedules.** The printed p.48 schedules agree with the derivation's transcription.

## Ambiguity 1 resolved: printed bases are statutory; G24 = $11,172

HRS §235-51(c), retrieved 2026-10-07, prints the Single/MFS schedule itself with
whole-dollar base amounts. For tax years beginning after December 31, 2017, it reads "Over
$48,000 but not over $150,000: $3,214.00 plus 8.25% of excess over $48,000". For tax years
beginning after December 31, 2024, it reads "Over $125,000 but not over $175,000: $8,391.00
plus 7.90% of excess over $125,000". The printed bases are therefore the law. They are not
a DOTAX convenience approximation of the exact cumulative amount ($3,213.60 or $8,391.20).

G24 = 3,214 + 0.0825 × 96,456 = 11,171.62, which rounds to **$11,172** under the printed
p.11 whole-dollar rule. This confirms the derivation's recommendation. G25 = 9,928
whichever base is used.

## Ambiguity 2 resolved: a claimable filer claims no dependents either

Printed p.9 in both years, Step 2 question 3 (qualifying child) and Step 3 question 4
(qualifying relative), asks: "Could you, or your spouse if filing jointly, be claimed as a
dependent on someone else's 2024 [2025] tax return? Yes. Stop. You cannot claim any
dependents." Codex flagged this in an independent spot check on 2026-10-07, and the
implementer verified it in both books. The rule applies per person, following printed p.9:

- **The filer is claimable.** Line 6a is barred, so is 6b, and so are dependents. Total
  regular exemptions are $0 (age-65 ovals sit on 6a/6b, so they go too).
- **On MFJ, only the spouse is claimable.** The filer still takes 6a ($1,144), since the
  line 6a test is about the filer alone. Line 6b is barred ("your spouse cannot be claimed as
  a dependent"), and dependents are barred because the question includes "your spouse if
  filing jointly". Total is $1,144 plus the filer's own 65+ oval, not zero.

## Supported domain (implementation)

The graph now derives the mandatory exemption base:
$1,144 × (1 + [MFJ] + `num_dependents`). The explicit `dependent_exemptions` input is the
**total** exemption dollars, and the effective amount is max(base, explicit total). That
rule can only *raise* the total above the base. The fix therefore covers **ordinary
exemptions only**, where nobody on the return is claimable as another's dependent and no
one takes the disability exemption. Two regimes are excluded.

1. **A claimable filer or joint spouse.** This regime lowers the total below the base, as
   described above. The public API has no "can be claimed as a dependent" input, federal or
   state. D1–D3 are preserved here as independent evidence but are not tested.
2. **The disability exemption** (blind, deaf, or totally disabled, certified on Form N-172).
   The printed p.20 text reads: "The Disability Exemption is in lieu of the regular personal
   exemption of $1,144. If you claim the Disability Exemption, you will not be able to claim
   the additional exemptions for your children or other dependents, or for being 65 or
   older." The maximums are $7,000 for one individual; $8,144 or $9,288 with a non-disabled
   spouse; and $14,000 when both are disabled. This is a *replacement* total that can fall
   below the derived base, which max() cannot express. Codex supplied the counterexample:
   Single, wages $100,000, six dependents, $7,000 disability exemption, both years.
   Officially TI = 100,000 − 4,400 − 7,000 = 88,600. The graph uses max(7 × 1,144 = 8,008,
   7,000), giving 87,592. `tests/hi_n11_exemptions_test.py` records it as a strict xfail
   that is labelled outside the supported domain. The limitation is owned by
   tenforty-avr.3.

Within the supported domain, the explicit total still carries the exemptions the inputs
cannot determine: the MFS spouse (no income, not filing) and age 65 or over.

**Signature arithmetic.** The graph prices the schedule from exact cumulative bracket
amounts at the return's own income and does not round. HI requires (a) the table row below
$100,000, which DOTAX built from the printed schedule at the row midpoint and rounded (Part
A, structural check), and (b) the statutory printed bases at $100,000 and above, rounded to
the dollar. The difference graph − expected is therefore:

    rate × (TI − row midpoint) + (exact cumulative base − printed base) ± $0.50   (table rows)
    (exact cumulative base − printed base) + (printed result − rounded result)    (schedule)

Tables below $100,000 belong to tenforty-tj2.20 (state tax tables). The printed-base and
whole-dollar offset at $100,000 and above is a separate schedule-method defect, owned by
tenforty-b72.41 (HI rate schedule whole-dollar bases).

The legacy unverified HI silver cases (Single $60k and $200k 2024, MFJ $100k 2024, HoH $75k
2024, Single $60k 2025) omitted the mandatory exemption, so they are replaced by the derived
cases above as state evidence. The rows themselves are kept, labelled unverified legacy,
for their federal tax and AGI assertions only; their state expectations are retired.

The `tests/regression_test.py` HI range rows now use published table rows that the
implementer looked up non-blind, on 2026-10-07, from the same PDFs: taxable income 54,456
(2024 row 54,450–54,500, S/MFS $3,748; 2025 same row, $3,031) and MFJ 88,912 (2024 row
88,900–88,950, MFJ $5,868). Their windows are the same signature, centered and ±$0.50.
