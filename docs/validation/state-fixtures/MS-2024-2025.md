## Official parameters

### Tax rate

| Year | Rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | "0% on the first $10,000.00 of taxable income and 4.7% on taxable income in excess of $10,000.00" | I24 p.21 ("Tax Rates"). I24 p.3 (HB 531 legislative changes) lists 2024 4.7%, 2025 4.4%, 2026 4%. The Schedule of Tax Computation is on I24 p.26. |
| 2025 | "0% on the first $10,000.00 of taxable income and 4.4% on taxable income in excess of $10,000.00" | I25 p.22 ("Tax Rates"). The Schedule of Tax Computation is on I25 p.27. |

The Schedule of Tax Computation has two lines: "1. First $10,000 x 0%" and "2. Remaining balance x 4.7% / 4.4%". Each line has a Column A (Taxpayer) and a Column B (Spouse). The $10,000 zero band is applied **per column** (I24 p.26 / I25 p.27, Line 1: "Enter the first $10,000 of taxable income or part ($0 - $10,000) in Column A and Column B if applicable"). See the MFJ note below.

The booklet contains **no tax table** and **no worked numeric examples** of the tax computation. Every scenario below is therefore my own arithmetic from the officially stated rate schedule (fixture kind (3)).

\*The table's footnote reads: "The additional $1,500 will be allowed in the calculation of the dependent exemption amount entered on line 10."

Additional exemptions: $1,500 for each dependent and for each age-65-or-over or blind box (I24/I25 p.6: "Each dependent, other than yourself or spouse ... $1,500"; Line 10: "Multiply line 9 by $1,500").

Head of Family requires a dependent. I24 p.5–6 / I25 p.6, Line 4 says: "You must have a dependent of yours living in the home with you for the entire year to file as head of family. By checking line 4 ... you are allowed $8,000 on line 11 and $1,500 for the required dependent listed on line 6 which totals $9,500 for your head of family status exemption."

### Flow from wages to taxable income (Form 80-105)

- Line 13, Mississippi AGI, comes from page 2, line 66. For W-2 wages with no adjustments, MS AGI equals wages.
- Line 14 is the standard deduction (or itemized). Line 15 is the exemption, equal to line 12 = line 10 (additional) + line 11 (filing status). The MFS exemption is divided by 2.
- Line 16, MS taxable income = line 13 − line 14 − line 15 (I25 p.7, Line 16).
- Line 17, tax, is computed with the Schedule of Tax Computation.
- A negative taxable income produces no tax.

### Rounding

I24 p.21 and I25 p.22, "Round to the Nearest Dollar", say: "All dollar amounts should be rounded to the nearest whole dollar (no pennies). Round down ... under $.50 and round up ... $.50 and over. For example, $2.15 becomes $2.00, $4.75 becomes $5.00, and $3.50 becomes $4.00."

The tax on line 17 is therefore the half-up rounded whole-dollar amount. With whole-dollar TI, 4.7% × (TI − 10,000) has at most one decimal place, and 4.4% × (TI − 10,000) also has at most one decimal place, so .50 ties can arise only at odd multiples of 5 cents. The table shows both the unrounded product and the rounded line-17 amount.

### MFJ alternative: combined return with income split between spouses

The Tax Computation Schedule gives a 0% band of $10,000 **per column**. A couple with two earners who file combined and split their income can therefore shelter up to $20,000 at 0%. The booklet notes they "should separate their incomes ... in order to take advantage of the lowest tax rates" (I25 p.6, MS AGI section). Exemption and standard deduction "may be divided ... in any manner they choose".

The kind (3) numbers below assume an equal 50/50 split of wages, exemption, and deduction, so each column's TI is half of the total TI:

| Year | Wages | Each column TI | Tax |

| 2024 | 20,000 | 1,700 | 0 |
| 2024 | 50,000 | 16,700 | 2×6,700×.047 = 629.80 → 630 |

The "one column" MFJ rows are the correct fixture when all wages belong to one spouse, or when a model applies a single $10,000 band to joint TI.

## Spot check of the claim

The claim reads: "HoH standard deduction is 3400 (not 4600); HoH W2 50k -> TI 38600, tax 1258.40 (2025) / 1344.20 (2024)".

- **HoH standard deduction $3,400: CONFIRMED.** It is $3,400 in both years (I24 p.5, I25 p.5). $4,600 is the MFJ amount.
- **TI 38,600 implies an HoH exemption of $8,000** (50,000 − 3,400 − 8,000). That matches the line-11 filing-status exemption exactly (I24/I25 p.5).
- **Tax 1,258.40 (2025) and 1,344.20 (2024): arithmetically CONFIRMED** as unrounded products: 28,600 × 4.4% and 28,600 × 4.7%.
- **Caveat 1, rounding.** The booklet's rounding rule would put $1,258 (2025) and $1,344 (2024) on line 17. The claim's cents are the pre-rounding product.
- **Caveat 2, the scenario.** HoH with zero dependents is not a status the booklet permits. HoH "must have a dependent", and the instructions say HoH status carries $8,000 + $1,500 for the required dependent, for **$9,500 total**. On an actual HoH return the minimum exemption is $9,500, which gives TI 37,100 and tax 1,192.40 → 1,192 (2025) or 1,273.70 → 1,274 (2024). The claim is right only under the literal "HoH box checked, zero dependents entered" reading: line 11 = $8,000 and line 10 = 0. A test harness with "HoH, no dependents" inputs would produce that reading. I report both readings and leave the choice to the consumer.

## Ambiguities and uncertainties

1. **HoH with no dependents** is outside the booklet's rules (see caveat 2). The literal-reading rows are the natural fixture for an input set with zero dependents. The with-dependent rows reflect a legally valid HoH return.
2. **MFJ single $10,000 band versus per-spouse bands.** The official schedule applies the $10,000 0% band per column. The result depends on how income is attributed between spouses, and the scenario leaves that unspecified. I give the one-column figure as primary and the 50/50 split as an alternative.
3. **Rounding.** Line 17 is shown rounded per the general whole-dollar rule. A model that keeps cents would match the "unrounded" column instead.
4. **2025 booklet location.** The 2025 PDF's filename is "80100251 2.pdf". The plain "80100251.pdf" returns 404. I read its content directly: it is headed "Form 80-100-25-1-1-000 (Rev. 12/25)" and "2025".
