## How VT taxable income is computed (both years, I25/I24 p.6–7)

- Line 1: **federal AGI**. VT starts from AGI, not from federal taxable income.
- Line 2: net modifications from Schedule IN-112. These are zero for W-2 wages only.
- Line 3 = Line 1 + Line 2.
- Line 4: the VT standard deduction, which is VT's own amount and not the federal one.
- Line 5e: personal exemptions × the per-exemption amount (yourself + spouse + dependents).
- Line 7 = Line 3 − (Line 4 + Line 5e), with a floor of 0.
- Line 8: if federal AGI ≤ 150,000, tax is on Line 7 **from the tax table or the rate schedule**.
  The schedules say "TAXABLE INCOME UNDER $75,000 USE THE TAX TABLES". If federal AGI >
  150,000, Line 8 = **max(3% × (federal AGI − US-obligation interest), rate-schedule tax on Line 7)**.
- Whole dollars: "Round entries to the nearest whole dollar" (I25/I24 p.3). The worked example on
  the schedule page rounds the marginal product before adding it to the base tax ($135.30 → $135).

## Rounding notes and ambiguity

- **Band semantics:** a table row "At least X, but less than Y" is lower-inclusive. TIs that land exactly on a
  boundary are placed accordingly: 2025 MFJ 4,100 → the 4,100–4,200 row, and 2025 MFJ 64,100 → the 64,100–64,200 row. An
  implementation that uses exact TI × rate instead of the table would give 137 rather than 139 for 2025 MFJ 30k
  (4,100 × .0335 = 137.35), and 2,147 rather than 2,149 for 2025 MFJ 90k. The same applies to 2024 Single 17,500 (586 exact vs 588 table)
  and 2024 HoH 13,800 (462 vs 464). **The table value is the official answer.** An exact-formula engine will
  differ by up to about $2 on these rows.
- **Schedule rounding:** round to the nearest whole dollar. Every derived case here has a fractional part
  well away from .5 except 19,443.125 and 19,656.25, and both of those round down under any convention.
- **Base-tax discontinuity at 75,000:** published bases are rounded, e.g. 2025 MFJ 10,482 against an exact
  10,482.7. Fixtures use the published bases verbatim, so a pure-bracket engine may differ by under $1
  before rounding. Here that would move 2025 MFJ 300k by at most 1 (16,155.85 → 16,156 when using exact
  cumulative brackets from 0). Treat ±1 as an acceptable tolerance for the 300k cases.
- **3% minimum tax** (AGI > 150,000): it does not bind in any scenario here, since 9,000 is well below the schedule tax.
  An engine that omits it still matches these fixtures. A fixture targeting that rule needs a low-TI, high-AGI
  case, which these inputs cannot produce.
- **Credits:** none of VT's automatic credits apply to these inputs. VT EITC keys off the federal EITC,
  which is 0 for childless filers at AGI ≥ 30,000 in both years. There are no dependents, so the child credit
  does not apply. So Line 8 is also the tax before the use tax and other items.
- HoH is assumed to still get only one exemption (no dependents), as the scenario specifies, even though
  HoH status federally generally requires a qualifying person. That is the literal-scenario reading.

### Rule summary (with citations)

### Taxable income (IN-111 Lines 1–7)
- Line 1 = federal AGI (1040 line 11a). Line 2 = net modifications (Schedule IN-112), which are **0** in this domain. Line 3 = Line 1 + Line 2. Sources: F24/F25 p.1; I24/I25 p.6–7.
- Line 4 = VT standard deduction (I24 p.7, I25 p.7; also printed in the filing-status box of F24/F25 p.1):

| Filing status | 2024 | 2025 | Add-on per federal 65+/blind box, 2024 / 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 7,400 | 7,650 | 1,200 / 1,250 (max 2 boxes) |
| MFJ / Qualifying Widow(er) | 14,850 | 15,300 | 1,200 / 1,250 (max 4) |
| MFS | 7,400 | 7,650 | 1,200 / 1,250 (max 4) |
| HoH | 11,100 | 11,450 | 1,200 / 1,250 (max 2) |

  The age test is "born before Jan. 2, 1960" for 2024 and "born before Jan. 2, 1961" for 2025 (I24/I25 p.7 chart). The add-on is not used in this domain (all filers under 65 and not blind).
- **Itemizing:** IN-111 has no itemized-deduction line. Line 4 is always the standard deduction (F24/F25 p.1). Under S5811(21) taxable income is federal AGI less the VT standard deduction and personal exemptions. The only Schedule-A-type item is a medical-expense deduction (S5811(21)(C)(iv)), taken through Schedule IN-112 as a modification. Outside that, VT gives charitable relief as a 5% credit capped at $1,000 (IN-111 lines 11–13), not as an itemized deduction.
- **Line 5 personal exemptions** (I24/I25 p.7; F24/F25 p.1):
  - 5a = 1 for yourself if no one can claim you as a dependent.
  - 5b = 1 for a jointly filed spouse/CU partner if no one can claim them. Per the instructions: "Do not enter '1' if your filing status is Qualifying Widow(er) or Married Filing Separately." **5b is 1 only for MFJ.**
  - 5c = the number of other dependents claimed on federal Form 1040.
  - 5d = 5a + 5b + 5c.
  - 5e = 5d × **$5,100 (2024)** / **$5,300 (2025)**.
- Line 6 = Line 4 + Line 5e. Line 7 = Line 3 − Line 6, floored at 0 (I24/I25 p.7).
- **Filing status** must equal the federal filing status, with two exceptions: civil-union couples, and a spouse without VT nexus (TB-55) (I25 p.6). The table and schedule columns map as follows: Single → Schedule X / Single column. MFJ and QW and CU-joint → Y-1 / "Married filing jointly*" column (footnote "*This column also applies to qualifying widow(er) and civil union filing jointly"). MFS and CU-separate → Y-2 / "Married filing separately**". HoH → Z.

### Line 8 tax
- **Tax table vs schedule** (I24/I25 p.13 schedule banner "TAXABLE INCOME UNDER $75,000 USE THE TAX TABLES"; tables on pp.14–20):
  - Taxable income under $75,000 uses the tax table. The table's intervals are $100 wide ("At least / But less than"), and its columns are, left to right, Single, MFJ*, MFS**, HoH. Every row checked equals the schedule rate applied to the interval midpoint, rounded to the nearest dollar (for example 37,550 × 3.35% = 1,257.9 → 1,258). Exception: the 0–100 row is 0.
  - Taxable income of $75,000 or more uses the rate schedule.
- **Federal AGI > $150,000** (I24/I25 p.7, Line 8; schedule footnote p.13; S5822(a)(6)): Line 8 = the greater of (1) 3% × (federal AGI − interest from U.S. obligations) and (2) the **rate-schedule** tax on Line 7. The statute says "three percent of the taxpayer's federal adjusted gross income". The form subtracts U.S.-obligation interest, which is 0 in this domain. **The test is strictly "greater than": AGI of exactly $150,000 does not trigger it.**
  - Does it bind for ordinary wage earners? For W-2-only filers taking the standard deduction it does not bind in any scenario here. Just above the threshold, the schedule tax is about 5% of AGI (for example Single 2025 with AGI $150,001 pays about $7,614), well above 3%. The minimum can bind only when Line 2 subtractions or very large exemption counts push Line 7 far below AGI, and neither happens in this domain.
- **Rounding:** "Round entries to the nearest whole dollar. The cents are preprinted with zeroes." (I24/I25 p.3, "Use Whole Dollars"). Schedule results are rounded to the nearest dollar. See the published examples below ($135.30 → $135).

### Ambiguities / open questions

1. **Schedule rounding convention.** The instructions require whole dollars (I24/I25 p.3), and both published examples round $135.30 → $135 and $165.00 → $165. Scenarios 8 and 15 rely on ordinary half-up rounding of .6 → up. No example rounds at exactly .5. Suggested tolerance for schedule cases: ±$1 at most, and $0 is expected.
2. **2024 Single schedule base at 75,000 (3,393)** does not equal round(1,605 + 0.066 × 27,100) = round(3,393.6) = 3,394. VT appears to build bases from unrounded bracket endpoints, or to truncate. This does not affect any scenario here, because a scenario uses the printed base and the TI ≥ 75,000 rows. It does mean a pure rate-formula implementation can differ by $1 from the printed schedule. Fixtures should follow the printed base amounts.
3. **The table is midpoint-based.** At TI < 75,000 a formula implementation (TI × rate) will differ from the table by up to about ±$2 within a $100 interval. Line 8 legally uses the table below $75,000 (except when AGI > $150,000, where the instructions say "rate schedule"). Expected values here are the table values exactly.
4. **AGI > $150,000 with TI < $75,000** (possible only with large modifications): the instructions direct the *rate schedule*, not the table. This is outside the domain.
5. **Minimum-tax base:** the form uses "3% of federal AGI less interest from U.S. obligations". S5822(a)(6) as summarized says "three percent of federal AGI". The difference is irrelevant in this domain (no Treasury interest).
6. **5b for MFS / QW** is explicitly 0 per I24/I25 p.7, even though the federal MFS rules allow a spouse exemption in some cases. QW gets 5a = 1 plus dependents only.
7. **Statutory base amounts** in S5811(21) (SD $6,000/$9,000/$12,000, exemption $4,150) are pre-indexing. The yearly figures used above come from the Department's forms and instructions, which apply the § 5811(21) and § 5822(b) CPI indexing. The Department's CPI computation was not independently re-derived.
8. Only selected tax-table rows (those used above) were transcribed. The full tables are I24/I25 pp.14–20.
