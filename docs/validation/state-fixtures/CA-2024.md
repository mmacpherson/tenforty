### Personal exemption credit (Form 540 Side 1, line 7; booklet p. 12)

- Line 7: enter 1 in the box if filing status 1, 3, or 4 (Single, MFS, HoH); 2 if status 2 or 5 (MFJ, QSS);
  multiply by **$149**. So Single = $149, HoH = $149, MFJ = $298.
- Blind (line 8) and senior (line 9) are each $149 per person too. They do not apply here (under 65, not blind). No dependents, so line 10 does not apply either.
- These are **credits against tax** (line 32), not deductions from income. Line 33 = line 31 − line 32, "If less than zero, enter -0-", so they are nonrefundable.
- **AGI phase-out** (booklet p. 14, line 32 instructions + AGI Limitation Worksheet). The worksheet applies when federal AGI (line 13) exceeds:
  - Single/MFS $244,857; MFJ/QSS $489,719; HoH $367,291.
  - Worksheet: c = AGI − threshold; d = c / $2,500 ($1,250 MFS), **rounded up to the next whole number**;
    e = d × $6; f = count of the line 7/8/9 boxes; g = e × f; i = max(0, (lines 7+8+9 dollars) − g). Dependent credits (line 10) are reduced the same way (lines j–m). "Use whole dollars only."

### Tax method and rounding (booklet p. 13, line 31; p. 75)

- **Taxable income ≤ $100,000 → the Tax Table must be used** (pp. 69–74, $100 brackets; the table value is the tax at the bracket midpoint, rounded to whole dollars).
- **Taxable income > $100,000 → the Tax Rate Schedule** (p. 75).
- Rate-schedule rounding (p. 75, "How to Figure Tax", Step 4): round the product (excess × rate) to two decimals, add the base amount, then round the result to whole dollars for line 31. Form 540 is filed in **whole dollars** ("Whole dollars only"). I used half-up rounding to the dollar. None of the cases here lands on exactly $0.50, so the choice of rounding rule does not matter.

### Arithmetic

Tax-table cases (cross-checked against the rate schedule at the $100 bracket midpoint):
- Single TI 24,460 → row 24,451–24,550, midpoint 24,500: 107.56 + 0.02 × (24,500 − 10,756) = 107.56 + 274.88 = 382.44 → **382**. This matches the table. (Exact TI would give 381.64, which also rounds to 382.)
- MFJ/HoH TI 18,920 → row 18,851–18,950, midpoint 18,900: 0.01 × 18,900 = **189**. This matches both columns.

Rate-schedule cases:
- Single 120,000: TI = 120,000 − 5,540 = 114,460. Schedule X band 70,606–360,659: 114,460 − 70,606 = 43,854; × 0.093 = 4,078.422 → 4,078.42; + 3,108.72 = 7,187.14 → **7,187**. AGI 120,000 ≤ 244,857, so the full credit of 149 applies. 7,187 − 149 = **7,038**.
- Single 600,000: TI = 594,460. X band 432,787–721,314: 161,673 × 0.113 = 18,269.049 → 18,269.05; + 37,512.83 = 55,781.88 → **55,782**. Phase-out: c = 600,000 − 244,857 = 355,143; d = 355,143 / 2,500 = 142.06 → 143; e = 858; f = 1; g = 858; i = max(0, 149 − 858) = **0**. Net 55,782.
- MFJ 120,000: TI = 120,000 − 11,080 = 108,920. Y band 80,490–111,732: 28,430 × 0.06 = 1,705.80; + 1,984.52 = 3,690.32 → **3,690**. Credit 2 × 149 = 298 (AGI ≤ 489,719). Net 3,392.

## Spot check: "official CA 2024 Single W2 120000 net state tax 7038.14"

**Refuted as stated, though the underlying calculation is correct.** 7,038.14 is the unrounded
arithmetic: 7,187.14 − 149. The official form procedure (p. 75, Step 4, and "Whole dollars only" on Form 540) rounds line 31 to **$7,187** before subtracting the credit. The official net tax is therefore **$7,038**. The claim is off only because it skipped the whole-dollar rounding (by $0.14). A tolerance of ±$1 would accept it; an exact whole-dollar comparison against the form would not.

## Notes, ambiguities, and uncertainty

1. **Tax-table cutoff.** The instructions require the table at TI ≤ $100,000. Table values are midpoint-based, so they can differ from the exact rate-schedule arithmetic by up to about ±$2 in the 4% band (up to ±$0.50 × rate × 100). Here they agree after rounding: Single 381.64 exact vs 382 table; 189.20 vs 189.
2. **Whole-dollar convention.** The booklet's own rate-schedule example rounds 4,920.48 down to 4,920, which confirms rounding to the dollar. It does not state explicitly whether $0.50 rounds up. That doesn't matter for these cases.
3. **Rounding the product to cents (Step 4)** comes before adding the base. That can change the whole-dollar result only when the fraction sits near .5. It doesn't matter here.
4. **Schedule P trigger at $600k.** Box C (p. 14) sends filers with CA AGI (line 17) over $337,678 (Single/HoH) or $450,238 (MFJ) to Schedule P (540). I checked that AMT is zero. Schedule P line 1 adds back the standard deduction, so AMTI = TI + std deduction = $600,000. Even before the exemption, TMT is at most 7% × 600,000 = $42,000. That is below every regular tax here ($47,854 / $50,330 / $55,782), so AMT = 0. The exemption credits are already $0 after the phase-out, so the TMT credit limitation has nothing to act on. Net figures stand.
5. **Nonrefundable renter's credit** (line 46) could apply to low-AGI renters, such as the 30,000 cases. The scenarios specify no credits other than exemption credits, so it is excluded.
6. **Page attribution.** I derived page numbers from the extracted PDF's printed footers: std deduction p. 13; line 7 instructions p. 12; line 31 method p. 13; line 32 phase-out worksheet and Box C p. 14; tax table pp. 69–74 (the 18,851 row on p. 69, the 24,451 row on p. 70); rate schedules p. 75.
