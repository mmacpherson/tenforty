### Lines pinned

| Runner quantity | Graph node | MI-1040 line | Record column |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `state_taxable_income` | `L13_mi_taxable_income` | Line 16, taxable income | "MI taxable income (line 16)" |
| `state_total_tax` | `L18_mi_total_tax` | Line 17 less nonrefundable credits, plus use tax | "MI tax (line 17)" |

The spec numbers its lines differently from the 2024 and 2025 form: its L13 is
form line 16 and its L14 is form line 17. Its L18 is tax less nonrefundable
credits plus use tax, which is no single form line. The pinned returns have no
credit for tax paid elsewhere, no historic preservation credit and no use tax,
so the spec's L18 is line 17 on each of them. The record does not derive those
later lines; it states line 17 only.

### Permitted alternatives

None. The book fixes the rounding (page 5, "Completing Michigan Forms": "Round
down amounts of 49 cents or less. Round up amounts of 50 cents or more"), so
the five pinned returns whose line 16 × 4.25% lands on exactly 50 cents (2025
S40, 2024 S85, 2025 S150, 2025 S60-ltcg10, 2025 MFJ250-3dep) have one value
each. Michigan publishes no tax table.

### Not pinned

Scenario 7, both years (`MI-2024-S50-age67`, `MI-2025-S50-age67`): Single, age
67, wages $50,000, record 30,000 / 1,275 in both years. The result rests on the
Tier 3 Michigan Standard Deduction (Schedule 1 line 26), which needs a birth
date inside the Tier 3 window and age 67 by year end. `filer_65_or_older`
carries neither fact, and no public input reaches the Schedule 1 subtractions
(MI-1040 line 13).

### Comparison with the graph (2026-11-25, after the record was committed)

Every pinned return departs, by one mechanism.

- `tenforty-s48h.45` (existing): the graph applies no exemptions. The spec
  takes MI-1040 line 15 as a raw input (`L12`, `total_exemptions`, in
  `tenforty-spec/forms/MI1040_2024.hs` and `MI1040_2025.hs`), and the Michigan
  entry in `src/tenforty/mappings.py` maps no public input to it
  (`natural_to_node={}`), so it stays zero whatever the filing status and
  `num_dependents`; `dependent_exemptions` is refused. Taxable income equals
  federal AGI. The record is right: line 9a of the form face gives $5,600
  (2024) or $5,800 (2025) for the filer, the spouse on a joint return and each
  dependent, line 15 carries that total, and line 16 subtracts it.
  - Taxable income: high by exactly the record's line 15, which is the record's
    line 9a count × the year's amount ($5,600 to $29,000 across the cases).
    Signature exact.
  - Tax: high by 4.25% of that amount, ± $0.50 (plus the runner's half-cent
    tolerance). The half dollar is the book's whole-dollar rule: the graph also
    multiplies without rounding (spec `L14`), so the record's rounded line 17
    and the unrounded product differ by at most 50 cents. That part is the
    `tenforty-b72.42` mechanism; it has no signature of its own here because
    every case already departs under `tenforty-s48h.45`, and a quantity carries
    one signature.
- No exact matches.

Once exemptions are applied the taxable-income signatures will fail as "matches
now". The tax signatures on the five half-dollar returns will then need
`tenforty-b72.42` signatures (± $0.50) unless the graph also rounds line 17.

Every departure is a strict, bounded signature in `DEFECTS`: the test fails if
the graph leaves the bound, and also if it starts matching. Bounds come from
the mechanism and the record's own figures (the line 9a count, the form's
per-exemption amount, the 4.25% rate, the whole-dollar rule), never from the
observed residual. MI stays out of graph scope (`tenforty.graph_scope`) while
`tenforty-s48h.45` is open.
