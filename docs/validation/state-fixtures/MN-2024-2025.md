### Quantities

The graph reports Form M1 **line 9** as `state_taxable_income` and **line 10**
(tax from the table or rate schedules) as `state_total_tax`
(`src/tenforty/mappings.py`, `OTSState.MN`; `tenforty-spec/forms/MNFormM1_2024.hs`
and `_2025.hs`, outputs `L9` and `L10`). The record's summary column is line 12
(line 10 plus the line 11 alternative minimum tax). Its per-scenario tables
give line 10 on its own row and show line 11 as 0 in every scenario, so the
pinned tax is the record's line 10, equal to its line 12 column. The graph has
no line 11, 12 or 13; a return that owes Minnesota AMT could not be pinned on
this output.

### Permitted alternatives

One. 2025 scenario 9 (MFJ, $300,000): Worksheet A step 3 is 1,831.50 and the
booklet gives only the general nearest-dollar rule, without saying whether
worksheet steps or only form entries are rounded (record, Uncertainties 1).
Line 9 is 261,531 (round the line 4 entry; primary) or 261,532 (round each
step); line 10 is 17,853 either way. Both are listed as whole outcomes.

Everywhere else the source fixes the rule: the table is mandatory below
$90,000 (2024) / $86,800 (2025), rows are "at least ... but less than" (so the
three 2025 cases exactly on a row boundary have one row), and schedule results
round to the nearest dollar with .50 up.

### Not pinned

None. Dependents are expressed with `num_dependents` and age with
`filer_65_or_older`; the graph ignores both for Minnesota, which is a signed
defect below, not a missing input.

### Comparison with the graph (2026-11-25, after the record was committed)

No case matches outright: Minnesota taxable income matches in the 10 returns
with no dependents, no age box and AGI below the limitation threshold
(scenarios 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8, both years), and
every tax carries at least the table or whole-dollar departure.

- `tenforty-tj2.20`: below the cutoff the form requires the tax table row; the
  graph prices the rate schedule at line 9 itself (`bracketTax` on `L9`,
  `MNFormM1_2024.hs` lines 61-63). A row is the schedule at the row midpoint to
  the nearest dollar, so the bound is ± (marginal rate × $50 + $0.50): ± $3.175
  in the 5.35% bracket, ± $3.90 in the 6.80% bracket. Scenarios 1, 2, 4 and 8,
  both years. Typical size: $1.60 low (2024 Single $85,000); $3.69 low on 2025
  scenario 4, which sits on a row floor.
- `tenforty-b72.42`: at or above the cutoff line 10 is a whole-dollar entry
  ("129.50 becomes 130"); the graph keeps the cents. Bound ± $0.50. Scenario 3,
  both years (9,078.4175 against 9,078; 9,005.77 against 9,006).
- `tenforty-s48h.59` (existing): the graph has no route from `num_dependents`
  to line 5 (`L5` is a raw `keyInput`, unmapped in `mappings.py`) and its
  standard deduction is `byStatusE` on filing status alone (`MNFormM1_2024.hs`
  lines 33-36), so the 65-or-older box adds nothing. Taxable income is high by
  exactly the omitted subtraction, taken from the record: $5,050 (2024) /
  $5,200 (2025) per dependent, and $1,950 / $2,000 for one age box
  (16,525 − 14,575; 16,950 − 14,950). Signature exact on taxable income. Tax is
  high by the omitted amount × the marginal rate, with the table or
  whole-dollar slack above added on each side; the record's rate schedules
  show the graph's higher line 9 stays in the same bracket in every case.
  Scenarios 5, 6, 7 and 9, both years. Typical size: $687 of tax on MFJ
  $100,000 with two dependents (2024), $134 on the age-67 return.
- `tenforty-b72.70` (fixed): the 2024 head of household standard deduction was
  21,862.50 in the graph (1.5 x 14,575) where the 2024 booklet's Standard
  Deduction Table for Line 4 prints 21,900 (record, I24 p.12). `TablesMN2024.hs`
  now carries 21,900, and 2024 scenario 6 is signed only for the omitted $5,050
  exemption under `tenforty-s48h.59` (exact 5,050 on taxable income).
- `tenforty-b72.71` (new): the graph does not
  reduce the standard deduction when line 1 exceeds $232,500 (2024) / $238,950
  (2025) (Worksheet A for Line 4; `TablesMN2025.hs` lines 50-51 say so). In
  scenario 9 the record's reduction is $2,025 (2024) and $1,831 (2025, primary
  rounding), which lowers the graph's taxable income while the missing
  exemptions raise it; the one signature carries the net, exact: +8,075 (2024)
  and +8,569 (2025), tax + net × 7.85% ± $0.50. On its own the limitation is
  worth $159 (2024) and $144 (2025) of tax on this return.

Every departure is a strict, bounded signature in the module's `DEFECTS`: the
test fails if the graph leaves the bound, and also if it starts matching.
Bounds come from each defect's mechanism and the record's own figures (the
omitted subtraction, the bracket rate, the $100 row, the whole-dollar rule),
never from the observed residual. A `GraphDefect` names one bead, so the
compound case (scenario 9 in both years) is signed under `tenforty-s48h.59`
with the second mechanism named in the reason; fixing either mechanism alone
moves the departure out of its bound and forces the signature to be rewritten. MN stays out of graph scope (`tenforty.graph_scope`) while any
of these beads is open.
