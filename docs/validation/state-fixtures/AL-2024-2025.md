### Which lines are pinned

The graph reports its `L14` as `state_taxable_income` and `L15` as
`state_total_tax` (`src/tenforty/mappings.py`, AL outputs). The graph numbers
its lines differently from the printed form. The values are the record's "AL
taxable (line 16)" and "AL tax (line 17)" columns: taxable income after the
standard deduction, federal income tax deduction and exemptions, and the tax
on it before credits. Every record scenario has both columns.

### Inputs

`w2_income`, `long_term_capital_gains`, `num_dependents` and
`filer_65_or_older`; Head of family (s7) is the HoH filing status. The record
could not know the children's ages (its Uncertainties 1), so each of s6 and s7
has two cases: the headline reading (under 17, the `num_ctc_dependents`
default) and the record's variant (`num_ctc_dependents=0`, children 17 or
older, $500 credit for other dependents), labelled `s6b` and `s7b`. The
variants have their own record values.

### Permitted alternatives

None. The Tax Table is mandatory ("You must figure your tax from the Tax
Tables") and the whole-dollar rounding rule is printed, so each case has one
outcome. The over-$100,000 worksheet applies to s4 only.

### Not pinned

None.

### Comparison with the graph (2026-11-25, after the record was committed)

- `tenforty-s48h.47`: the graph's taxable income equals income. Form 40 line
  8 is federal total income, the standard deduction is reachable only through
  `state_adjustment` (`tenforty-yu51`, which holds AL out of graph scope), and
  the federal income tax deduction, personal exemption and dependent
  exemption are not in the spec at all
  (`tenforty-spec/forms/ALForm40_2024.hs` and `ALForm40_2025.hs`, lines
  22-49). The graph's taxable income is therefore high by exactly the record's
  total deductions, income less the record's line 16 (for example $5,043 in
  s1 2024). The signature is that amount, to within a cent. Signed on all 22
  returns.
- The graph's tax is the bracket schedule on that too-high income (2% of the
  first $500, 4% to $3,000, 5% above; $1,000, $6,000 joint), at 5% for every
  return here. Its departure from the record's tax is the rate on the omitted
  deductions, 5% of the same amount, plus how the record prices the rest:
  - Table returns (income under $100,000): the record reads a $100 row, which
    is the schedule at the row midpoint rounded to the dollar, and the graph
    prices the schedule exactly (the `tenforty-tj2.20` mechanism, with the
    `tenforty-b72.42` cents). Slack is rate times half the row width plus half
    a dollar: 5% x $50 + $0.50 = $3.00 each way.
  - s4 (over $100,000, worksheet): the record is the worksheet, $4,958.00 plus
    5% of the excess over $100,000, to the whole dollar. The schedule at
    $100,000 is $10 + $100 + $4,850 = $4,960, $2 above the worksheet's base,
    so the graph is $2 higher still, with $0.50 for whole-dollar rounding. The
    MFJ worksheet base ($4,918) differs from its schedule ($4,920) by the same
    $2, but no MFJ return here is over $100,000.
  Signed on all 22 returns under `tenforty-s48h.47`; no other bead is
  assigned for the table and worksheet pricing, because it is dominated by the
  omitted deductions until those are fixed.
- Federal 1040 line 9 income (wages plus the long-term gain) is the graph's
  line 8 and matches the record's line 10 in every return.

Every departure is a strict, bounded signature in the module's `DEFECTS`: the
test fails if the graph leaves the bound, and also if it starts matching.
Bounds come from each mechanism and the record's own figures, never from the
observed residual.

### Doubtful points in the record

- The Alabama worksheet base ($4,958 / $4,918) is two dollars below the
  schedule at $100,000; the record takes the worksheet as printed, which is the
  mandatory method.
- Scenarios 6 and 7 depend on children's ages the brief did not give; both
  readings are pinned.
- Table rows were read from the PDF text layer (Uncertainties 5).
