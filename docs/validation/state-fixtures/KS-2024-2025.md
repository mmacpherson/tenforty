## 2. Adjudication of the open questions

**A3: does federal QSS (filing as KS Head of Household) get the $2,320 HoH extra?
Decision: yes, following the form (scenario 5a, $2,704).** The K-40 directs a
federal Qualifying Widow(er) to "check the Head of Household box" (IP24/IP25 p.6).
The form's exemption block then says "If Filing Status above is Head of Household,
enter $2,320" (K-4024/K-4025 front page), and the instructions say the same about
"your filing status" (p.6). K.S.A. 79-32,121b(b)(1) words the extra as "filing a
federal income tax return under the status of head of household, as defined in 26
U.S.C. § 2(b)". QSS is defined in § 2(a), so the statute read alone would deny it
(scenario 5b, $2,832; taxable income $2,320 higher). We follow the form because it
is what KDOR administers and what a filer completes. Scenario 5a is therefore an
**instruction-method** value, not an uncontested tax-law value: the QW→HoH instruction makes
the filer's Kansas status HoH, and the form keys the extra to that status. Notice
25-07 calls HB 2231 "codifying" an allowance KDOR already gave on the form. This
choice should be revisited if KDOR publishes guidance to the contrary. The
standard deduction ($6,180), the $9,160 base and the rate schedule (K.S.A.
79-32,110(a)(2), "all other individuals") are the same for QW under either reading.

**A1: worksheet $87/$175 vs statute $87.40/$174.80. Decision: the expected value
is the worksheet's whole-dollar line 8, labelled by that method.** K-40 line 8
instructs the worksheet above $100,000 and whole dollars. Method by method:
scenario 7 is $7,571 by the worksheet ($7,570.71 before rounding) and $7,570 by
the statute ($7,570.31). Scenario 8 is $11,905 by both ($11,904.58 worksheet,
$11,904.78 statute). The graph prices the unrounded statute. This is a precision
difference, not a table-method one, and is recorded as the strict known defect
`ks_worksheet_gap`, owned by **tenforty-b72.42** (state whole-dollar
rounding). Its center is (statute − worksheet: −$0.40 Single/HoH/MFS, +$0.20
MFJ) + (unrounded − whole-dollar worksheet), and its width is ±$0.01. Both terms
come from the published figures above: −0.687 for scenario 7, −0.216 for
scenario 8.

**Table rows (≤ $100,000).** The Tax Table governs, and the graph prices the
smooth schedule at actual income. The booklets publish no rule for how the rows
were built, so the `ks_table_gap` bounds claim nothing universal. They hold for
these six fixtures only. For each named cell, the statutory schedule at the row
midpoint (row_low + 25.5) differs from the published amount by at most $0.50.
The deriver checked this (section 3, Arithmetic), and Codex confirmed it for
each named cell. With one marginal rate across each row, graph minus table is
therefore rate × (TI − midpoint) ± $0.50. Owner: tenforty-tj2.20.

The worksheet note in section 3 (A1, "±$1 tolerance") is the deriver's original
suggestion. It is superseded by the ±$0.01 `ks_worksheet_gap` signature above
(tenforty-b72.42).

The taxable-income values (line 7), which are what tenforty-b72.34 fixes, are
asserted exactly in `tests/ks_k40_test.py`.

**Input contract.** `dependent_exemptions` is the TOTAL line-5 allowance,
including the status base and HoH extra. The graph's line 5 is max(derived base,
explicit total). Line 5 is a single sum of fixed amounts, so the agreed
compatibility rule fits Kansas. Per Mike's decision (tenforty-aqx.4.1.6), KS does
not map `num_dependents`, and a nonzero count raises rather than being silently
dropped. Callers with dependents therefore pass base + $2,320 × dependents. A
filer who can be claimed as another taxpayer's dependent still gets the status
base. The booklets change only the dependents count ("If you are claimed as a
dependent by another taxpayer, enter '0' in the number of dependents box", IP24
p.6), so there is no claimable-filer exclusion. The disabled-veteran and (2025)
newborn/stillbirth exemptions are reachable only through the explicit total.

**Legacy rows.** The legacy silver rows KS Single $50,000 and HoH $60,000 (2024)
keep their federal tax and AGI checks as unverified legacy. Their state amounts
were retired: the HoH amount omitted the $2,320 HoH extra, and the Single amount
is superseded by blind case 1.

##### Taxable income (line 7)
Line 7 = line 3 (KAGI) − line 4 − line 5, and 0 if negative (IP24 p.7, IP25 p.7). For this domain, KAGI = federal AGI = wages (no Schedule S modifications).

##### Tax (line 8)
- **Line 7 ≤ $100,000: you must use the Tax Table** (IP24 p.7: "If line 7 is $100,000 or less, use the Tax Tables..."; same in IP25 p.7). The table uses $50 brackets ("at least X+1, but not more than X+50"). There are two columns: "Single, Head of Household or Married Filing Separate" and "Married Filing Joint" (IP24 pp.27–34, IP25 pp.27–34). The first printed row is 26–50. All rows I spot-checked equal the statutory formula at the bracket midpoint, rounded to the nearest dollar.
- **Line 7 > $100,000: use the Tax Computation Worksheet** (IP24 p.34, IP25 p.34):
  - MFJ: $0–$46,000: TI × 0.052 − $0. $46,001 and over: TI × 0.0558 − **$175**.
  - Single/HoH/MFS: $0–$23,000: TI × 0.052 − $0. $23,001 and over: TI × 0.0558 − **$87**.
- The statute, KSA110(a)(1)(B) and (a)(2)(B), for TY2024 and later:
  - MFJ: 5.2% up to $46,000, then $2,392 + 5.58% of the excess. This equals 0.0558·TI − **174.80**.
  - Others: 5.2% up to $23,000, then $1,196 + 5.58% of the excess. This equals 0.0558·TI − **87.40**.
  - The worksheet's subtraction amounts are rounded versions of these, which makes up to a $1 difference (see Ambiguities).
- Amounts are entered in whole dollars (K-40 p.2: "ENTER AMOUNTS IN WHOLE DOLLARS ONLY"). The booklet does not state the rounding rule; I assume nearest dollar, half up.
- **Mid-2024 law change:** SB 1 (2024 Special Session) was signed in June 2024 and applies retroactively to TY2024 (N24-08). It replaced the 2023 three-bracket schedule (3.1/5.25/5.7%) and the $2,250 exemption. The 2024 booklet already reflects SB 1.
- **2025:** Rates are unchanged. KSA110c (revenue trigger) makes its first determination on Aug 15, 2025, effective for the "next tax year". The IP25 worksheet still shows 5.2%/5.58%, so 2025 is unaffected.

#### Ambiguities / open questions

- **A1 — Worksheet vs. statute above $100k (up to $1).** The worksheet subtracts $87 (Single/HoH/MFS) and $175 (MFJ). The exact statutory constants are $87.40 and $174.80. For Single/HoH/MFS above $100k, the worksheet result is $0.40 higher than the statute. It rounds to $1 more whenever the fractional part of 0.0558·TI is in [0.10, 0.50); scenario 7 is such a case (7,571 vs 7,570). For MFJ the worksheet is $0.20 lower, and it rounds to $1 less when the fraction is in [0.50, 0.70). I recommend the worksheet value, since it is what the K-40 instructs filers to compute, with a ±$1 tolerance or an explicit note. The table rows below $100k match the statute at the bracket midpoint.
- **A2 — Rounding of line 8 is not stated.** The form says "whole dollars only" but gives no rounding rule. Nearest dollar is assumed. Scenarios 7 and 8 are unaffected by the half-up vs. half-even choice.
- **A3 — QSS and the HoH extra exemption.** The booklet maps federal QSS to the KS HoH box, and both the booklet and the form give the $2,320 extra to anyone whose KS "filing status is Head of Household". The statute, KSA121b(b)(1), keys the extra to an individual filing federally "under the status of head of household, as defined in 26 U.S.C. § 2(b)". QSS is defined in § 2(a), not § 2(b), so a strict statutory reading would deny the extra to QSS filers. I recommend 5a (2,704), following the booklet and form mechanics, and recording 5b (2,832) as the statutory alternative.
- **A4 — Legal timing of the 2024 HoH extra.** HB 2231 codified the HoH extra in 2025, retroactive to TY2024 (N25-07, dated Nov 19, 2025). Notice 25-07 calls this "codifying" the extra exemption, which suggests KDOR already allowed it administratively. The IP24 booklet as currently posted (last modified 2025-03-14) already includes it. I could not establish whether the very first printing of the 2024 booklet (Sept 2024) had it. Either way, the operative 2024 answer includes it.
- **A5 — Two versions of the exemption statute.** The exemption statute exists in two versions: 79-32,121 (HB 2062: newborn/stillbirth; still shows the disabled veteran amount as $2,250) and 79-32,121b (HB 2231: HoH extra; disabled veteran $2,320 for 2025). The revisor notes the section was amended twice in 2025. The IP25 booklet applies both: newborn $2,320 and disabled veteran $2,320. Neither affects these scenarios.
- **A6 — MFS standard deduction.** KSA119(c)(2) lists only "married filing status, $8,240". The $4,120 for MFS comes from the booklet (IP24/IP25 p.2, p.6), not from explicit statute text. It is consistent with the MFS filing threshold of $13,280.
- **A7 — Stale page references in IP24.** IP24 p.7 refers to tables "beginning on page 21" and the worksheet "on page 28"; the actual pages are 27 and 34. This has no effect on values.
- Out of scope but noted: a dependent who is 18 or older does not count toward the food sales tax credit, and the HoH extra exemption is not counted for that credit (IP24 p.6). This affects line 18 credits, not line 8 tax.
