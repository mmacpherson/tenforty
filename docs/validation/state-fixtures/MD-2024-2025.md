Graph limits and known defects, recorded as strict known-defect signatures:

- **Below $100k of taxable income** Line 21 is the state table row. The graph prices the schedule (tenforty-tj2.20). The signature is 4.75% × (graph taxable income − row midpoint) ± $0.50.
- **2024 Joint/HoH/QSS standard deduction.** Worksheet 16A, Worksheet 2 (B24 PDF p. 20) gives $3,650 at $24,333 or less, 15% from $24,334 to $36,332, and $5,450 at $36,333 or more. The graph applies the Single bounds ($1,800/$2,700) to every status, so taxable income on these returns is $2,750 too high. This is an adjacent defect outside b72.35; it is tracked as **tenforty-b72.45**. The 2025 flat $3,350/$6,700 and the 2025 brackets above $500k/$600k match the booklet.
- **Dependents** have no count input on the graph. `dependent_exemptions` is the Line 19 TOTAL in dollars, after the Chart 10A reduction and including any $1,000 age/blind exemptions. The graph takes max(derived taxpayer/spouse base, explicit total).
- **Dependent taxpayer (status 6, $0 exemption) is outside the supported domain.** Anyone who can be claimed as a dependent gets $0 in Exemption Box A (Instruction 7 chart, B24/B25 PDF p. 12). The graph has no such status, and the `max(base, total)` rule cannot express a total below the base, so a claimable filer would wrongly receive the taxpayer base. The `dependent_exemptions` field documentation states this limit.
- **`num_dependents` is not mapped for MD**, by design (tenforty-aqx.4.1.6): passing it to the graph raises `NotImplementedError` instead of silently dropping it federally. Dependents reach Line 19 only through the explicit `dependent_exemptions` total.

## Rule summary

### Filing status mapping (B24/B25 Instruction 7 chart, PDF p. 12; F25 filing status block)
- Federal Single → MD 1 Single. Federal MFJ → MD 2 Joint. Federal MFS → MD 3 Married filing separately. Federal HoH → MD 4 Head of household. Federal QSS → MD 5 "Qualifying surviving spouse with dependent child". Anyone who can be claimed as a dependent → MD 6 Dependent taxpayer (0 in Exemption Box A).
- Rate schedule: Schedule I = Single, MFS, Dependent (and fiduciaries). Schedule II = Joint, HoH, QSS (B24 PDF p. 21 and p. 40; B25 PDF p. 22 and p. 41).
- Exemption chart and standard deduction: MFS uses the Single column; HoH and QSS use the Joint column (chart PDF p. 12; std. deduction B24 PDF p. 20 Worksheet 16A, B25 PDF p. 21).

### Personal and dependent exemptions (Exemption Amount Chart 10A; B24 PDF p. 12, B25 PDF p. 12; identical text both years)
> "The personal exemption is $3,200. This exemption is reduced once the taxpayer's federal adjusted gross income exceeds $100,000 ($150,000 if filing Joint, Head of Household, or Qualifying Surviving Spouse with Dependent Child). This reduction applies to the additional dependency exemptions as well; however, it does not apply to the taxpayer's age or blindness exemption of $1,000."

- Count: Box A yourself (+ spouse on a joint return), Box B 65+/blind at $1,000 each (not reduced), Box C dependents (Form 502B line 3). Total goes on Line 19 (F25 Exemptions block A–D; B25 Instruction 10).
- The tier test uses **federal** AGI (Form 502 Line 1), not MD AGI.

#### Exemption tier table — per exemption (TY2024 and TY2025 identical)

|---|---|---|---|
| $100,000 or less | $3,200 | $3,200 | $0 |
| over $100,000 to $125,000 | $1,600 | $3,200 | $0 |

| over $150,000 to $175,000 | $0 | $1,600 | $0 |

(Published table rows: B24 PDF p. 12 and B25 PDF p. 12. The bands are "Over X / But not over Y", so an AGI of exactly $125,000 gets $1,600 Single.)

### Standard deduction
- **TY2024** (B24 Instruction 16, PDF p. 21; Worksheet 16A, PDF p. 20): 15% of MD AGI (Line 16), with a minimum and maximum.
  - Single / MFS / Dependent (Worksheet 1): $12,000 or less gives $1,800. $12,001–$17,999 gives 15% × income. $18,000 or over gives $2,700.
  - Joint / HoH / QSS (Worksheet 2): $24,333 or less gives $3,650. $24,334–$36,332 gives 15% × income. $36,333 or over gives $5,450.
- **TY2025** (B25 Instruction 16, PDF p. 21): flat, with no percentage.
  > "If your filing status is single, dependent, or married filing separately, your standard deduction amount is $3,350. If your filing status is married filing jointly, head of household, or qualifying surviving spouse, your standard deduction amount is $6,700."

  B25 "What's New" (PDF p. ~3) attributes this to the BRFA, HB 352 (2025). The same act introduced an itemized-deduction phaseout at federal AGI $200,000 ($100,000 for MFS) at 7.5% (Worksheet 14A). That phaseout does not apply to the standard deduction.

### Rates (state only)
Tax tables are mandatory below $100,000. Quote (B24 PDF p. 21; B25 PDF p. 22): "You must use the tax tables if your taxable income is less than $100,000." At $100,000 or more, use the Tax Computation Worksheet Schedules (17A) (B24 PDF p. 40; B25 PDF p. 41). The single state table is shared by all statuses: both schedules are identical below $100,000. Its rows are $50 wide (except $0–50, 50–75, 75–100). Each row's tax equals the schedule tax at the row midpoint, rounded to the nearest dollar. I checked this on every row used below; it is an observation, not a published rule.

### Local tax (not part of state tax)
Montgomery County rate is .0320 in both years (B24 PDF p. 25 Local Tax Rate Chart; B25 PDF p. 25). Local tax = Line 20 taxable net income × rate (Local Tax Worksheet 19A), "rounded to the nearest cent or whole dollar".

## Published worked example (transcribed)

**Published worked example** — B25 Instruction 13, subtraction code hh (Exemption adjustment for U.S. obligations), PDF p. ~17. The identical text is in B24 (text line ~1482):

> "Pat and Chris Jones had a federal adjusted gross income of $180,000. They also had $40,000 on interest from U.S. Savings Bonds and had a dependent son whom they claimed on the Maryland tax return. Using Instruction 10, they found the exemption amount on their Maryland return (based upon $180,000 of income) was $2,400 ($800 for three exemptions). If it were not for the $40,000 of U.S. Savings Bonds, their federal adjusted gross income would have been $140,000 and their exemption amount would have been $9,600 ($3,200 for three exemptions). Therefore, Pat and Chris Jones are entitled to claim a subtraction of $7,200 ($9,600 - $2,400) on Line hh of Form 502SU."

This example confirms the Joint-column tiers: $175k–$200k gives $800 each, and ≤ $150k gives $3,200 each. It also confirms that the reduction applies to the dependent exemption. Applies to both TY2024 and TY2025.

No full-return worked example (wages → Line 21) was found in either booklet.

## Ambiguities / open questions
1. **Table vs schedule below $100k.** The booklet makes the table mandatory below $100k. The table is the rate schedule evaluated at the row midpoint and rounded half-up to the dollar (my inference, consistent on all 12 rows checked). An implementation that applies the rate schedule directly will differ by up to about ±$1.19 from the table.
2. **Cents vs dollars at ≥ $100k.** The booklet says "State calculations are rounded to the nearest cent" but allows rounding to whole dollars. 2024 #9 is the only scenario whose value has a sub-cent fraction (6,984.625). Tests should accept 6,984.63, or allow a whole-dollar tolerance.
3. **Row boundary semantics.** The 2025 schedules print bands as "$1,001–$2,000", "$100,001–$125,000" (whole-dollar bands). The 2024 schedules print "$1,000–$2,000" and "At least $100,000". The 17A worksheet in both years says "At least $100,000 but not over $125,000 / Over $125,000…". Values are continuous at the knots, so this does not affect results.
4. **Exemption test uses federal AGI**, not MD AGI. These are identical in this domain, but an implementation must key on Line 1.
5. **QSS** requires a dependent child under MD's status label ("with dependent child"). A QSS with 0 dependents is not a valid MD status 5. Not exercised here.
6. **2025 standard deduction indexing.** The booklet gives flat $3,350 / $6,700. I did not consult HB 352 for any future inflation indexing (not relevant to TY2025).
7. **Statute not consulted.** The booklets are the sole authority; Tax-General §§10-105/10-211/10-217 were not cross-checked.
8. **Local tax.** Montgomery is flat 3.20% both years. Anne Arundel and Frederick use income-tiered local rates in both years (B25 "New local tax brackets", PDF p. ~3–4), and none of the local figures above apply to them.
9. **PDF page citations** marked "~" are approximate (located by text line, not by per-page extraction). The table-row and chart page numbers were verified by per-page extraction.
