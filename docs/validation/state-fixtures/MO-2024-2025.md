The amounts equal the federal ones, and the 2025 figures are the OBBBA-increased federal amounts.

### How MO taxable income is formed (MO-1040, both years, same line structure)

- L1Y/L1S: federal AGI, split between spouses on an MFC return (MO-I25 p.7 worksheet: wages by each spouse's W-2).
- L5: MO AGI = L1 + additions − subtractions. There are none in these scenarios, so **MO AGI = wages**.
- L6: total MO AGI (5Y + 5S). L7: income percentages, 5Y/L6 and 5S/L6, "round to the nearest percentage", summing to 100% (MO-I25 p.7).
- L9: "Tax from federal return" = Form 1040 line 22 minus line 27a (EIC; line 27 in 2024) and line 29, with Schedule 2/3 adjustments (MO-I25 p.7, MO-I24 p.7). "If you have an earned income credit, you must subtract the credit from the tax on your federal return."
- L11 = L9 + L10 (other federal tax; 0 here).
- L12: federal tax percentage, from **MO AGI (Line 6)**. The same chart appears on both forms (MO-F24/F25 p.2, MO-I25 p.8):
  $25,000 or less 35%; $25,001–$50,000 25%; $50,001–$100,000 15%; $100,001–$125,000 5%; $125,001 or more 0%.
- L13: federal income tax deduction = L11 × L12, capped at $5,000 (individual) or $10,000 (MFC).
- L14: standard deduction. L25 = L8 + L13..L24. **L26 = L6 − L25, floored at 0** (MO-I25 p.9: "If less than zero, enter '0'").
- L27Y/L27S = L26 × L7Y / L7S. L29 = L27 − L28 (0 here). **L30Y and L30S: each spouse's tax, computed separately from the chart**, each rounded to the dollar. L36 = total tax (L35Y + L35S).

So for MFC in 2024 and 2025, the deductions are taken jointly and the joint taxable income is then
apportioned by each spouse's share of MO AGI. Each share runs through the full chart, including its own 0% band.
MO-1040A 2025 (short form) instead computes a single tax on combined TI (line 9 to line 10). For a
one-earner couple (100%/0%) that equals the MO-1040 result.

### Rounding (MO-I25 p.4 "Dollars and Cents"; MO-I24 same)
Whole dollars on every line: 1–49 cents round down, 50–99 cents round up. I round L13 (federal-tax deduction) to the
nearest dollar. The L7 percentages round to whole percent. The chart tax rounds to the nearest dollar.

## Scenario assumptions (stated plainly)

- W-2 wages only; federal AGI = wages; standard deduction; under 65, not blind, no dependents; full-year MO resident.
- **MFJ = one earner**: all wages belong to "Yourself" (L7Y = 100%, L7S = 0%). I also give a 50/50 split
  as an alternative, because the per-spouse charts make the MFC result depend on the allocation. The task did not say which applies.
- **Federal tax (L9)** is taken from the official IRS Tax Table (TI < $100,000) or the Tax Computation
  Worksheet (TI ≥ $100,000). Federal TI = wages − federal standard deduction.
- **EIC**: the primary fixtures assume the taxpayer is aged 25–64 and so claims the no-child EIC where eligible.
  The EIC is subtracted in L9 and the result is floored at 0. The alternative column gives the no-EIC result (for example, age under 25).
  EIC matters to the MO tax in only one scenario (2024 Single 16,750).

## Spot check: "2025 Single W2 16750 -> MO TI 1000 -> official tax $0"

**Tax $0: confirmed** under every reading. **TI 1,000: confirmed only if the federal-tax deduction is 0.**
- Federal TI = 16,750 − 15,750 = 1,000, which gives a Tax Table tax of $101 (row 1,000–1,025).
- If the filer claims the no-child EIC (age 25–64; EIC Table row 16,750–16,800 = $178), L9 = 101 − 178, which is floored at 0. That gives L13 = 0 and **MO TI = 1,000**, so the claim holds.
- If the filer does not claim the EIC, L13 = 35% × 101 = 35.35 → 35, and **MO TI = 965**, so the claimed TI is off by 35.
- Either way TI ≤ 1,313 (the 2025 0% band), so the **MO tax = $0**.
A system that ignores the MO federal income tax deduction (L13) altogether would also report 1,000, so
the TI of 1,000 alone does not show that the deduction is modelled correctly.

## Ambiguities and uncertainty

1. **L9 negative after EIC**: the instructions say to subtract EIC from the federal tax, but they do not say to floor the result at 0. I
   floored it, since a negative "deduction" is not meaningful and L25 is a sum of deductions. This matters only where EIC > tax
   (2024 S 15,600 and 2025 S 16,750), and the MO tax is $0 in those cases either way.
2. **EIC eligibility** depends on age (25–64), which the scenario does not give. This changes the 2024 Single 16,750 fixture: **17** with EIC, **16** without.
3. **MFC spouse allocation** is not specified. The primary rows assume one earner; the 50/50 rows are an alternative. The totals differ at 60k and 200k.
4. **L13 rounding**: I rounded the product to the nearest dollar under the general whole-dollar rule. Nothing in these scenarios sits at a .5 boundary
   that would change the result, except 2024 S 16,750 (26.95 → 27), which is not near .5.
5. The 2025 MO booklet notes that the "IRS had not finalized the 2025 federal income tax instructions" when it went to print. The line
   references (1040 L22, L27a, L29) match the final 2025 IRS form. The federal figures used here come from the final IRS 2025 instructions.
6. The 2025 chart's Section B worksheet refers to "Form MO-1040A, Line 9", while its header refers to MO-1040 L29Y/29S. It is the same chart, and the difference is cosmetic.

## Adjudication notes (implementation, 2026-10-07)

- **Line 9 floor.** Line 9 = 1040 line 22 − EIC (line 27/27a) − refundable AOTC
  (line 29), floored at 0. The floor is the adjudicated reading: the
  instructions text is silent, and the evidence is DOR's own fillable PDF
  (`NNG()` "No Negative" validator on `line9`, below). Codex's independent check
  did not inspect that JavaScript, so the floor rests on this derivation alone.
- **Percentage base.** The statute's chart (RSMo §143.171) literally keys the
  percentage to "Missouri gross income on the return". DOR's line 12
  instruction explicitly uses total Missouri adjusted gross income on line 6.
  Implementation follows the form (MO AGI, graph node `mo_1040_L16_mo_agi`);
  the statutory wording is a noted discrepancy, not a resolved one.
- **Band edges.** The bands are printed in whole dollars ("$25,000 or less",
  "$25,001 to $50,000"). The graph applies each lower rate to MO AGI strictly
  above $25,000 / $50,000 / $100,000 / $125,000, which agrees with the chart at
  every whole-dollar amount.
- **Line 13 rounding.** The form enters line 13 in whole dollars; the graph
  carries the unrounded product (state whole-dollar rounding, tenforty-b72.42).
  Tests hold line 13 to the $0.50 rounding bound and MO TI and tax to the $1
  chart tolerance.
- **Line 10** (other federal taxes, and the Schedule 2 line 3 / Schedule 3
  line 9 adjustments to line 9) is not modelled; it is 0 throughout the test
  domain.
- **Wiring.** Line 22 and line 27 are imported from the federal 1040. Line 29
  is imported from Form 8863 line 9, which the federal 1040 line 29 is itself
  defined as.
- **Known gaps in the graph that keep correct expectations as strict xfails:**
  the federal EIC is not computed (tenforty-tj2.14: HoH rows 7 and 14, and the
  earlier 2024 Single $15,600/$16,750 and 2025 Single $16,750 rows); the 2025
  federal CTC is $2,000 per child, not $2,200 (tenforty-aqx.2.1: row 13). Line
  15's $1,400 HoH/QW exemption (MO-1040 Instructions 2024 and 2025, "Line 15 -
  Head of household and qualifying widow(er)") is not computed by the graph
  (tenforty-b72.46). The blind derivation applies it correctly (rows 7 and
  14), so the HoH whole-return expectations are unchanged and held as strict
  xfails citing tj2.14 and b72.46; only the line 9 floor itself is asserted
  as passing. Rows 6, 7, 13 and 14 run through the raw
  graph because the natural API does not accept dependents on the graph
  backend.
- **Cap tests.** The derivation shows the caps cannot bind on a wage-only
  return. The cap tests add a $180,000 Missouri subtraction to a $200,000-wage
  2024 return so that MO AGI is $20,000 (35%), using the federal tax already
  recorded above (Single 37,538.50; MFJ 27,682): Single 13,138.48 → capped at
  $5,000; MFJ 9,688.70, under the $10,000 combined cap. Kind: independent
  derivation (implementer arithmetic from the published rule).

### Rule summary

#### Line numbers (same in both years)
Form face (MO24 p.21–22; MO25 equivalent):
- 1Y/1S federal AGI split by spouse → 5Y/5S Missouri AGI → **6 total Missouri AGI** (5Y + 5S) → **7Y/7S income percentages**
- **9** Tax from federal return; **10** Other tax from federal return; **11** Total = 9 + 10
- **12** Federal tax percentage (from MO AGI on Line 6)
- **13 Federal income tax deduction**: "Multiply Line 11 by the percentage on Line 12. Enter this amount not to exceed $5,000 for an individual or $10,000 for combined filers."
- 14 Missouri standard deduction; 15 HoH/QW additional exemption; 25 total deductions (Lines 8 and 13–24); 26 = Line 6 − Line 25 (not below 0, MO25 Line 26 text); **27Y/27S = Line 26 × 7Y / 7S**; 29Y/29S taxable income; **30Y/30S tax from the Tax Chart, computed separately for each spouse.**

The federal tax deduction is **Line 13 in both 2024 and 2025** (verified on both form faces).

#### Line 9: which federal amount
MO24 p.7 and MO25 p.6–7, "LINE 9 - TAX FROM FEDERAL RETURN" chart, Federal Form 1040/1040-SR:
- Line 22 **minus** Line 27 (2025: "Line 27a") and Line 29;
- Schedule 2, Part 1, Line 3;
- Schedule 3, Part 2, Line 9.

The text adds: "If you have an earned income credit, you must subtract the credit from the tax on your federal return." So Line 9 = 1040 line 22 − EIC (line 27/27a) − refundable AOTC (line 29), adjusted by Sch 2 line 3 and Sch 3 line 9. Line 10 adds Sch 2 Part 1 line 3 back, with Sch 2 Part 2 lines 8, (10 in 2024), 14, 15, recapture taxes in Sch 2 line 21, and Sch 3 Part 1 line 1. For the test domain (W-2 wages only, no AMT/APTC/FTC/other taxes), **Line 9 = 1040 line 22 − EIC, and Line 10 = 0.**

1040 line 22 = line 18 (tax) − line 21 (line 19 CTC/ODC + line 20 Sch 3 nonrefundable credits). Additional child tax credit (line 28) is **not** subtracted under the DOR chart.

Statute (RSMo §143.171): the deduction is a percentage of "federal income tax liability," defined as "after reduction for all credits thereon, except the credit for payments of federal estimated tax, the credit for the overpayment of any federal tax, and the credits allowed by … 26 U.S.C. Section 31, 26 U.S.C. Section 27, and 26 U.S.C. Section 34." EIC (§32) is therefore subtracted, consistent with the form. (I took this text from a WebFetch rendering of the revisor page, not from a PDF.)

**Negative Line 9:** the instructions say nothing about it. DOR's own fillable calculating PDFs (MOFC24, MOFC25) attach validator `NNG()` to the `line9` field. Its source comment reads "//NNG = No Negative", and it does `if(event.value<0){ event.value=0; … "Sorry, negative numbers are not allowed in this field." }`. **DOR's form therefore floors Line 9 at 0.** That is the treatment used below. See Ambiguities.

#### Line 13: caps and MFJ
MO24/MO25 p.8: "If you selected any filing status other than married filing combined on the MO-1040, your federal tax deduction may not exceed $5,000. If you selected married filing combined, your federal tax cannot exceed $10,000." The deduction is computed **once, on the combined return** (Line 11 is joint federal tax and Line 12 uses combined Line 6). It enters Line 25 and then Line 26 **before** the spouse split at Line 27. The fillable PDF's calculation is `Math.round(line11 * line12 / 100)`, capped at 10000 if "married" is checked and 5000 otherwise.

**The cap cannot bind in this domain.** Reaching $5,000 would take federal tax of at least $14,286 with AGI ≤ $25k (35%), $20,000 at ≤ $50k (25%), or $33,334 at ≤ $100k (15%). With wages only and the standard deduction, none of these is possible, and none is possible for MFJ against $10,000 either. Tests of the cap would need synthetic federal-tax inputs.

**Rounding:** "For 1 cent through 49 cents, round down … For 50 cents through 99 cents, round up" (MO24/MO25 "Dollars and Cents"). Line 13 is entered in whole dollars.

#### MFJ spouse split ("combined return")
MO24 p.7 "Worksheet for Line 1": "Missouri law requires a combined return for married couples filing together … taxpayers are required to split their total federal adjusted gross income … between spouses." Each spouse's own W-2/1099 income goes in column Y or S, and jointly owned income is split by ownership. Line 7Y/7S = 5Y/6 and 5S/6, rounded to the nearest whole percent and summing to 100% (MO25 p.6 "LINE 7"). Line 26 (after the joint federal tax deduction and standard deduction) is multiplied by 7Y and 7S, and **each spouse's tax is figured separately on the Tax Rate Chart** (MO24 p.26: "A separate tax must be computed for you and your spouse"). Each spouse therefore gets their own trip through the low brackets.

**Assumption for MFJ scenarios:** one-earner couple. Spouse wages are $0, so 1S = 0, 7Y = 100%, 7S = 0%, and 29S = 0, giving 30S = $0.

### Domain and assumptions
Full-year MO resident, W-2 wages only (federal AGI = wages, no adjustments), federal standard deduction, under 65, not blind, no MO additions or subtractions (MO AGI = federal AGI), Line 8 = 0, Line 10 = 0, no itemizing, no other MO deductions. **No federal credits except** (a) the federal Child Tax Credit for qualifying children under 17 with SSNs, where a scenario has dependents, and (b) the federal EIC where eligible, because MO Line 9 subtracts it. Single scenarios take no CTC. 2025: no Schedule 1-A (tips/overtime/senior/car-loan) deductions, so taxable income = AGI − standard deduction.

### Ambiguities / open questions

1. **Negative Line 9 (EIC > line 22).** This drives rows 7 and 14. The instructions say to subtract EIC but give no floor. The only DOR evidence is the fillable calculating PDF's `NNG()` ("No Negative") validator on `line9`, which sets negatives to 0. I used the floor. If an implementation instead carries the negative through, row 7 would give 25% × −205 = −51.25 → −51, i.e. MO TI 21,751 and tax 248 + 0.048 × 12,840 = 864.32 → 864. Row 14 would give 25% × −839 = −209.75 → −210, i.e. MO TI 20,185 and tax 256 + 0.047 × 10,994 = 772.72 → 773. I believe the floor at 0 is correct: RSMo describes a "deduction," and DOR's own form forbids a negative. It is still not stated in the instructions text.
2. **ACTC (1040 line 28) and the statute.** The DOR chart subtracts only lines 27 and 29. RSMo §143.171's liability definition subtracts "all credits" except §§31, 27 and 34, which on its face would include the refundable ACTC (§24). It also would *not* subtract the foreign tax credit (§27), though 1040 line 22 is already net of it. Neither triggers in these scenarios (ACTC = 0, no FTC), but a scenario with ACTC > 0 would expose the divergence. Follow the form (lines 27 and 29 only).
3. **Line 12 when Line 11 = 0.** The fillable PDF blanks Line 12 when Line 11 is 0. The result (deduction 0) is the same.
4. **Line 12 boundaries are stated as whole dollars** ("$25,000 or less", "$25,001 to $50,000"). The fillable JS uses `b < 25001` then `b >= 25001 && b <= 50000`, which leaves a gap for non-integer AGI such as 25,000.50 (it would fall to "else", i.e. 0%). Since MO amounts are rounded to whole dollars, the question is moot in practice. Row 2/9 ($50,000 exactly → 25%) tests the upper edge of the 25% band.
5. **Federal TCW cents (row 5).** $23,138.50 vs $23,139. MO effect is 0 because of the 0% percentage.
6. **Statute wording** was taken from a WebFetch summary of the revisor page. The table and caps match the form verbatim, but the exact statutory phrase for the income measure ("Missouri adjusted gross income") was not re-verified from a PDF.
7. **2025 Line 10** drops Sch 2 Part 2 line 10 from the list (2024 had "Lines 8, 10, 14, and 15"; 2025 has "Lines 8, 14, and 15"). This is irrelevant to the domain.
8. Both MO booklets carry the note "At the time the Department finalized their tax booklets, the Internal Revenue Service had not finalized the [year] federal income tax instructions." The 2025 chart's "Line 27a" reference matches the final 2025 1040 numbering (Sch 8812 2025 references "line 27a").
