### How MO taxable income is formed (MO-1040, both years, same line structure)

- L1Y/L1S: federal AGI, split between spouses on an MFC return (MO-I25 p.7 worksheet: wages by each spouse's W-2).
- L5: MO AGI = L1 + additions − subtractions. There are none in these scenarios, so **MO AGI = wages**.
- L6: total MO AGI (5Y + 5S). L7: income percentages, 5Y/L6 and 5S/L6, "round to the nearest percentage", summing to 100% (MO-I25 p.7).
- L9: "Tax from federal return" = Form 1040 line 22 minus line 27a (EIC; line 27 in 2024) and line 29, with Schedule 2/3 adjustments (MO-I25 p.7, MO-I24 p.7). "If you have an earned income credit, you must subtract the credit from the tax on your federal return."
- L11 = L9 + L10 (other federal tax; 0 here).
- L12: federal tax percentage, from **MO AGI (Line 6)**. The same chart appears on both forms (MO-F24/F25 p.2, MO-I25 p.8):
  $25,000 or less 35%; $25,001–$50,000 25%; $50,001–$100,000 15%; $100,001–$125,000 5%; $125,001 or more 0%.
- L13: federal income tax deduction = L11 × L12, capped at $5,000 (individual) or $10,000 (MFC).
- L14: standard deduction. L25 = L8 + L13..L24. **L26 = L6 − L25, floored at 0** (MO-I25 p.9: "If less than zero, enter '0'").
- L27Y/L27S = L26 × L7Y / L7S. L29 = L27 − L28 (0 here). **L30Y and L30S: each spouse's tax, computed separately from the chart**, each rounded to the dollar. L36 = total tax (L35Y + L35S).

So for MFC in 2024 and 2025, the deductions are taken jointly and the joint taxable income is then
apportioned by each spouse's share of MO AGI. Each share runs through the full chart, including its own 0% band.
MO-1040A 2025 (short form) instead computes a single tax on combined TI (line 9 to line 10). For a
one-earner couple (100%/0%) that equals the MO-1040 result.

### Rounding (MO-I25 p.4 "Dollars and Cents"; MO-I24 same)
Whole dollars on every line: 1–49 cents round down, 50–99 cents round up. I round L13 (federal-tax deduction) to the
nearest dollar. The L7 percentages round to whole percent. The chart tax rounds to the nearest dollar.

## Scenario assumptions (stated plainly)

- W-2 wages only; federal AGI = wages; standard deduction; under 65, not blind, no dependents; full-year MO resident.
- **MFJ = one earner**: all wages belong to "Yourself" (L7Y = 100%, L7S = 0%). I also give a 50/50 split
  as an alternative, because the per-spouse charts make the MFC result depend on the allocation. The task did not say which applies.
- **Federal tax (L9)** is taken from the official IRS Tax Table (TI < $100,000) or the Tax Computation
  Worksheet (TI ≥ $100,000). Federal TI = wages − federal standard deduction.
- **EIC**: the primary fixtures assume the taxpayer is aged 25–64 and so claims the no-child EIC where eligible.
  The EIC is subtracted in L9 and the result is floored at 0. The alternative column gives the no-EIC result (for example, age under 25).
  EIC matters to the MO tax in only one scenario (2024 Single 16,750).

## Spot check: "2025 Single W2 16750 -> MO TI 1000 -> official tax $0"

**Tax $0: confirmed** under every reading. **TI 1,000: confirmed only if the federal-tax deduction is 0.**
- Federal TI = 16,750 − 15,750 = 1,000, which gives a Tax Table tax of $101 (row 1,000–1,025).
- If the filer claims the no-child EIC (age 25–64; EIC Table row 16,750–16,800 = $178), L9 = 101 − 178, which is floored at 0. That gives L13 = 0 and **MO TI = 1,000**, so the claim holds.
- If the filer does not claim the EIC, L13 = 35% × 101 = 35.35 → 35, and **MO TI = 965**, so the claimed TI is off by 35.
- Either way TI ≤ 1,313 (the 2025 0% band), so the **MO tax = $0**.
A system that ignores the MO federal income tax deduction (L13) altogether would also report 1,000, so
the TI of 1,000 alone does not show that the deduction is modelled correctly.

## Ambiguities and uncertainty

1. **L9 negative after EIC**: the instructions say to subtract EIC from the federal tax, but they do not say to floor the result at 0. I
   floored it, since a negative "deduction" is not meaningful and L25 is a sum of deductions. This matters only where EIC > tax
   (2024 S 15,600 and 2025 S 16,750), and the MO tax is $0 in those cases either way.
2. **EIC eligibility** depends on age (25–64), which the scenario does not give. This changes the 2024 Single 16,750 fixture: **17** with EIC, **16** without.
3. **MFC spouse allocation** is not specified. The primary rows assume one earner; the 50/50 rows are an alternative. The totals differ at 60k and 200k.
4. **L13 rounding**: I rounded the product to the nearest dollar under the general whole-dollar rule. Nothing in these scenarios sits at a .5 boundary
   that would change the result, except 2024 S 16,750 (26.95 → 27), which is not near .5.
5. The 2025 MO booklet notes that the "IRS had not finalized the 2025 federal income tax instructions" when it went to print. The line
   references (1040 L22, L27a, L29) match the final 2025 IRS form. The federal figures used here come from the final IRS 2025 instructions.
6. The 2025 chart's Section B worksheet refers to "Form MO-1040A, Line 9", while its header refers to MO-1040 L29Y/29S. It is the same chart, and the difference is cosmetic.
